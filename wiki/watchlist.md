# Watchlist

Overflow from the weekly brief: candidates that surfaced during a sweep, passed the wiki-fit test, but lost
the cut for that week's five captures. Also the parking lot for sources noticed outside a sweep.

Entries are grouped by the week that surfaced them. Prune anything that has sat here unpicked for eight weeks
and is no longer interesting — a watchlist that only grows is not a watchlist.

To promote an entry: run `/research` scoped to it, or capture it directly and `/ingest`.

## Week of 2026-08-25

First sweep. 26 in-scope candidates surfaced; 5 captured (see `wiki/weekly-briefs/2026-08-25.md`), 10 listed
here, 11 discarded outright (mostly camp-imbalanced blog posts and thinner NBER/arXiv items — see the brief's
Run notes for the full discard list). Three entries below were this week's *original* top-5 picks, bumped
only because Wiley's Cloudflare bot-check blocked capture — they are stronger candidates than most of what
follows and should be tried again first, ideally via institutional access or a manual PDF pull rather than
the automated tooling.

### Journals

- Gradual institutional change divergence (AJPS, Wiley) — theory paper directly bearing on Q8/Q40 and
  [[rival-models-of-institutional-change]]. **Capture blocked**: Cloudflare bot-check on onlinelibrary.wiley.com.
- Captured participatory institutions: Venezuela's Consejos Comunales (AJPS, Wiley) — institutional capture,
  Latin America. **Capture blocked**: same Wiley bot-check.
- Bureaucratic paradox of citizen agency: Shanghai co-production (Governance, Wiley) — China party-state
  gap-closer. **Capture blocked**: same Wiley bot-check.
- Presidential discretion under the Antiquities Act (Journal of Institutional Economics, Cambridge Core) —
  credible-commitment/executive-discretion case, not yet attempted.
- The state of policy capacity problems in Africa: Ghana (Review of Policy Research, Wiley) — Africa gap,
  historical institutionalism; likely hits the same Wiley bot-check, untested.
- Functional hierarchies and strategic control: Chinese SOEs in CCP statecraft (Asian Politics & Policy,
  Wiley) — China gap; likely hits the same Wiley bot-check, untested.
- Scandinavian Journal of Public Administration, Vol. 29 No. 4 — Nordic public-management special issue,
  published via Kungliga biblioteket (Sweden), open-access publisher — good odds of a clean capture.
- Is the pursuit of a competition-friendly industrial policy desirable? Evidence from Korea's post-Asian
  Financial Crisis policy shift (ScienceDirect) — Korea developmental-state gap; likely paywalled, untested.

### Data, indices and multilateral output

- Incubators and accelerators for public sector innovation (OECD Public Governance Policy Papers No. 95) —
  128-case comparative study. **Capture blocked**: oecd.org returns a hard 403 to all URL patterns tried.
- Army Modernization: Better Schedule and Cost Information Needed to Support Scaling Battlefield Network
  (GAO-26-108019) — primary-document evidence of a specific institutional-capacity failure. **Capture
  blocked**: gao.gov returns a hard 403 to all URL patterns tried.

## Week of 2026-09-08

Second sweep. 26 in-scope candidates surfaced; 5 captured (see `wiki/weekly-briefs/2026-09-08.md`), 10 listed
here. The top entry below was this week's *original* fifth pick, bumped only because Cloudflare blocked
capture — it is a stronger candidate than most of what follows and should be tried again first, ideally via a
manual browser pull rather than the automated tooling (matches the pattern from the 2026-08-25 sweep's Wiley
items above).

### Blogs, essays and commentary

- Don't drain the swamp! Populism, bureaucracy and economic performance (VoxEU/CEPR) — empirical result that
  career civil-service protections erase most of the economic drag from populist governance; state-capacity
  camp. **Capture blocked**: Cloudflare bot-check on cepr.org, confirmed on retry with `--js`.
- Europe's federated democratic learning state (VoxEU/CEPR) — proposes VAT-invoice data + AI as a public
  accountability/coordination layer; novel institutional-design mechanism. Same Cloudflare block expected,
  untested.
- All the microbes you can't have (Works in Progress) — engineered-microbe regulatory bottlenecks (TSCA
  classification trigger blocking plastic-eating, landmine-detecting, metal-extracting microbes); pairs
  directly with [[regulatory-accretion]]'s new TSCA subsection (captured this week via Marginal Revolution) —
  a second angle on the same doctrine-level mechanism, worth a follow-up capture.
- One National Market, Fifty Antitrust Vetoes (Mercatus) — federal/state overlap in antitrust enforcement, a
  federalism/institutional-design question; public-choice camp.

### Journals

- The corporation from the Middle Ages to intellectual monopoly capitalism (Ugo Pagano, Journal of
  Institutional Economics) — theory-of-the-firm history, institutional evolution of corporate form; open
  access, same symposium issue as this week's two captured JOIE pieces.

### Data, indices and multilateral output

- Tracking Progress in the Governance of Critical Risks (OECD Public Governance Policy Papers) — 34-country
  comparative overview of critical-risk governance implementation, 2017–2023. **Capture blocked**: oecd.org
  hard-403, confirmed again this sweep.
- Federal Rulemaking: Agencies' Use of Exceptions to Public Comment Requirements (GAO-26-108208) —
  administrative-procedure/institutional-design angle on how agencies bypass notice-and-comment rulemaking.
  **Capture blocked**: gao.gov hard-403, confirmed again this sweep.
- Managing Digital Identity (UK NAO) — digital-government institutional design, UK Identity Wallet rollout;
  closer to a single-programme IT review than broad institutional theory, borderline scope.

### Working papers

- Rust in Motion: The Political Environmental Trap of Vehicle Tax Exemptions (NBER w35713, Monteiro &
  Severnini) — a policy-design lock-in mechanism (tax exemption creating a political trap) distinct from this
  wiki's existing lock-in/path-dependence sources.
- Pensions and Turbulence: Automatic Adjustment, Risk, and Fairness in Long-Term Pension Design (NBER w35693,
  Diamond) — theoretical contribution on automatic-adjustment mechanisms disciplining political
  decision-making in pension institutions.
