# Institutions as a Design Space for Artificial Agents

Provisional [model]-tier page on an un-refereed arXiv position paper (Gurney & Morstatter, 2611.15017). It argues that economic institutions should be treated as a typed design space whose implementable regions depend on the capacities of their occupants (for example, cheap copying), not as a fixed catalog inherited from human experience. This is the wiki's first source on AI agents as institutional occupants.

**Evidence tier note.** (b): a position paper with a small illustrative simulation, not a test. In it, one technical act (copying a competence) is institutionalised three different ways with identical technical traces. The authors state their own scope limits; the preprint is un-refereed.

## What the paper claims

- **[model]** An institution is a typed record with nine chart coordinates (including information-with-force, discretion location, external recognition, contestation paths). It is graded three ways: well-formed, implementable, viable.
- **[model]** Transaction-cost economics varies costs only inside the human constraint set. When occupant capacities change, the set of implementable institutions changes with it; Kocherlakota's "money is memory" is the precedent for capacity determining institution.
- **[model]** Ownership is a separable bundle: the paper splits ownership, duty and payment claim, so identical technical acts can sit under different institutions with different outcomes.

## Reading for the wiki

**[wiki synthesis]** The three grades give a conservative economic-boundary test that [[what-is-an-institution]] lacks, and capacity-dependence is a candidate scope-condition axis for [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]]. Treat as a design-space argument, not evidence about how AI agents behave.
