# Corporate Ownership and Control Around the World

La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes & Shleifer trace the **ultimate control chain** of the 20 largest publicly traded firms (plus a medium-firm sample) in each of 27 wealthy economies — following shareholders of shareholders, recursively, to whichever family, state, financial institution, widely-held corporation, or miscellaneous entity sits at the top — and find the Berle & Means (1932) image of the modern corporation, dispersed ownership with control held by unaccountable professional managers, is largely a US/UK-specific phenomenon rather than a global norm. Only 36% of large sample firms are widely-held at a 20%-control threshold; family control (30%) and state control (18%) are both more common globally, and controlling shareholders typically hold voting power well in excess of their cash-flow stake, mainly via **pyramid structures**.

**Evidence tier note.** **[empirical]** — 540–691 firms across 27 countries, control chains traced from WorldScope plus annual reports, 20-F filings, proxy statements and country-specific ownership sources, mostly 1995–96 data. The sample excludes wholly private and wholly state-owned unlisted firms by construction, which the authors note biases the results *toward understating* true family and state control.

## Who a corporation actually answers to

**[empirical]** The headline reversal, in the authors' own words: **"the principal agency problem in large corporations around the world is that of restricting expropriation of minority shareholders by the controlling shareholders, rather than that of restricting empire building by professional managers unaccountable to shareholders."** Widely-held ownership correlates strongly with legal shareholder protection (the anti-director-rights index): 48% widely-held in strong-protection countries vs. 27% in weak-protection countries for large firms (t = −1.95), widening to 38% vs. 13% for medium firms (t = −2.86) — mostly tracking the common-law/civil-law legal-origin split.

## The control/cash-flow wedge

**[empirical]** Controlling shareholders achieve voting power beyond their economic stake mainly through **pyramids** (26% of firms with an ultimate owner) rather than dual-class shares (average only 18.6% of capital needed to control 20% of votes) or cross-shareholdings (rare). **[wiki synthesis]** This is a corporate-governance instance of the same mechanism [[de-jure-vs-de-facto-power-and-captured-democracy]] formalises for political elites: control disproportionate to a nominal, verifiable stake, achieved through structure rather than raw ownership.

## No internal check on the controller

**[empirical]** In 75% of firms with a 20%-controlling shareholder (71% for family controllers specifically), there is **no other shareholder holding a non-overlapping 10% control chain** — the controlling shareholder faces no rival blockholder. **[wiki synthesis]** This is a corporate analogue of a zero-effective-veto-player configuration (see [[veto-players-and-policy-stability]] and [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D44/D127): a controller can hold nominal accountability to a shareholder base while facing, in practice, no internal actor capable of contesting a decision.

## Family control collapses the owner/manager separation

**[empirical]** Family-controlling shareholders participate directly in management (CEO, Chairman, or Vice-Chairman) in 69% of cases — 75% in good-protection countries, 64% in poor-protection countries (t = −2.33) — undercutting the classic Berle-Means managerial-agency story at its root: in most of the world's largest firms outside the US/UK, the owner-manager separation the theory assumes does not hold.

## An institution-level, document-scoreable instrument

**[wiki synthesis]** The control-tracing method itself — read the top shareholders' holdings, then their shareholders', recursively, from filings and proxy statements, until an ultimate controller type is identified — is directly executable on one firm from its own documents, in the same spirit as [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] **D110**. Registered as **D128**: ultimate ownership/control-type classification (widely-held / family / state / financial institution / widely-held corporation / miscellaneous), with the control/cash-flow wedge and the presence-of-a-rival-blockholder check as two attached sub-measures.
