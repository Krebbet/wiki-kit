# Owner-board disputes and alienable reputation (academic journals)

Cumming (arXiv 2611.02302, un-refereed preprint, Nov 2026) applies the property-rights theory of the firm to academic journals: the owner holds the alienable asset (title, back catalogue, ranking-list slot) while the reputation sits with editors and boards who are not employees and can quit. Across all 57 editorial mass resignations on Retraction Watch's list (2015-2026), owners never reversed a decision after the board resigned; whether a departing board could take the journal's standing with it depended on institutions outside the board. A stated ten-year editor-tenure cap turned out to be applied at the owner's discretion. This is the wiki's first case-coded test of the exit/voice prediction in [[property-rights-theory-of-the-firm]].

**Evidence tier note.** (b) for the formal model (owner withdraws when the policy gain G < loss L = θ·m·R; five propositions, Appendix C). (a) for the coding and regressions, but descriptive and small-N: the Retraction Watch list is not a census, only 35 of 57 ownership codes are documented, and the author took part in one case (disclosed). Treat as provisional.

## Mechanism

- **[model]** Residual control rights sit with the owner; the board has only real authority, honoured at the owner's pleasure ([[formal-and-real-authority]]). Voice works only when exit is credible, and only *before* exit.
- **[model]** Two outside variables set the board's bargaining power: θ, the share of reputation a departing board can carry, and F, the cost of founding a successor. A society that adopts the successor lowers F; a society that treats the journal as its own raises θ; noncompetes raise F; ranking lists that rate *titles* rather than people make reputation stick to the owner's asset.

## Evidence

- **[empirical]** 0 of 19 replacement episodes saw the owner back down after the board resigned (3 of 38 other episodes did, all over individual papers; Fisher p = 0.54, not significant).
- **[empirical]** A successor was launched or planned in 25 of 57 episodes (44%), by ownership: publisher-owned with society affiliation 6/7, publisher-owned without 17/38, society-owned 1/8. By field: medicine and life sciences 3 of 19 versus 22 of 38 elsewhere (p = 0.004), consistent with scarce non-profit hosts. Successors increasingly land with non-commercial hosts (5 of 8 before 2023, 13 of 15 since).
- **[empirical]** Tenure test at 110 economics, finance and accounting journals: of 26 Elsevier chief editors passing ten years in post, 8 left (31%) versus 10% elsewhere (p = 0.20). A binding cap is rejected, but the evidence is thin; the one documented non-renewal was at Elsevier's lowest-revenue finance journal.
- **[empirical]** Finance Research Letters after the 2014 board replacement: volume about 3.8-fold versus 15 controls (placebo p = 0.06); estimated revenue about 2.1-fold (p = 0.13); citations per document unchanged. This is a short-run owner gain with unknown long-run effect on standing. Revenue is estimated, not observed.
- **[empirical]** Voice-before-exit success: European Law Journal, 2018, Wiley backed down after public collective protest. Such cases seldom appear on resignation lists, so the list over-samples failures of voice.
- Ongoing: *Games and Economic Behavior* (July 2026; 58 of 62 advisory editors resigned, the Game Theory Society ended its association 9 Oct 2026). Outcome unknown.

## Dimensions this adds

**[wiki synthesis]** Candidates for [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]]: (1) whether reputation attaches to the asset or to the people (θ); (2) availability of receiving institutions for exit (F); (3) basis of authority, contract versus convention ("official journal" of a society); (4) consultation rights over appointments; (5) whether announced rules (term limits) bind or are applied discretionarily, cf. [[institutional-myths-and-decoupling]] and [[organizational-lifespan-as-commitment]].

## Generalisation and limits

- The paper itself extends the structure to firms whose value rests on people who can leave: professional-service firms, newsrooms, software projects (OpenOffice→LibreOffice, Terraform→OpenTofu), the OpenAI board episode of 2023. **[model]**: that extension is argued, not tested. No government-body evidence; no public/private contrast claimed.
- Levers proposed but untested ([[reform-levers]]): board consultation rights, society contractual appointment rights, diamond open-access hosting, provisional ratings for successors founded by most of a departing board, published term and non-renewal policies.
- Scope: academic publishing, short-run horizon, descriptive counts rather than rates.
