# Korea's Economic Planning Board

Korea's Economic Planning Board (EPB, 1961–1994) fused planning authority, budget authority and foreign-capital-inducement authority in a single ministry, elevated in 1963 to Deputy Prime Minister rank, and disciplined other ministries chiefly through a **Monthly Economic Trend Report Meeting personally chaired by President Park**. This is a direct empirical case where the concentration of decision rights the wiki's veto-point and decision-rights literature would normally flag as a dangerous authority fusion is instead framed, by both the source and cited independent scholarship (Haggard), as the decisive success factor — because a single external veto point, the President, personally disciplined the concentrated body.

**Evidence tier note.** **[model]**, a historical-institutional case narrative (Korean-language histories, government archives, interviews with former officials), not original econometric analysis. Descriptive statistics (planned vs. actual GNP growth, exports) are reported, not causally modelled.

## Fusion as the mechanism, not the pathology

**[model]** The report's central causal claim, stated plainly: "When the planning ministry and budget ministry are separated within a government and cooperation is not facilitated, national economic plans could lead to paper plans." The Budget Bureau was transferred into the EPB from the Ministry of Finance at founding for exactly this reason. **[empirical, cited]** Haggard's independent assessment, quoted directly: the EPB "held stronger status and authority than the ones in Japan and Taiwan... because [it] was in a position that was superior to other ministries as it oversaw functions related to exercising authority for budget allocation and monitoring."

**India's Planning Commission is the report's own natural-experiment contrast**: planning authority *without* budget or licensing control over other ministries, and — per the report, citing Evans (1995) and Chibber (2002) — unable to coordinate investment and import licensing with its own plan, a contributing cause of its failure to industrialise as intended. **[wiki synthesis]** This is the report's implicit claim that decision-rights *decomposition* (the wiki's usual prescription, e.g. via [[decision-rights-and-authority-theory-of-the-firm]]) was the failure mode here, not the safeguard — read alongside, not as a refutation of, that literature, since the two describe different institutional problems (delegated authority within a firm vs. coordination authority across sovereign ministries).

## A third mode of "shackling"

**[model]** The report explicitly denies institutional determinism: the EPB model is "a necessary condition... but not a sufficient condition," contingent on (a) a single, stable, personally-committed veto player (the President, in office unbroken 1961–1979), (b) a meritocratic bureaucracy recruited via open civil-service examination and insulated from interest-group capture, and (c) minimal external legal constraint on the EPB itself — only six governing statutes through the 1960s–70s, framed by the report as flexibility rather than an accountability gap.

**[wiki synthesis]** This complicates [[credible-commitment]]'s arm's-length, procedurally-constrained model of investment security directly. The mechanism here is neither North's structural shackling (independent courts, divided veto power) nor Wade's low-constraint-but-disciplined-by-performance-conditions model exactly — it is **concentrated authority, personally and informally supervised by a single outside principal with direct removal power**. Accountability runs through presidential trust and personal oversight (the Monthly Economic Trend Report Meeting), not through institutionalised procedure, which is also the report's own explanation for why the model could not survive Korea's democratisation and could not be exported: the EPB was abolished in 1994, a self-correction the report attributes to super-ministry overreach once external conditions grew more complex. See [[constraint-vs-capacity-as-the-investment-mechanism]], the existing conflict this case extends with a third mode rather than resolving.
