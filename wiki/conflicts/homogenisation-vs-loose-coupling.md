# Conflict: Homogenisation vs. Loose Coupling

**Status: OPEN — not adjudicated.** A within-tradition disagreement, which is what makes it useful. Meyer & Rowan (1977) and DiMaggio & Powell (1983) are the two founding papers of sociological neo-institutionalism; DiMaggio & Powell build directly on Meyer's work and cite Meyer & Rowan throughout. They agree on the large claim — that organisational structure increasingly tracks **institutional legitimacy** rather than technical or output requirements — and from the **same antecedent condition** they predict **opposite outcomes**. Neither tests the disagreement. DiMaggio & Powell name it themselves in the text.

## The antecedent both start from

An organisation operating under **uncertainty**: an ill-defined or poorly understood technology, ambiguous goals, no reliable output test. Both traditions agree such an organisation will import externally legitimated structural elements it did not derive from its own work requirements.

## Position A — loose coupling, and therefore internal variation

**Source:** `raw/research/institutional-evolution/05-meyer-rowan-institutionalized-organizations.md` (Meyer & Rowan 1977).
**Evidence tier:** (b) conceptual essay with hand-picked illustrations. No model, no data, no systematic comparison — the weakest tier this wiki uses.

- Propositions 4–6: attempts to tightly coordinate and control activity in an institutionalised organisation produce **conflict and loss of legitimacy**, because categorical ceremonial rules collide with case-specific technical requirements and because myths arriving from a pluralistic environment contradict each other.
- So the organisation **decouples**: formal structure is detached from actual activity and from other structural elements, and the gap is maintained by a "logic of confidence and good faith" — avoidance, discretion, overlooking — plus active minimisation of inspection and evaluation.
- The prediction that matters here: the ceremonial structure is uniform across the field *and the work underneath it is not*, because nothing is forcing the work to conform. **Loosely coupled organisations are more likely to vary internally.**

## Position B — substantive homogenisation, and therefore less internal variation

**Source:** `raw/research/institutional-evolution/04-dimaggio-powell-iron-cage.md` (DiMaggio & Powell 1983).
**Evidence tier:** (b) theoretical synthesis. The authors present no original data and say so: "an empirical test of such predictions is beyond the scope of this paper."

- **Hypothesis A-3**: "The more uncertain the relationship between means and ends the greater the extent to which an organization will model itself after organizations it perceives to be successful."
- The divergence is stated in their own text, at that hypothesis: **"Here our prediction diverges somewhat from Meyer and Rowan."** Where Meyer & Rowan get loose coupling and internal variation, DiMaggio & Powell predict that organisations under uncertainty undergo **substantive internal change in tandem with ceremonial adoption** — "thus greater homogeneity and less variation and change."
- Their mechanism is mimetic isomorphism reinforced by normative isomorphism: copying a legitimate model is cheap under uncertainty, and personnel filtering through common schools, credentials and professional networks then makes managers "virtually indistinguishable", which homogenises what the organisation actually does and not only what it displays.
- They do not deny that ceremonial compliance occurs. Their coercive-isomorphism section grants it explicitly and then argues it is **not** inconsequential, because staff hired to perform ceremony become internal advocates and shift the organisation's power distribution — a mechanism by which ceremony converts into substance over time.

## Where the disagreement actually is

Not on whether structure tracks legitimacy — both say it does. Not on whether ceremonial adoption happens — both say it does. The disagreement is **what happens underneath the ceremony**:

- Position A: the ceremony insulates the work, so the work stays idiosyncratic. Uniform façades, variable interiors.
- Position B: the ceremony pulls the work with it, through imitation and through the people hired to service it. Uniform façades, converging interiors.

**These are not compatible readings of the same organisation**, and they imply opposite answers to a question this wiki cares about: does an institution that has adopted a legitimated form actually *behave* like the others that adopted it? Position A says no and predicts that reformers who change the structure will find the practice unmoved. Position B says yes, on a lag.

**A candidate scope condition, stated by neither.** Position A's illustrations sit at the extreme institutionalised end of Meyer & Rowan's own continuum — schools, hospitals, welfare bodies, defence contractors, regulated utilities — where no output test operates at all. Position B's cases include fields with at least some competitive discipline (newspapers, conglomerate subsidiaries, Fortune 500 boards). If ceremony converts into substance only where *some* output pressure exists, both could be right in their own domain. **No source says this**, and it is recorded as a hypothesis, not adopted.

## What would settle it

1. **Measure coupling directly on a field that recently adopted a legitimated form.** [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D56 gives the procedure: sample formal structural elements, ask whether each is actually invoked in the work, report the invoked fraction. Do it at adoption and again five and ten years later. Position A predicts a stable low fraction; Position B predicts it rising.
2. **Measure internal variation, not just structural variation.** Both positions predict converging *formal structure* across a field. They differ on the variance of *practice*. The test is the cross-organisation standard deviation of a practice measure, not of an org chart — and DiMaggio & Powell's own proposed method (standard-deviation-based homogeneity measures) is the right instrument pointed at the wrong variable.
3. **Test the ceremony-becomes-substance mechanism.** DiMaggio & Powell's Ritti & Goldner argument is specific and checkable: do staff hired for compliance functions accumulate influence over non-compliance decisions over time? Personnel and decision records answer it.
4. **Score the output-test exposure of the organisations in each sample.** If the candidate scope condition above holds, Position A's cases should cluster at zero output pressure and Position B's above it. This is a re-analysis of existing cases, not new fieldwork.

## Why this conflict is worth keeping open rather than dissolving

**[wiki synthesis]** Because the two positions are the same tradition's two halves, the wiki cannot use "sociological institutionalism says X" as a citation for anything about internal behaviour. It says two incompatible things. Any downstream page that wants the legitimacy mechanism must state which limb it is using and why. This also matters for [[functional-vs-rent-seeking-growth]], where both papers are cited together as the third mechanism (structure tracks legitimacy) against Niskanen's rent extraction and Garicano/Rossi-Hansberg's knowledge cost: they are a united front on *why structure grows* and not on *what the growth does to the work*.
