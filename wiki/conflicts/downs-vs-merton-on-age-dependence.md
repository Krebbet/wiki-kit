# Conflict: Downs vs. Merton on Whether Goal Displacement Is Age-Dependent

**Status: OPEN — and this is a conflict between two *models*, not between two bodies of evidence. Neither side has any data.** Read that sentence before reading anything else on this page, because the temptation this file exists to block is the reading that the wiki's long-open age question has started to move. It has not. What has changed is narrower and worth recording exactly: **an age-dependent decay mechanism at organisational level now exists in theory in this wiki**, where previously three founders of the decay literature had disowned the age reading of their own mechanisms unprompted. It still has no measurement behind it, and its author runs no test a critic could fail.

## Position A — displacement is a stage of the bureau's life-cycle (Downs)

**Source:** `raw/research/bureaucracy-and-public-choice/02-downs-theory-bureaucracy.md` — Anthony Downs, "A Theory of Bureaucracy", RAND P-3031, 1964.
**Evidence tier: (b) [model], and at the weakest end of that tier.**

Compendium hypothesis **C.11**, in Downs's own words: bureaus have "predictable life-cycles" and, once established, almost never die "within a given historical era" — "as with politicians, few die and none retire." As they grow older they:

1. learn to be more efficient;
2. develop more and more extensive rules and regulations;
3. **"shift their goals from performing their functions well to maintaining their organizational structures"**;
4. become increasingly subject to inertia;
5. expand the scope of their functions.

Item 3 is goal displacement tied explicitly to **elapsed bureau age**.

**What Position A does not have, stated at the same volume as the claim:**

- **No data anywhere in the document.** No citation to a study, no regression, no case tally. The single number in the whole paper is an unsourced aside about the share of the US workforce in bureaucracy.
- **No stated mechanism.** Downs gives the *sequence* of what happens with age and not *why* it happens, beyond "as they grow older, they learn to be more efficient…". Compendium items C.7–C.11 read as bare assertions with no visible derivation from the paper's own axioms, and the document presents every hypothesis with identical declarative confidence regardless.
- **No size variable.** Size in this document is only the definitional threshold for what counts as a bureau. There is **no Downs claim that larger bureaus behave differently from smaller ones**, and no budget variable of the kind Niskanen's account runs on.
- **No falsification condition.**

See [[rational-actor-accounts-of-bureaucratic-behaviour]].

## Position B — displacement attaches to bureaucratic form, not to duration (Merton)

**Source:** `raw/research/institutional-stagnation/01-merton-bureaucratic-personality.md` — Robert K. Merton, "Bureaucratic Structure and Personality", *Social Forces*, 1940.
**Evidence tier: (b) [model], conceptual sociology essay, no original data.**

Merton attaches goal displacement to rational-legal bureaucratic form *as such* — rule-generality, career-incentive design (seniority promotion, pensions, tenure security), esprit de corps, the impersonality norm. On his own logic **a brand-new bureau and a century-old one are equally subject to it**, provided both carry those features. He volunteers the scoping unprompted, addressing no decay thesis, since none existed for him to answer. The only size-adjacent sentence in the source concerns shrinkage as a threat to job security, not size or age as a driver.

Merton's quasi-empirical material is thin and secondhand — a 1931 newspaper clipping, a secondary citation of Bakke's Greenwich interviews (deployed with Bakke's own caveat *against* the naive "arrogant bureaucrat" reading), an impressionistic 1908 account, and bird pecking-order behaviour used as analogy. He closes by framing his claims as hypotheses awaiting test.

See [[goal-displacement-and-bureaucratic-ritualism]].

## What the disagreement actually is

| | Position A (Downs 1964) | Position B (Merton 1940) |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | Goal displacement is a predictable stage of an ageing bureau's life-cycle | Goal displacement attaches to bureaucratic form; age is irrelevant to it |
| Driver | Unstated — the sequence is asserted, not derived | Over-cultivated sentiment under career incentives; rule sanctification |
| Evidence tier | (b) model, bare assertion | (b) model, conceptual essay |
| Original data | **None** | **None** |
| Age measured? | No | No |
| Size measured? | No | No |
| Falsification condition stated? | No | No |
| Does the author engage the other? | No | No — Merton predates Downs by 24 years; Downs cites Simon, March, Buchanan and Tullock, not Merton, in this text |

**[wiki synthesis] The two do not disagree about any observation.** They classify the same phenomenon — an organisation coming to prioritise its own maintenance over its function — and attach it to different variables, neither of which either author measures. Nothing either says would be surprising to the other; the disagreement is entirely about what to write in the causal slot.

**The asymmetry worth noting.** Merton's age-independence is a *derived* consequence of where he locates the mechanism: if displacement follows from rule-generality and seniority incentives, then it is present from the day those features exist. Downs's age-dependence is not derived from anything in his own text — it is a separate assertion sitting alongside his axioms rather than following from them. **Position B is the better-argued of two unevidenced positions**, which is not the same as being supported.

## Why this is filed rather than adjudicated

**[wiki synthesis]** It would be cheap to rule for Merton on the ground that his position is derived and Downs's is not. The reason not to is that the wiki's standing finding on age — recorded across six research jobs at [[open-questions]] Q7, Q60 and Q61 — is that the age reading is **unevidenced, not refuted**, and that the critics run no age test either. Ruling here would convert a theoretical preference into a substantive finding, which is exactly the move this wiki has criticised the decay literature for making in the other direction.

**What Downs does add, and it is a real addition:** until this batch, every mechanism-level source in this wiki that could have supported an age-driven decay account **declined to** — Merton, Stigler and Selznick each stating their mechanism's age-independence without being asked. Downs is the first source in the project to assert an age-dependent decay mechanism at organisational level. That changes the shape of the dispute from "one camp asserts age and its own founders deny it" to "one camp asserts age, one denies it, and neither measures it."

## Settlement criterion

**Unchanged from [[decay-as-real-vs-decay-as-overstated]], and this conflict is a sub-case of it.** What would settle it: **a specific institution's own age, entered against a directly observed accumulation variable measured on that same institution** — rule count, coalition or constituency count, or a capture indicator meeting Carpenter & Moss's three-part test — in a panel rather than a cross-section, with controls for the vintage of the external legal regime and for principal polarisation. Downs's C.11 is unusually convenient here because it makes **five separate directional predictions** about what happens with age (efficiency up, rule count up, goal displacement up, inertia up, scope up), at least three of which are directly countable from an agency's own records. Merton predicts a flat line on all five. **Nobody has run it**, and this conflict closes the moment somebody does.
