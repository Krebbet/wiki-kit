# Place-Based Policy in Italy's Inner Areas

Un-refereed arXiv preprint (2611.31100, Caravaggio et al. 2026). A staggered difference-in-differences on satellite night lights finds that funding under Italy's National Strategy for Inner Areas (SNAI) is associated with about 5% more lit area in treated municipalities and no change in radiance per lit cell: activity spreads at the edges of settlements rather than intensifying in existing centres.

**Evidence tier note.** (a) for the association: 941 funded municipalities against 3,112 eligible inner-area controls, 2011-2022, Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator, annual VIIRS composites (the first two years reconstructed). Identification rests on no anticipation and parallel trends, defended with pre-trend plots, covariates and alternative comparison groups; it is not randomised. Night lights are a proxy for activity, not a measure of welfare, jobs or services delivered. The funding-intensity result is descriptive only. The institutional reading below is **[wiki synthesis]**; the paper does not test governance design.

## Findings

- **[empirical]** Outcomes are log lit area, total radiance and radiance per lit cell. Treatment starts the year the first SNAI-funded project begins. Lit area rises about 5%, growing over six years; radiance per lit cell does not move.
- **[empirical]** The result holds with and without twelve baseline covariates (three proxy institutional quality), against unfunded pilot-area members, the full eligible universe or an Italy-wide pool of 6,787 municipalities, and at 2 and 5 nW/cm2/sr thresholds. It weakens when the monthly composite is summarised by its minimum.
- **[empirical]** Dropping any one of eleven thematic areas leaves the effect positive; it is significant in ten of eleven (not when digital networks are dropped). Effects appear only above a minimum funding per resident, with no proportional gain at higher levels (descriptive).
- **[empirical]** Setting: about EUR 800m across 3,565 projects in 72 project areas (1,060 municipalities). Areas are pre-selected by a central technical committee, confirmed in dialogue among regions, the centre and local actors, draft an area strategy and sign a framework agreement; central funds are conditioned on measurable outcomes and reform of local services.
- **[model]** The authors argue night lights give faster, finer evaluation than lagged census and firm statistics, as a complement to conventional evaluation, and concede annual composites are slow and miss activity below the light background.

## Reading for the wiki

**[wiki synthesis]** SNAI is a case of central funding delivered through local institutions: the centre sets eligibility, conditions money on reform and monitors via a public system (OpenCoesione), while regions and municipalities supply local knowledge. The paper cannot say whether the conditionality or participatory planning drove the effect; it shows only that money reaching a threshold per head moves footprints, which fits the capacity-limited reading in [[state-capacity-and-level-of-government]]. Selection into pilot areas by collective ability to form an area is a capacity filter; the pilot-area comparison addresses it only partly. Dependence on a minimum funding scale echoes a fixed-cost view of local capacity ([[endogenous-state-capacity]]). Joint central, regional and local control of a project is a multiple-principals structure ([[incentives-under-multiple-principals]]). Treating lit area as success invites the metric-as-target risk in [[goal-displacement-and-bureaucratic-ritualism]], and the proxy needs grading under [[measurement-validity-framework]]. No existing page is contradicted.
