# Stop-and-Go Navigation on a Locked Map — drivable space, planning in {turn, short leg}, relocalise-at-every-stop, replanning, honest scoring

**Summary.** A robot that only scans at rest should navigate the way it mapped: plan on a three-way grid (free / occupied / unknown — unknown is never free) inflated by the chassis' spin clearance plus an ~8 cm error budget, execute the path as a chain of {turn in place, forward leg ≤ 0.5 m} primitives, and relocalise into the locked map at every stop from the odometry prior — the regime in which this rig already measures 0.5 cm / 0.4° median (99 % ≤ 3 cm / 2°), better than continuous AMCL, which itself only updates every 0.25 m / 11.5° of motion.
The binding constraint between stops is heading, not distance: ~1.5° per 90° turn (6° per 180° of turning) against a 5° registration gate means the prior after a big spin can leave the basin, so legs stay ≤ 0.5–1 m, turns are minimised in the planner's cost, and every stop registers with the multi-start heading sweep; a rest-zeroed gyro would remove this constraint and is the one hardware change worth making.
Score with Nav2's goal-checker semantics at a tolerance this rig can honestly meet (5 cm / 3°, tightened from Nav2's 0.25 m / 0.25 rad default), report success rate + SPL + final goal error from the relocalised end pose, and close the loop on the map with a tape spot-check against the nearest wall — never across the room, where the map is 2 % short of the tape.

> **Scan-plane note (librarian, 2026-09-24).** The "~1.1 m" scan plane on this page is the **June capture rig** (a tripod carrying the stereo camera, `data/calib/rig_geometry.json`). The **prototype rover** scans at **0.2972 m** — sd 0.5 mm, seven independent down angles at open floor, drone-prototype `eda/EDA228-actuator-verification/FINDING.md` (2026-09-24; an earlier 0.337 m was wall hits read as floor). The rover's map plane *and* its proximity guard both sit at **0.2972 m**, not 1.1 m — so § references to the 1.1 m plane seeing over a desk are inverted for this platform: it sees desk **legs** and is blind **above** ~0.3 m. The hand-drawn drivable polygon is still load-bearing, for the between-the-legs free-space case rather than the over-the-counter one. ## 0. What the existing pages already settle

[[stop-and-scan-room-mapping]] §3 settles the grid product (2 cm, three-way label carved only along station rays and driven segments, the hand-drawn drivable polygon outside which nothing is ever free) and §5 the rig hazards (1.1 m plane sees over the desk; rig zones 163–188.5° / 234.5–248.5° filtered; the guard is forward-only). What none of them answers is how to *drive* on the locked map with a robot whose only observation is a rest scan — that is this page.

## 1. Drivable space from a wall + object map

**Three-way, never free by default.** The map's grid carries free / occupied / unknown from the mapping run; the planner treats **unknown as blocked** — June's EDA060 already did this ("`inflate` treats non-FREE as obstacle") and it is the [[robust-evidence-mapping-principle]] made spatial. Note that Nav2's default is the *opposite*: `track_unknown_space` defaults to `false` ("treats unknown space as free space") and Smac 2D's `allow_unknown` defaults to `true`; a Nav2 port of this rig must flip both [src: nav2-costmap, nav2-smac2d]. Furniture flush to a wall (W6) is "wall" in the map — harmless for planning, since occupied is occupied; it only matters for the object-layering stage.

**Inflation = chassis clearance + error budget.** Nav2's inflation layer marks lethal within the *inscribed* radius and decays exponentially out to `inflation_radius` (default 0.55 m, `cost_scaling_factor` 10.0) [src: nav2-inflation]; the ROS tuning guide's rule is inflation ≥ the circumscribed radius so the planner never asks the controller to thread a gap the footprint cannot rotate in [src: ros-tuning-guide]. For this rig the number the runner already uses is `spin_clear` = **0.25 m** (chassis 0.245 m wide; circumscribed radius + margin — `clear_around` refuses a turn with any return inside it). The budget on top of it, per ≤ 0.5 m leg from a relocalised stop: relocalisation of the start pose 3 cm (99 %), primitive 1 % of 0.5 m = 0.5 cm, the *physical* crab 6.6 %/m × 0.5 m = 3.3 cm (modelled in the tick integrator, so the odometry knows it, but the body still moves sideways), yaw walk 3.5°/m × 0.5 m → ~0.8 cm lateral at leg end, guard overshoot 3 cm forward. Linear sum ≈ 7.6 cm lateral / 6.5 cm forward; RSS ≈ 4.6 cm. **Use a lethal band of 0.25 m + 0.08 m ≈ 0.33 m from every asserted wall / object cell, plus a soft-cost band to ~0.45 m** so a cost-aware planner centres in the 1.2 m band (Smac 2D `cost_travel_multiplier` 2.0 [src: nav2-smac2d]). In the office that leaves a lethal-free core of roughly 1.25 × 0.55 m inside the 1.9 × 1.2 m drivable band — one lane, not a room; goals are chosen inside that core.

**The hand-drawn polygon is standard practice, not a hack.** Nav2 implements it as the keep-out costmap filter: a PGM/PNG mask drawn in GIMP over the map, applied to both global and local costmaps [src: nav2-keepout]; robot vacuums ship the same thing as no-go zones and virtual walls ([[robot-vacuum-navigation]]). Here it is the *only* thing that stops the 1.1 m plane certifying the desk footprint free ([[lidar-floorplan-extraction]] §6.5): drivable = (polygon ∩ carved-free), and the polygon is an input recorded with the map, not derived from it.

**Resolution.** Keep the 2 cm map grid (C1 resolution 15 mm); plan at 2–5 cm. Nav2's `resolution` default is 0.1 m with 0.05 in the bringup configs [src: nav2-costmap]; June planned at 3 cm (EDA060) and 5 cm (EDA023/024). In a 1.9 × 1.2 m band a 5 cm grid is 38 × 24 cells × 8 headings ≈ 7 k states — any planner is instantaneous, so resolution is a *clearance* question: at 5 cm a cell straddling the 0.33 m lethal boundary costs 2.5 cm of budget, which the 8 cm already covers.

## 2. Planning for a robot whose motion set is {turn in place, forward ≤ 0.5 m}

**Grid A*/Dijkstra vs lattice.** NavFn (Dijkstra, or A* with `use_astar`) and Smac 2D are 8-connected cost-aware searches that assume a holonomic particle [src: nav2-navfn, nav2-smac2d]; their paths change heading at every 45° kink, which for a stop-and-go robot means a turn (and its 1.5° heading cost) per kink. Lattice planners (SBPL, Nav2's Smac State Lattice) search in (x, y, θ) over a *minimum control set* of short kinematically feasible primitives — Nav2 ships diff/omni/Ackermann sets, a generator for custom ones, and prices rotations in place with `rotation_penalty` (default 5.0, "set sufficiently high unless rotations are strictly necessary"), `non_straight_penalty` 1.05, `reverse_penalty` 2.0 with `allow_reverse_expansion` false [src: nav2-lattice, sbpl]. This rig's primitives *are* a control set: {rotate ±45°, ±90°, 180°} ∪ {forward 0.3, 0.5 m} — exactly `mapper.py`'s `CANDIDATE_TURNS × CANDIDATE_DISTS` — so a lattice A* over 5 cm × 8 headings is the natural planner and it costs nothing at this scale.

**The pragmatic route: grid A* + line-of-sight simplification → waypoints.** Theta* interleaves a line-of-sight check with A* expansion so paths are not constrained to grid edges [src: theta-star]; the same effect post hoc is "string pulling" (Ramer–Douglas–Peucker on the A* polyline, then greedy LoS shortcutting on the inflated grid). The result is a polyline of a few vertices; each vertex is a stop (turn to the next bearing), each edge is split into legs ≤ 0.5 m, and every leg end is a stop. EDA024 showed the June A* is robust to ⅓ m start noise (112/112 valid) — with 3 cm relocalisation the start pose is a non-issue.

**How many stops per metre.** Set by the leg cap, not by the planner: ≤ 0.5 m legs ⇒ ≥ 2 stops/m on straights, plus one per heading change. The M2 mapping run was 31 moves (turn+leg pairs) for 6.5 m ≈ 2.4 stations/m; Surmann's Kurt3D took 10 stations along a corridor and drove each NBV leg as one continuous forward-only trajectory with the laser in horizontal mode as a collision guard [src: surmann-2003]. Nav2's waypoint follower is the equivalent for a continuous robot (`WaitAtWaypoint`, `stop_on_failure`) [src: nav2-waypoint]. On a 3 m goal in the office expect **6–8 stops, 2–3 of them turns**, ~1 s per stop for settle + 3 revolutions + registration.

**Turning cost.** The turn is the expensive primitive: ~1.5° heading σ per 90° (EDA223) versus 2 cm/m on a leg; the runner already prices a 90° turn at 0.5 m of driving (`COST_PER_90DEG`), Surmann at c₂ = 0.01 per degree [src: surmann-2003]. Rule: plan Manhattan-ish paths with ≤ 2 turns, prefer 45° over 90° where the band allows, never plan reverse ([[move-slowly-for-autonomy]], prefer-forward), and put a goal *heading* only when the task needs it — a free-heading goal saves the last turn.

## 3. Localisation during execution

**Relocalise at every stop vs continuous AMCL.** AMCL only performs a filter update after `update_min_d` = 0.25 m or `update_min_a` = 0.2 rad (11.5°) of motion [src: nav2-amcl] — a stop-and-go robot with 0.5 m legs is *already* at AMCL's cadence, and its observation is strictly better: a rest scan has no motion distortion ([[lidar-floorplan-extraction]] §6.4: 7–17 cm skew at 28–67 °/s), the multi-start ICP against walls + objects returns a strong/ambiguous verdict and a residual, and the plan is always issued from a relocalised pose. Measured here: median 0.5 cm / 0.4°, p90 1.2 cm / 0.8°, 99 % ≤ 3 cm / 2° from a 2 cm / 3° prior (EDA223); TaskSLAM-Bench's SLAM Toolbox-in-map precision is 1.75 cm ([[ros2-nav2]]). What is lost: no map-referenced pose *during* a leg — the guard's corridor is the only protection in motion, which is why legs are short and slow (0.12 m/s, 3 cm stop).

**Bounding the between-stop dead reckoning.** The prior must stay inside the registration gate, which for this map is **10 cm / 5°** (EDA218: multi-stage ICP 0.30 → 0.10 → 0.04 m, prior ± 90° heading sweep in 30° steps, accept only a strong *and* unambiguous basin). Drift: 2 cm/m position; heading ~1.5° per 90° turn plus the modelled −3.5°/m yaw walk on legs. Prior error after one turn + one leg of length L ≈ 3 cm (last fix) + 0.02 L + L·sin(1.5°) ≈ 3 cm + 0.046 L: **3.5 cm at L = 0.5 m, 6 cm at 1 m** — so ≤ 1 m legs stay inside the 10 cm window and 0.5 m gives 3× margin. Heading is tighter: 3σ of one 90° turn is 4.5° against the 5° gate, and EDA218 found 10/253 moves (spins) off by > 10°. **Distance is not the constraint; turns are.** Hence: after any turn ≥ 90° the stop registers with the full heading sweep, and a stop that returns "ambiguous" is retried after a 0.3 m forward step (new viewpoint) before anything else happens.

**Initialisation and the symmetric room.** Nav2 offers an operator pose (`set_initial_pose` + `initial_pose.{x,y,yaw}`, or RViz "2D Pose Estimate") or global localisation (`reinitialize_global_localization` — particles spread uniformly over the map, converging only as the robot moves and observes; augmented MCL's random-particle injection is *off* by default, `recovery_alpha_slow/fast` = 0.0) [src: nav2-amcl, thrun-2005]. In a near-symmetric room global localisation stays bimodal until an asymmetric observation; this rig's cold search lands in the 180° mirror basin for 2/44 stations with a *strong* fit (rms 0.85 cm, 2.1 m / 180° off) — indistinguishable by residual. The standard answer is the vacuum's: start from a known pose (the dock) and never cold-start ([[robot-vacuum-navigation]]). For this rig: start each run at station 0 (the map origin) facing the known heading, or take an operator pose, so the prior is inside the basin; if a cold start is unavoidable, take the best two basins, step 0.3 m toward the asymmetric feature (the desk), re-register, and keep the basin that survived — the mirror hypothesis predicts a different scan after the move.

## 4. Replanning and obstacle handling

**When a guard stop triggers a replan.** A guard trip (return inside `stop_m` = 0.25 m within the ±0.20 m corridor, at 0.12 m/s, stopping in ~3 cm) is a stop like any other: settle, rest scan, relocalise, then compare the scan to the static map inside the drivable polygon. Returns that fall in map-free cells are a **new obstacle** → mark, replan from the relocalised pose. Nav2 does the same with `IsPathValid` (replan only when the path crosses an obstacle, else on a timer) [src: nav2-ispathvalid]. Do *not* replan on every stop: a plan issued from a pose within 3 cm of the previous one is the same plan.

**A temporary obstacle layer with the right semantics.** Nav2's obstacle layer *marks* returns within `obstacle_max_range` (2.5 m) and *clears* by ray-tracing to `raytrace_max_range` (3.0 m); `observation_persistence` (default 0.0 s = only the latest message) bounds how long a mark survives without re-observation, `footprint_clearing_enabled` clears cells under the robot, and the spatio-temporal voxel layer replaces persistence with a time decay [src: nav2-obstacle, ros2-nav2]. The stop-and-go translation: the layer is the **union of the last K stop scans** (K ≈ 3), each stop's rays *clear* what they pass through (so a chair that moved away is erased at the next stop that sees the cell empty), a mark older than K stops decays, and cells no recent ray touched keep the static label. Never let the temporary layer *free* a static-unknown cell — marking is allowed anywhere, clearing only where the static map is already free (Nav2's `MaxWithoutUnknownOverwrite` combination).

**Recoveries, forward-only.** Nav2's ladder is ClearEntireCostmap → Spin → Wait → BackUp, conservative to aggressive, with Spin/BackUp/DriveOnHeading simulated ahead (2.0 s) against the local costmap before they move [src: nav2-behaviors]. On this rig: **Clear** (drop the temporary layer, replan) and **Wait** (a person passing; re-scan after ~5 s) are free; **Spin** needs `clear_around` (0.25 m all round from the stop scan, excluding the rig zones) and is the right move when registration is ambiguous or the way ahead is blocked; **BackUp is never certifiable** — the rear (bearing ~185°) is inside the mast zone 163–188.5°, so the stop scan cannot clear it — a backward escape is a 180° turn + forward, which needs the spin clearance first. If a stop has neither spin clearance nor a clear forward corridor the run ends there with an honest "boxed in" and a scan in the log; that is a finding, not a fault (the M2 planner's lesson 4: check clearance at the *destination* before committing to a leg).

## 5. Goal tolerance and evaluation

**What the standards say.** Nav2's `SimpleGoalChecker` defaults to `xy_goal_tolerance` 0.25 m / `yaw_goal_tolerance` 0.25 rad, `stateful` (position is checked once, then only yaw) [src: nav2-goalchecker]; Smac 2D returns an approximate path if the goal is unreachable within `tolerance` 0.125 m (lattice: 0.25 m) [src: nav2-smac2d, nav2-lattice]. Embodied-navigation benchmarks: Anderson et al. define success as the agent *stopping* within τ of the goal, recommend τ = 2× body width (0.4 m default) and **SPL** = 1/N Σ Sᵢ·ℓᵢ/max(pᵢ, ℓᵢ) as the primary metric, with success-vs-τ sweeps, final distance to goal, infractions (contacts) and time as auxiliaries; "an SPL of 0.5 [is] a good level" in unseen complex environments [src: anderson-2018]; Habitat PointNav tightens τ to 0.2 m geodesic and requires an explicit stop action [src: habitat-2019]. RoboCup@Home scores task completion with obstacle avoidance mandatory and collisions penalised; it sets no metric goal tolerance [src: robocup-2022]. TaskSLAM-Bench measures repeatability of arrival (precision) in a pre-built map — 1.75 cm for SLAM Toolbox ([[ros2-nav2]]).

**What to claim here.** With 99 % of relocalisations ≤ 3 cm / 2° and primitives at ~1 %, a **5 cm / 3° goal tolerance is honest** (final approach: turn to bearing, leg to within 5 cm, optional final turn); report success at τ ∈ {5, 10, 25 cm} so the number is comparable to both Nav2's default and Habitat's. Per run: success, SPL with ℓᵢ = grid shortest path inside the drivable polygon, final goal error (position and yaw), guard stops, recoveries, stops per metre, wall-clock time — Anderson's list, plus the stop-and-go specifics.

**Scoring without external GT.** The relocalised end pose measures consistency with the *map*, not truth (EDA223's caveat). Two independent checks close the gap: (a) **tape spot-check** — the goal is marked on the floor by tape from the *nearest* asserted wall(s), and the rover's centre is measured to the mark at the end; agreement ≤ 5 cm is the pass. Measure from the near wall only: the map's W–E span is 2.990 m against a tape 3.048 m (2 % short, O14), so an across-room tape reference carries up to 6 cm of scale error that would be misread as navigation error. (b) **Return-to-start** — every run ends by navigating back to station 0 and registering against the origin scan (the same closure the mapping run uses); the residual is the whole-run error independent of any single relocalisation. Report distributions over ≥ 10 goals from ≥ 2 starts, block-wise by goal, not pooled ([[dont-overstate-accuracy-honest-validation]] is a standing rule).

## 6. What a cheap yaw gyro would change

[[odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]] §3 puts the arithmetic on a $3–15 MEMS gyro zeroed at every rest: per-turn heading σ 3.15° → 0.1–0.3° (Gyrodometry class, < 0.5°), the 2 m-leg landing error 11 cm → ~1 cm. Against this page's constraints: the heading prior after any spin sits at ≤ 1° instead of 4.5–10° — inside the 5° gate with 5× margin — so the multi-start heading sweep becomes a check rather than a necessity and the "ambiguous after a 180°" retry disappears; legs could stretch to 1–2 m within the 10 cm window (prior ≈ 3 cm + 2 cm/m), halving stops per metre; the −3.5°/m yaw walk becomes *observable during the leg*, enabling a heading-hold in the velocity loop; and rest scans stop being the only source of heading, which is what in-motion de-warp needs. What it does not fix: the 6.6 %/m crab is a translation the gyro cannot see (only the LiDAR at the next stop can), and the 2 % map scale (O14). Verdict unchanged from that page: buy it after the first navigation runs, not before — the protocol below works without it, just with shorter legs and more turns re-registered.

## Recommendation for this rig — the navigation protocol

1. **Build the costmap from the locked map:** 2 cm three-way grid (free / occupied / unknown; unknown blocked), occupied = asserted walls ∪ object layer ∪ flagged walls; intersect free with the hand-drawn drivable polygon recorded with the map; lethal inflation 0.33 m (spin clearance 0.25 + 0.08 budget), soft cost to 0.45 m; plan at 5 cm.
2. **Initialise from a known pose:** start at station 0 facing the map heading (or an operator-given pose); register the first rest scan with that prior and refuse to move on anything but a `strong`, unambiguous basin.
3. **Plan** with an 8-connected cost-aware A* on the inflated grid (or a lattice A* over {±45°, ±90°, 180°} × {0.3, 0.5 m} with a rotation penalty), string-pull to a few vertices, split edges into legs ≤ 0.5 m; cost = distance + 0.5 m per 90° of turning; never reverse; goal heading only if the task needs it.
4. **Execute stop by stop:** turn only after `clear_around` (0.25 m, rig zones excluded); leg only after `clear_in_direction` (leg + nose + stop + 0.05 m) and destination clearance; drive at 0.12 m/s with the guard on.
5. **At every stop:** settle ≥ 0.3 s, 3–5 revolutions, clean, register against walls + objects from the odometry prior; after a turn ≥ 90° use the full heading sweep; accept ≤ 10 cm / 5° from the prior and a strong unambiguous basin; if ambiguous, step 0.3 m forward and re-register before replanning.
6. **Maintain the temporary obstacle layer:** union of the last 3 stop scans inside the polygon; a stop's rays clear cells the static map has free; never free a static-unknown cell; decay marks older than 3 stops.
7. **Replan only when the remaining path crosses a marked cell** (or a guard trip added one); otherwise continue the existing plan from the relocalised pose.
8. **Recovery ladder, forward-only:** clear the temporary layer → wait 5 s and re-scan → spin (only with `clear_around`) → step 0.3 m forward if the corridor is clear; no BackUp (the rear is in the mast zone); boxed-in = stop the run and log it.
9. **Goal:** final leg ends within 5 cm, then the goal turn if requested; declare "arrived" from the relocalised pose at the last stop; tolerance 5 cm / 3°.
10. **Score:** per goal — success at τ = 5 / 10 / 25 cm, SPL (ℓ = grid shortest path in the polygon), final error, guard stops, recoveries, stops/m, time; per run — return to station 0 and register against the origin scan; ≥ 3 goals tape-checked from the nearest wall (≤ 5 cm pass); ≥ 10 goals from ≥ 2 starts before quoting any number.
11. **Then** add the rest-zeroed gyro ([[odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]] §3) and re-run steps 3–10 with legs up to 1 m to measure the stops/m and turn-retry savings.
