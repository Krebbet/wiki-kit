# Regionally Decentralized Authoritarianism

Xu's account of China's fundamental institution — **regionally decentralized authoritarianism (RDA)**: centralized political and personnel control at the national level combined with regionally decentralized administrative and economic authority — is the batch's most direct test of whether the wiki's existing decision-rights vocabulary travels. Xu explicitly, by his own citation, models the Chinese economy as an **M-form (multiregional) organization** against the Soviet **U-form (unitary, specialised-ministry) organization**, borrowing the terms directly from Chandler (1967) and Williamson (1975) — the same M-form/U-form distinction already in this wiki via [[organizational-capabilities-and-the-m-form]]. RDA is explicitly **not federalism**: regional autonomy is centrally granted and revocable (the 1994 fiscal recentralisation, subnational revenue share cut from ~70% to ~40%, is offered as proof), officials are appointed rather than elected, and Xu tests the arrangement directly against Montinola, Qian & Weingast's own "institutionalised durability" criterion for market-preserving federalism and finds it fails.

**Evidence tier note.** **[model]** for the RDA/M-form framework itself; substantial **[empirical]** content cited throughout from the author's own and others' econometric work — Li & Zhou (2005, 254 provincial leaders, ordered probit: one-SD-above-average growth raises promotion probability 33%), Maskin, Qian & Xu (2000, SOE panel testing the "condition A" comparability criterion), Xu, Wang & Shu (2007, governor-rotation diff-in-diff, +1pt GDP growth).

## The mechanism that substitutes for market exit and electoral voice

**[model]** Xu's condition A: regional tournament competition is *informative* — a governor's performance is judged validly against peers — specifically because regions are comparable, unspecialised units facing correlated shocks (M-form), whereas ministry-level "tournaments" under a Soviet U-form fail this test, since specialised sectoral shocks aren't comparable across ministries. **[wiki synthesis]** This gives Xu's tournament claim a stated, checkable design criterion that Li & Zhou's promotion-tournament literature (cited but not itself captured in this wiki) lacks: the tournament only works where condition A holds, which is a testable property of the organisational form, not an assumption.

**[wiki synthesis] Registered as a new mechanism, not a restatement of the M-form/U-form axis alone.** The M-form/U-form vocabulary describes *decision-rights decomposition* — this wiki already holds that via Chandler. What Xu adds is the **accountability channel that substitutes for the two mechanisms Western M-form theory assumes are available** (capital markets disciplining division managers; elections disciplining subnational officials): relative-performance-based personnel control — appointment, promotion, rotation and dismissal run entirely through the centre. Registered as [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] **D131**.

## Regional experimentation as a risk-reduction mechanism

**[model]** Because M-form regions are self-contained, a failed local experiment doesn't propagate; successful ones build consensus and diffuse nationally ("crossing the river by touching the stones") — contrasted with failed Soviet decentralisation attempts (Khrushchev's *Sovnarkhoz*, Gorbachev's agriculture reform), where U-form ministerial specialisation made isolating a local experiment structurally impossible. **[empirical]** The household responsibility system's output effect (+61% agricultural output 1978–84, 49–78% attributed to the institutional change itself by McMillan/Whalley/Zhu and Lin) and SEZ FDI/export shares (35%→93.5% and 1.5%→93.1% of national totals, 1980–2005) are the paper's headline instances.

## Not unconditional: the pathologies are structural, not incidental

**[model]** Xu is explicit that RDA's failure modes are produced by the same mechanism as its successes, not separable defects: the multitask problem (GDP-tournament incentives crowd out unmeasured goals — environment, inequality, rule of law), regional protectionism, land-conversion abuse, and a weak judiciary. His own quoted prediction — "if there was no fierce regional competition, corruption would lead to the collapse of the Chinese government and Chinese economy" — states the tournament mechanism as load-bearing for regime stability, not just growth.
