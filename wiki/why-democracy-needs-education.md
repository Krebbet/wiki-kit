# Why Democracy Needs Education

Glaeser, Ponzetto & Shleifer formalise, in a game-theoretic model, the mechanism [[schooling-and-institutional-quality]]'s empirical claim needed and never derived: schooling raises the local/social benefit of civic participation ($b(h)$), and because dictatorship needs few strongly-incentivised supporters (concentrated rents, credible punishment of free-riders) while democracy needs many weakly-incentivised ones (shared rents, weak individual sanction), a rise in $b(h)$ disproportionately strengthens the broader, weaker-incentive coalition — democracy — relative to the narrower, stronger-incentive one.

**Evidence tier note.** **[model]** for the formal apparatus (Propositions 1–3, a full game with derived comparative statics); **[empirical]** for the motivating cross-country correlation (Polity IV democracy vs. 1960 schooling, r=0.74, 91 countries) and the mechanism-selection tests (Section III). **Not independent of [[schooling-and-institutional-quality]]**: Glaeser and Shleifer are co-authors of both this paper and the 2004 paper that page is built on, sharing the identical 1960 schooling terciles and the identical directional finding. Read as the same research programme's mechanism paper, not as corroboration from outside it.

## The mechanism, and the two rivals it tests and rejects

**[model]** Education raises participation's local benefit $b(h)$ via socialisation — schools teach the skills to persuade peers and understand one's own stake in collective action, not political content specifically. **[empirical]** The paper explicitly tests and rejects two rival explanations: **political indoctrination** (schools directly teach pro-democracy values) — rejected because education raises *all* forms of social participation (church attendance, community work), not only political ones, and because heavily indoctrinated communist-bloc citizens nonetheless drove anti-communist revolutions; and **private material benefits** of political skill — rejected because education also predicts participation with no private payoff (voting, refusing to surrender in battle, even suicide bombing, per cited studies).

## A direct tension with Cantoni's causal evidence

**[wiki synthesis]** [[curriculum-and-ideology]] is a direct causal test of the indoctrination hypothesis this paper rejects — and finds it works: a targeted 2004 Chinese curriculum reform measurably shifted students toward the specific political views the state's own documents stated as the reform's goal (more trust in government, more market scepticism), with persuasion rates (~20%) larger than comparable media-persuasion estimates. **This does not refute the socialisation mechanism** — Cantoni's shifts could themselves be partly socialisation-mediated — but it directly undercuts one of this paper's two grounds for rejecting indoctrination (that education's effects are general, not targeted): Cantoni shows content-specific, state-directed shifts are achievable and measured. The two papers should be read together, not as one confirming the other.

## Quotable claims

**[model]** "Dictatorial regimes provide strong incentives for a favored few; democratic regimes with many insiders provide weak incentives for their potential supporters... Higher levels of human capital favor democratic regimes because they increase the benefits of participation for everyone." **[wiki synthesis]** The paper's own flagged puzzle, left unresolved: "why do some dictators invest in education if they know that doing so eventually dooms them?" — answered only speculatively (a dictator prefers being replaced by a democracy in an educated country to being replaced by another dictator in an uneducated one).
