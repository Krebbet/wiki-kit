# Closed-Loop Odometry → Scan Matching: the Standard Pipeline from Here

**Summary.** With wheel-velocity PI + tick pose integration in place, the *standard* remaining calibration for a differential/mecanum base is three scalars (distance scale, heading/track scale, per-side or lateral ratio) found from ~10 closed-path runs, and it is finished when the systematic return-to-start error drops below ~3× the random scatter [Borenstein96] — this rig's tick model (σ 2.2 cm / 3.15° per move, effective track already fitted) is at or past that line. Every mainstream 2D scan matcher tolerates a prior error of ±0.1–0.25 m and ±20° per matching step (Cartographer RTCSM 0.1 m/20°, SLAM Toolbox/Karto 0.5 m grid/±20°, Olson "0.5 m, 20°" typical incremental window) [Cartographer-cfg][SLAMToolbox-cfg][Olson09]; our odometry is 10–50× inside that, so more odometry calibration buys nothing for mapping. The one measurably weak channel is heading (3.15°/turn, walk σ 4.6°); the standard fix is a $3–15 MEMS gyro fused for yaw rate (≈0.1–0.3°/turn after a one-time scale calibration), not more encoder campaigns — and even that is optional once scan matching is the pose source.

## 0. Where this rig is (2026-09-17)

- Closed-loop: per-side wheel velocity PI on 990-tick Hall encoders (50 ms window); pose `(x, y, θ)` integrated from ticks with `k = 0.2848 mm/tick` (rolling circumference 0.282 m vs geometric 0.3047 → scale 0.926) and effective track 0.405 m vs geometric 0.245 m (×1.65, mecanum scrub); distance/turn goals with taper.
- Labels: Δpose per move from consecutive rest-scan ICP (RPLIDAR C1, 10 Hz, 0.1 cm per-scan wall RMS). Tick model σ 2.2 cm / 3.15° over 490 straights + 114 spins (leave-one-episode-out); straight-move heading walk σ 4.6°; sensor lever arm 5.2 cm ahead of the rotation centre; two floors (hardwood, rubber mat). No IMU (deferred).
- Campaign to date: 5 × 400-move runs in a 1 × 2.8 m lane with a station-keeping runner.

The question is what the *standard* path is from here. Short answer: one closed-path calibration check, then mapping.

## 1. Q1 — Minimal standard calibration for a diff/mecanum base

**Parameters.** The 1996 error model is still the one every tool uses: `E_s` (mean wheel-diameter scale → distance), `E_b` (effective wheelbase → rotation scale) and `E_d` (left/right diameter ratio → curved straights). Borenstein & Feng drop `E_s` from the benchmark because "this error is so easy to measure and correct" [Borenstein96 §6]. For mecanum add one more: lateral scale (strafe per tick ≠ forward per tick); Road Runner v1 measures `inPerTick` and `lateralInPerTick` separately and notes the lateral value "includes the inevitable strafing slip … expect it to be smaller … by a modest amount" *(shipping, FTC)* [RoadRunner-tuning]. So: **3 scalars for diff-drive, 4 for mecanum.** This rig has fitted `E_s` (0.926), `E_b` (1.65) and part of `E_d` (tick asymmetry → the 4.6° walk); lateral scale is unmeasured.

**Procedures (standard set).**

| Method | What it measures | Trials | Reference needed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UMBmark bidirectional square | `E_b`, `E_d` from CW/CCW return-position clusters; `E_max,syst = max(r_cg,cw, r_cg,ccw)` | 4 × 4 m square, **5 CW + 5 CCW** | tape/plumb (or any pose reference) | *(shipping since 1996)* [Borenstein96 §3.3] |
| "Drive N m / spin 360° × K" | `E_s` from straight; `E_b` from spins | a few of each | wall/laser | folk standard; formalised as `robot_calibration` `rollout`/`spin` steps → `rollout_scale`, `track_width_scale`, `imu_scale` *(shipping, ROS 1/2)* [robot_calibration] |
| Censi simultaneous calibration | `r_L, r_R, b` **and** the LiDAR extrinsic (6 params), closed-form ML, CRB-optimal | any exciting trajectory; authors use ±(1,1), ±(1,−1), ±(1,0), ±(0,1) wheel-speed pairs pieced into closed loops "so that the robot can run unattended" | scan matching only, no external sensor | *(demoed, Khepera III + URG-04LX; T-RO 2013)* [Censi13] |
| OptiOdom | generic (diff/tricycle/omni) iRprop⁻ fit on positions | square + arbitrary path | motion capture in the paper; any pose ref works | *(demoed, 4 robots)* [Sousa22] |
| Mecanum static/dynamic indices | longitudinal, lateral, angular correction indices + accel index | square + curved paths | external | *(demoed; "at least 80 %" position-error improvement)* [Tu20] |
| SLAM residual tuning (Cartographer/SLAM Toolbox) | none of the above directly — they *down-weight* bad odometry rather than calibrate it (`odometry_rotation_weight` "can be reduced, even down to zero") | — | — | *(shipping)* [Cartographer-tuning] |

**Accuracy to expect.** UMBmark on a LabMate: `E_max,syst` 317 mm → 21 mm after one compensation on a 4 × 4 m square (16 m path + 4 turns, ≈0.13 % of path); "at least one order of magnitude" reduction of systematic error was consistent across experiments, second pass 66 → 20 mm [Borenstein96 §5, Table I]. Random scatter of the 5 returns was σ ≈ 25 mm, SEM 11.2 mm — that scatter is the floor calibration cannot touch. Censi reports accuracy "very close to the attainable limit given by the Cramér–Rao bound" with 1 mm-class scan matching [Censi13]. Nobody in this literature runs hundreds of trials: **10 runs (UMBmark) or a few minutes of unattended closed loops (Censi) is the norm.**

## 2. Q2 — What a 2D scan matcher needs from the odometry prior

The prior only has to land inside the matcher's search basin *per matching step*; beyond that, odometry quality is invisible to the map.

| Matcher | Where the prior comes from | Basin / search window (defaults) | Matching step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cartographer local SLAM, Ceres only (default) | pose extrapolator (odom/IMU or constant velocity) | gradient-based; no window — needs "a good starting point" (few cm / few deg) [Cartographer-cfg] | every scan (motion filter: insert if > 0.2 m, > 1°, or > 5 s) |
| Cartographer + `use_online_correlative_scan_matching = true` | same, then brute-force CSM | `linear_search_window = 0.1 m`, `angular_search_window = 20°` [Cartographer-lua] | every scan |
| Cartographer loop closure (fast CSM) | none (branch-and-bound over the submap) | `7 m`, `30°` [Cartographer-lua] | asynchronous |
| SLAM Toolbox / Karto | odometry pose | `correlation_search_space_dimension = 0.5` (0.5 × 0.5 m grid, i.e. ±0.25 m), `coarse_search_angle_offset = 0.349 rad` (±20°), 0.01 m / 2° coarse, 0.2° fine; deviation-from-odometry penalty `distance_variance_penalty 0.5`, `angle_variance_penalty 1.0` [SLAMToolbox-cfg]; Karto SDK defaults 0.3 m, ±20°, penalties 0.3², (20°)² [Karto-Mapper] | after `minimum_travel_distance 0.5 m` or `minimum_travel_heading 0.5 rad` |
| Olson CSM (the reference algorithm) | odometry / commanded motion | "0.5 m, 20 deg" is "typical of an incremental scan matching operation (used for correcting odometry)"; the method's posterior error is "independent of the initial error" up to 3 m / 74° in the sweep [Olson09 Fig. 4, 7] | every scan |
| PL-ICP (Censi 2008, `csm` lib) | odometry `q0` | quadratic convergence but "less robust to very large initial displacement errors" than ICP; Olson: "errors of more than a few centimeters or a few degrees are unacceptable" for hill-climb/ICP priors [Censi08][Olson09] | every scan |

Cartographer's own FAQ: in 2D the correlative matcher "is computationally expensive but can often render the incorporation of odometry or IMU data unnecessary" [Cartographer-FAQ]; the shipped `revo_lds.lua` example (Neato robot-vacuum LiDAR, the same sensor class as the C1) runs with `use_odometry = false`, `use_imu_data = false`, RTCSM on at 0.1 m [Cartographer-revo].

**This rig against those windows.** At 10 Hz and typical speeds (≤0.3 m/s, ≤90°/s) the true inter-scan motion is ≤3 cm / ≤9°; the tick model's *residual* on that is ≈0.1–0.3 cm / ≈0.1–0.3° per scan (2.2 cm and 3.15° are per *move* of ~0.5 m / one turn). SLAM Toolbox's coarser cadence (0.5 m / 28.6°) sees the full per-move σ: 2.2 cm and 3° against a ±25 cm / ±20° window — a 6–10× margin, and the deviation penalty (σ_d = √0.5 ≈ 0.7 m, σ_θ = 1 rad) barely notices it. **Odometry is already "good enough" for every listed matcher; the remaining error is below the map resolution (5 cm) the matcher works at.** Further odometry calibration has zero value for the map; it retains value only for dead-reckoning across matcher failures (corridor degeneracy, the LiDAR blind sector) and for in-scan de-warping (`num_subdivisions_per_laser_scan` unwarps scans "acquired while the scanners are moving" using the extrapolator [Cartographer-ros-cfg]).

## 3. Q3 — Standard fusion, and the IMU question

- **SLAM Toolbox:** odometry → prior → correlative match → pose graph; "needs high quality odometry" is the README's framing, but the tolerances above define "high quality" as ±25 cm / ±20° per half-metre [SLAMToolbox-cfg]. No IMU input.
- **Cartographer:** odometry enters twice — the pose extrapolator (prior) and the global optimization as a relative constraint (`odometry_translation_weight = 1e5`, `odometry_rotation_weight = 1e5`, same as local-SLAM poses) [Cartographer-lua]. The tuning guide: "odometry from wheel encoders often has a high uncertainty in rotation. In this case, the rotation weight can be reduced, even down to zero" [Cartographer-tuning]; RFC 0018 was motivated by "slight rotational drift in our wheel odometry, which explains the inferior performance if the odometry constraint is used" and proposes keeping "the high-resolution translation of the wheel odometry" while discarding its rotation [Cartographer-RFC18]. That is exactly this rig's profile (translation σ 2.2 cm good, heading 3–4.6° poor).
- **Nav2 / robot_localization:** "A usual robot setup consists of at least the wheel encoders and IMU … IMUs drift over time while wheel encoders drift over distance traveled, thus they are often used together" [Nav2-odom]; fuse wheel *velocities* (not poses) and one orientation source, `two_d_mode`, and do not fuse two absolute yaws unless both covariances are honest [RL-prep]. The fused `odom` frame then feeds the SLAM node; `map→odom` corrections come from scan matching.
- **Why an IMU is added for yaw.** Heading is the dominant odometry error term because it is multiplied by lever arm: 3.15° over a 2 m leg = 11 cm. Wheel heading is corrupted non-systematically by bumps/cracks (a bump gives a permanent `Δθ ≈ ΔD/b`), while gyro drift is slow; Gyrodometry uses odometry by default and swaps in the gyro only when they disagree, getting <0.5° heading error with a gyro drifting 3–15°/min [Borenstein96b]. On a scrubbing mecanum base the "bump" mechanism is continuous — every turn scrubs by a floor-dependent amount (hence 1.65× and two floors), which is why the effective track is a fitted, not a measured, quantity.

**Should this rig add a cheap gyro now?** Arithmetic with datasheet numbers (MPU-6050 class, ~$3; ICM-42688 class ~$10–15 has 2.8 mdps/√Hz noise): scale-factor tolerance ±3 % → ±10.8° per 360° *uncalibrated*, but that is one scalar (`imu_scale`, the same spin-against-wall recipe as track width [robot_calibration]) and is stable (0.2 % nonlinearity); initial ZRO ±20°/s is removed by zeroing at each rest (the rig already rests before every move); rate noise 0.005°/s/√Hz integrates to ≈0.01° over a 4 s turn [MPU6050-DS]. Expected per-turn heading σ ≈ 0.1–0.3° vs 3.15° today, i.e. ~10×, matching the Gyrodometry result. *Case for:* it is the cheapest, most standard fix for the one weak channel, it makes stop-and-go dead reckoning between rest scans trustworthy (11 cm → ~1 cm per 2 m leg), and it de-warps in-motion scans. *Case against:* once mapping runs, scan matching supplies heading at ~0.1° and the deferral stands ([[imu-vio-integration-reality]]); Cartographer/SLAM Toolbox do not need it in 2D [Cartographer-FAQ]. **Verdict:** add it (one afternoon, no firmware architecture change: fuse yaw rate in the ESP32 pose integrator with a rest-zero), but *after* the first map exists — it is not on the critical path.

## 4. Q4 — Mecanum specifics

- **1.65× effective track is normal in kind, high in degree.** Road Runner: "Although track width is a physical quantity, different rotation behavior may be observed due to scrub and other effects … computes an empirical track width" *(shipping, FTC)* [RoadRunner-tuning]. `mecanum_drive_controller` (ros2_control) takes a single geometric `sum_of_robot_center_projection_on_X_Y_axis` (`lx + ly`) and `wheels_radius`, computes odometry from the ideal inverse kinematics, returns it "raw", and offers only fixed `pose_covariance_diagonal` / `twist_covariance_diagonal` — **no slip model, no calibration hook**; users substitute an effective `lx + ly` to calibrate rotation [ros2c-mecanum]. Nobody publishes a "normal" ratio; the 2020 calibration paper needed separate longitudinal, lateral and angular indices and still had to add an acceleration-dependent term [Tu20]. Han et al. attribute mecanum position error mainly to roller slip [Han10]. A 1.65× rotation scale on small rollers with 12 V gearmotors on hardwood/rubber is plausible; the *symptom to watch* is that this scale is floor- and speed-dependent, which a single constant cannot follow.
- **Lateral scale is the missing 4th parameter.** Strafes slip more than forward moves; measure `lateralInPerTick` from rest-scan labels of pure strafes (the Road Runner recipe) and expect a modest deficit vs forward.
- **Recommended practice (community consensus).** FTC teams overwhelmingly abandon drive encoders for unpowered dead wheels because "drive encoder localization on mecanum drive can be quite inaccurate due to lack of traction" [gm0-deadwheels]; ROS users run mecanum bases with `odometry_rotation_weight` cut and scan matching as the pose source [Cartographer-tuning][Cartographer-RFC18]. **Translation from ticks: keep and trust (σ 2.2 cm is dead-wheel class). Heading from ticks: treat as a prior only; get it from gyro and/or the scan matcher.**

## 5. Q5 — Validation

- **Metrics (standard):** ATE (RMSE of aligned absolute poses) and RPE (drift over a fixed distance/time, "corresponds to the drift of the robot's trajectory") from the TUM RGB-D benchmark [Sturm12]; compute with `evo` (`evo_ape`, `evo_rpe`) [evo]. For odometry-only reporting use **RPE per metre and per turn** (this rig: 2.2 cm/0.5 m ≈ 4 %/m random, 3.15°/turn) and **return-to-start on a closed path** (UMBmark's `E_max,syst`, plus the CW/CCW scatter as the non-systematic floor) [Borenstein96].
- **Cheapest independent check here:** drive a closed loop (e.g. 2 × 2 m square, 5 CW + 5 CCW, ~80 m total), ICP the final rest scan against the *first* rest scan — the same labeller already in use, ~mm/0.1° accurate, no tape measure. Once a map exists, scan-to-map after the loop gives the same number without needing the start scan. Cross-method agreement (tick pose vs scan-to-map) over the loop is the honest number; report block/leave-one-episode-out spread, not pooled σ.

## 6. Q6 — Stopping criteria, campaign size, learned correctors

- **Stop rule (standard):** compute `E_max,syst` and SEM = σ/√n from 5 runs each direction; "if `E_max,syst` < 3·SEM it is unlikely (here: 5 %) that the result can be improved by a second compensation" [Borenstein96 §5]. For this rig the non-systematic floor is heading-dominated: 3.15°/turn × 4 turns on a 2 m square ≈ 2 × 11 cm ≈ 20 cm return scatter, SEM ≈ 9 cm → the rule will fire on the first pass unless the systematic residual exceeds ~27 cm, which the existing fit (2.2 cm/move) already rules out. **Calibration is done; only the random heading term is left, and calibration cannot remove it** (§3).
- **Campaign size:** 10 closed-path runs (UMBmark) or a few minutes of Censi-style unattended loops is the literature norm; 2,000 labelled moves is 2 orders of magnitude beyond it. The excess data is not wasted — it is exactly the leave-one-episode-out validation set — but no further collection is warranted before mapping. If you want an unattended, standard, one-shot procedure that also yields the LiDAR extrinsic, run Censi's four canonical wheel-speed pairs as closed loops [Censi13] (see [[odometry-self-calibration-classical]] for the maths).
- **Learned residual correctors:** the evidence for them is skid-steer / rough or low-friction terrain — Jackal skid-steer with tight curves, hard braking, spins (m-ATE −60–74 %) [Navone23]; skid-steer online neural kinematics on the factor graph [Okawara24]; 4-wheel skid-steer over four friction levels, −51–94 % ATE, *simulation only* [SlipOdometryNet]. All start from kinematic baselines that are far worse than 4 %/m. On a hard indoor floor with a closed velocity loop and a scan matcher supplying the pose, the residual left (regime-dependent scrub) is a *covariance* question for the fusion filter, not a mean-correction question; the standard answer is to widen `angle_variance_penalty` / odometry rotation weight, not to train a net. **Not warranted now**; revisit only if a specific, repeatable, floor-dependent bias shows up in the scan-to-map residuals during navigation ([[learned-odometry-correction]] keeps the design if it does).

## Recommendation for this rig

1. **Close the calibration with one UMBmark-style check (½ day, 10 runs).** 2 × 2 m square, 5 CW + 5 CCW, rest-scan ICP first→last. Report `E_max,syst`, CW/CCW scatter σ, SEM. Expected: `E_max,syst` ≤ 5 cm (systematic, from the 2.2 cm/move fit), scatter σ ≈ 15–20 cm (heading-driven). If `E_max,syst` < 3·SEM → **stop calibrating**. While there, do 10 pure strafes ±0.5 m to fit lateral scale (expect 0.85–0.95 of forward). No more 400-move campaigns.
2. **Start mapping with the odometry as-is.** SLAM Toolbox defaults (0.5 m/±20° window) or Cartographer 2D with `use_online_correlative_scan_matching = true`, `linear_search_window = 0.1`, `use_imu_data = false`, `use_odometry = true`, `odometry_rotation_weight` cut to 1e2–1e3 (or 0) and translation weight kept at 1e5. Expected: map quality limited by the C1 (0.1 cm wall RMS) and loop closure, not by odometry; ATE against the locked map ≤ 5 cm.
3. **Add a MEMS gyro for yaw after the first map exists (1 day, ~$3–15).** Zero at every rest, calibrate `imu_scale` once by 5 × 360° against the LiDAR wall angle [robot_calibration], fuse yaw rate in the ESP32 pose integrator (replace tick heading between rest scans). Expected per-turn heading σ 3.15° → 0.1–0.3°; 2 m-leg landing error 11 cm → ~1 cm; in-motion scan de-warp becomes possible.
4. **Validate with the standard metrics only:** return-to-start on the closed square (before/after gyro), RPE per metre and per turn from rest-scan labels, ATE of tick-pose vs scan-to-map over a full room loop. Report leave-one-episode-out spread on two floors.
5. **Do not** build a learned corrector, a finer tick model, or a station-keeping campaign runner unless step 4 shows a repeatable floor-dependent bias > the matcher's tolerance (which is ±10 cm / ±20° per step — it will not).
