# The V-Dem Measurement Model

V-Dem's measurement model is a **Bayesian ordinal item-response-theory estimator** that turns roughly five expert coders' *disagreeing* ordinal ratings of a country-year into a point estimate and a credible interval for a latent regime characteristic. Its core move is to model rater error and rater **threshold** behaviour separately: two coders can perceive the same reality and still assign different scores because they cut the ordinal scale in different places, and correcting for that — **differential item functioning**, DIF — is what the model exists to do. It is identified using **anchoring vignettes** (unlabelled hypothetical cases with designed-in latent positions, fielded since 2016, ~80% response among returning coders), **bridge coders** who rate a second country, and **lateral coders** who rate many countries for one cross-section year. The output is country-year, exclusively and without a single exception in the paper. Two things make it worth a page in a wiki that cannot use its data. First, the **technique is level-agnostic** — nothing in multi-rater IRT with DIF correction requires the unit to be a country, and it is the directly applicable answer to what happens if this wiki ever fields several coders scoring one institution against an ordinal rubric ([[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D111). Second, the paper is an **unusually candid self-documentation of failure modes**, including approaches the team tried, found face-invalid, and rejected — a kind of disclosure most measurement projects do not make.

**Evidence tier note.** **(b) model** throughout: a formal Bayesian measurement model fit by MCMC in Stan, with no external validation against ground truth attempted or claimed, because there is no true score to check against — a limitation the authors state rather than paper over. The internal evidence is of two kinds. **Strongest**: the documented failures of alternatives the team built and discarded (the dynamic-prior experiment, the vague-prior shrinkage artefact), which are real negative results about real fitted models. **Weakest**: the face-validity plots (Netherlands, Germany, Cambodia, US time series), which are illustrative, not accuracy tests. Gelman-Rubin convergence statistics are tracked and per-variable convergence failures disclosed in the codebook when they occur. **(c) wiki synthesis** is marked inline.

## The model

**[model]** Rater *r* observing country *c* at time *t* perceives the latent trait with error:

`ỹ(c,t,r) = z(c,t) + e(c,t,r)`

and then maps that perception onto the published ordinal scale using **thresholds of their own**, which the model estimates simultaneously with the latent trait — a multi-rater ordinal probit / ordinal IRT parameterisation. Two raters who agree exactly about a country can therefore report different categories, and the model recovers the agreement instead of averaging the disagreement.

**Why naive aggregation is not an option, stated by the authors.** Reporting raw rater means ± standard deviation — the standard practice among expert-coded projects — produces misleading point estimates *and* misleading confidence measures whenever raters use scales differently. The paper cites documented cases (Marquardt & Pemstein 2018; Lindstädt, Proksch & Slapin 2020) where naive means yield substantively wrong conclusions. This is the single most transferable warning in the source: **the failure is not that the mean is noisy, it is that the mean is biased in a way the SD does not reveal.**

**Scale of the operation, as context for what the apparatus costs.** V-Dem runs 5 principal investigators, 23 project managers, 26 regional managers, 134 country coordinators and 4,200+ country experts, with typically ~5 coders per case — falling to 1–2 for pre-1900 "Historical V-Dem" and for newly onboarded lateral coders. More coders per case tightens the credible interval; fewer widens it and, under a naive prior, causes unwarranted shrinkage (below). This is a data-density effect on the *estimate*, and must not be confused with any property of the institutions being scored.

## The failure modes the authors document on themselves

**[model]** This is the most valuable content in the source, and it is all self-reported.

- **Vague-prior shrinkage artefact.** With a standard weakly-informative `z(c,t) ~ N(0,1)` prior, countries with little cross-national bridging data shrink toward the scale's centre *even when every rater agrees the country is at an extreme*. Worked case: the 2013–14 Netherlands, the only period with unanimous "fully free from political killings" agreement, scored **lower** under the vague prior than under an empirical prior. Fixed — not eliminated — by switching to an empirical prior centred on confidence-weighted raw means.
- **Dynamic / temporally-smoothed IRT produces face-invalid results, and was rejected.** Documented at length in a footnote. Smoothing priors pulled **Holocaust-era Germany upward** toward post-war scores despite universal rater agreement that those years belonged in the lowest category; the same approach produced "death spirals" in low-scoring, low-coder countries, where an early low score permanently anchored a very low prior for all *preceding* years against contrary rater-level data. V-Dem uses vague, non-dynamic priors instead and calls its own choice "pessimistic" about uncertainty by design — preferring wider intervals to false precision.
- **Lateral-coder jumps.** Coders who rate many countries for one cross-section year (~2012) apply systematically different thresholds than long-tenured single-country coders, producing spurious trend breaks. Patched by treating lateral codings as vignette-like bridge data rather than direct score inputs — an admitted, imperfect fix that double-counts some coders' information.
- **Historical-cohort bias.** Pre-1900 coding relies on 1–2 coders per country and historical coders skew systematically more lenient than contemporary ones. Corrected by an offset on the empirical prior, with residual "jumps" at the historical/contemporary boundary the correction does not remove — flagged by the authors.
- **Cross-national DIF is not fully identified where bridging is sparse.** For the 1–3 countries per variable nearly isolated from the bridging/vignette/lateral network, the authors state plainly they "cannot altogether rule out cross-national comparability problems."
- **Linearised ordinal output is non-uniquely identified and distorts distances** between scale points — flagged as an interpretive hazard of one of the three published output formats.

**[wiki synthesis]** Set this against [[governance-indicators-and-their-construction]]: WGI's candour is about *what its intervals imply*; V-Dem's is about *which of its own construction choices failed*. Both are rare, and both come from instrument-builders rather than critics. A wiki that treats "the index-makers are unreflective" as its critique would be wrong on the record.

## The coder instruction, and why it is decisive here

**[empirical]** From the QoG codebook's reproduction of V-Dem's own coder instructions, repeated across several public-sector-corruption items: coders are told to **average out any perceived discrepancy "between branches of the public sector, between the national/federal and subnational/state level, or between the core bureaucracy and employees working with public service delivery"** before answering.

**[wiki synthesis] This is the mechanism of the loss, and it should be read alongside one number.** [[world-management-survey]] establishes that **77% of the variation in a measurable dimension of institutional quality sits within country-and-industry** — between individual organisations facing the same national legal order. V-Dem's instruction tells its coders to discard exactly that stratum of variation *at the point of data generation*, before any aggregation happens. The heterogeneity is not lost in the averaging step, where a determined analyst might in principle recover it; it never enters the dataset.

Three things follow, in order. **(i)** This is not a criticism of V-Dem. The instrument is designed to characterise national regimes, and for that purpose instructing coders to give one national answer rather than an idiosyncratically-weighted one is the *correct* choice — it removes a source of rater-specific noise about which sub-unit each coder happened to be thinking of. **(ii)** It is nonetheless decisive for this wiki. The field's dominant instrument is structurally built to discard the variation that carries most of the signal at the level this wiki operates at, which means no amount of careful reuse of V-Dem scores can produce an institution-level measurement. **(iii)** The finest decomposition V-Dem offers anywhere is **branch of government** — executive bribery and embezzlement, legislature corrupt activities, public-sector corrupt exchanges and theft as separate items — and even those are constructed under the same averaging instruction. Branch is not agency. Recorded on [[open-questions]] Q90 and at D122.

## The technique, and where it would apply here

**[wiki synthesis]** Four components, all level-agnostic, all directly applicable to [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] **D111** if this wiki ever fields multiple coders scoring one institution:

1. **Anchoring vignettes** — unlabelled hypothetical cases with designed-in latent positions, scored by every coder, used to identify each coder's own thresholds. On a single institution the analogue is a set of written hypothetical institutions or hypothetical practice descriptions scored alongside the real one. This is the cheapest DIF correction available and it requires no second institution.
2. **Bridge coders** — each coder scores at least one unit in common with others, which is what makes cross-unit comparability estimable at all. Scoring one institution in isolation makes DIF unidentifiable in principle: with no overlap, "this coder is strict" and "this institution is bad" are the same observation.
3. **Collapse periods into regimes** — spans within which no rater disagreement occurs are treated as one observation rather than as many independent annual ones, to avoid overstating precision from what is really one judgment repeated. On an institution scored over years, this is the guard against manufacturing a time series out of a single standing opinion.
4. **Publish full posterior draws, not point estimates**, so downstream users can propagate measurement uncertainty into their own analyses. V-Dem supplies a tutorial and a worked example for doing so.

**The condition that makes this bite for D111.** [[world-management-survey]]'s re-rater reliability is **r = 0.51** (p = 0.001, n = 222) between independent interviewers scoring different plant managers blind. That is a substantial DIF-shaped disagreement in the wiki's most portable institution-level instrument, and WMS handles it by averaging and by entering interviewer identity as a covariate. V-Dem's apparatus is the more principled treatment of the same problem, and nobody has applied it to WMS-style data. **[wiki synthesis]** Cheapest available step: field anchoring vignettes with any multi-coder institutional scoring exercise, because it is the one component that works on a single unit.

## What it does not supply

**[empirical]** No causal claim of any kind — the paper is measurement engineering. No dimension of institutional variation in this register's sense. No scale or age variable about any institution (the only "scale" in the paper is coder count per case). Nothing on decision rights inside a scored institution — the paper describes who does the measuring, never who holds authority in what is measured. Nothing on economic production. Silent on private institutions: the scope is entirely state regime characteristics, and the psychometric technique itself is imported from educational testing, not native to this domain. The paper explicitly excludes V-Dem's directly-observable, research-assistant-coded indicators from its scope, so it speaks only to the expert-coded ordinal family.
