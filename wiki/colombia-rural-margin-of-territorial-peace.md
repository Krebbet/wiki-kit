# The Rural Margin of Territorial Peace (Colombia)

Un-refereed arXiv working paper (2611.26882, Galindo-Silva, October 2026). Colombia's 2016 peace agreement created two rural reforms: participatory development programmes (PDET, 170 municipalities) and reserved congressional seats for conflict victims elected by rural voters (CITREP, 167 municipalities, all inside PDET). Using five waves of a face-to-face civic survey (BARCAS, 1997-2023) and a triple-difference event study, the paper finds that rural engagement (coordination on shared problems, civic participation, party linkage) rose relative to urban engagement in covered municipalities, by 0.58 SD on a composite index. The two reforms overlap fully, so neither is separately identified.

**Evidence tier note.** (a) for the association: repeated cross-sections (the survey re-samples municipalities, not people), no sampling weights, observational comparison of rural-urban gaps across covered and uncovered municipalities. Identification is parallel counterfactual gaps, supported by flat pre-period coefficients (2005, 2011; joint p = 0.92) and Rambachan-Roth bounds that hold the composite to a relative-magnitude bound of 1 but not its constituents. The mechanism (assemblies and reserved seats raise the returns to organising) is the author's **[model]**, supported only by suggestive associations. The author's own caveats are heavy: minimum detectable effect about 0.55 SD, so the estimate is near the detection floor; representativeness for all constituency municipalities is not established.

## Findings

- **[empirical]** Composite public-collective engagement index: +0.582 SD (municipality-clustered p = 0.005, 95% CI 0.19 to 0.98) in 2023 against a 2017 reference. Excluding 2017 and using 2011 as reference gives 0.625 (p = 0.008). Pre-period coefficients are near zero.
- **[empirical]** Coordination around collective problems is the most robust component (0.445, positive and significant in both specifications). Party linkage is less stable; civic participation is positive but imprecise.
- **[empirical]** The gap closes partly because covered rural areas improved (+0.11 to +0.28 in the relative gap) and partly because rural engagement fell behind urban in comparison municipalities (-0.34 to -0.47). A "gain" is therefore not purely an improvement in covered places.
- **[empirical]** Robust to conflict and development covariates, exclusion of reincorporation-site municipalities, coca-exposure adjustment, reweighting and leave-one-out; the rurality restriction is less precise.
- **[empirical]** Within covered municipalities, gaps move more favourably where more PDET initiatives were adopted, and membership in organisation types named as CITREP nominators (especially ethnic organisations) rises. Consistent with the channels, not a test of them.
- **[model]** Mechanism: participatory assemblies create occasions to deliberate and coordinate with authorities; reserved seats make rural collective demands electorally consequential, raising the return to sustained organising.

## Reading for the wiki

**[wiki synthesis]** A data point on whether state presence in conflict-affected periphery can be rebuilt from the demand side. The paper's baseline is a rural population reaching authorities through brokers and regional power holders, with uneven state presence; the reforms add institutional channels (assemblies, a reserved voice in the legislature) and engagement outside the voting booth moves. This sits beside [[state-capacity-and-level-of-government]] and [[endogenous-state-capacity]], which treat capacity as a product of institutional design and bargaining rather than a fixed endowment; here, the observed outcome is citizen-side coordination, not state-side delivery, so it speaks to one half of capacity only. The finding that engagement can fall in uncovered places is a reminder that "relative to comparison" results in post-conflict settings can mask deterioration.

**[wiki synthesis]** The joint-coverage design is the main limit for institutional design lessons: the wiki cannot say whether participatory budgeting-style programmes or reserved representation does the work, or whether they are complements. The paper also finds no improvement in public goods or state responsiveness, which it does not measure. Participatory programmes elsewhere have improved local public goods without changing collective action (the paper cites Casey et al., Olken); this result runs the opposite way (engagement, not delivery) and the wiki should not treat the two as the same finding.

**[wiki synthesis]** Coverage gap: this is a Latin American, post-conflict case and partly closes the region's thin representation in the wiki. Together with [[threshold-manipulation-in-colombian-procurement]] it makes Colombia the wiki's best-covered Latin American state. No existing page is contradicted.
