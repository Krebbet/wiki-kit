# Team Production and Monitoring

Alchian & Demsetz's answer to "what is a firm" runs entirely on a metering problem. Where production is **nonseparable** — output is a joint function of several inputs with a positive cross-partial, so no individual's contribution can be read off the total — each input owner's private cost of slacking is below the cost slacking imposes on the team, and the gap is exactly the cost of detecting it. Specialising one party as a **monitor** who watches input behaviour rather than output lowers that detection cost but raises the obvious regress: who monitors the monitor? The paper's resolution is to make the monitor the **residual claimant** — give her title to earnings net of contracted payments — and to bundle that with the right to be the central contracting party, to alter any single member's contract without unwinding the team, and to sell the whole bundle. That bundle *is* the classical firm, by the authors' own definition. Everything else in the paper is a typology generated by varying who holds the residual claim and whether it is capitalizable: profit-sharing partnership, the Yugoslav socialist firm, the diffuse-ownership corporation, the mutual and the nonprofit. The paper denies that any of this is authority: the employer's power over an employee is, on their account, structurally identical to a customer's power over a grocer. It is untested theory throughout, and the authors say so.

**Evidence tier note.** **(b) a formal-and-verbal theoretical argument**, and the authors self-label it as such — "testable implications are suggested" (future tense), and the final section is called by them "a highly conjectural but possibly significant interpretation." The only formalism is the separability criterion; a footnoted suggestion that a Herfindahl index of profit shares could measure shirking incentives is never computed. Everything else is illustrative anecdote chosen to make the logic plausible: two men loading a truck, university faculty misusing office phones, the English putting-out-to-factory transition, Japanese lifetime employment, professional sports leagues. Two claims are weaker still and are flagged by the authors themselves — the voting/nonvoting share-price parity claim ("our casual evidence deserves no more than interest-initiating weight") and the Yugoslav workers'-committee claim, asserted **with no citation at all**. **(c) wiki synthesis** is marked inline.

## The separability criterion

**[model]** The paper's one clean, checkable definition. Production is a **team** technology when output `Z` is a joint function of inputs `Xi, Xj` with

> ∂²Z / ∂Xi ∂Xj > 0

— the marginal product of one input depends on the level of another, so there is no summable individual output to observe. Separable production has no such term, and market exchange meters it adequately by observing what each party delivers.

The chain from there is short and every step is derived rather than observed:

1. Nonseparability ⇒ individual marginal product cannot be cheaply metered from total output.
2. ⇒ each input owner's privately borne cost of taking more "leisure" is below the cost imposed on the team, in proportion to detection cost.
3. ⇒ team production is *more* shirking-prone than separable market exchange.
4. ⇒ specialise a **monitor** who observes input *behaviour* rather than output.
5. ⇒ regress: the monitor can shirk at monitoring.
6. ⇒ make the monitor the **residual claimant**, so her own income is what monitoring failure destroys.
7. ⇒ and give her the right to renegotiate or terminate any *individual* input's contract without dissolving the team, or the discipline has no teeth.

**Shirking** is the paper's single named failure mode, and it is defined precisely — the wedge between an individual's privately realised cost of relaxation and its true cost to the team, driven entirely by costly detection. It is an ancestor of, and narrower than, the mechanism on [[multitask-incentive-theory]]: this one is single-task and driven by nonseparability, Holmstrom & Milgrom's is multitask and driven by *differential* measurability across tasks.

## The rights bundle, and why the paper denies it is authority

**[model]** The classical firm, on this account, is a package of five rights held by one party:

| Right | What it does |
|---|---|
| Residual claim on earnings | Resolves the who-monitors-the-monitor regress |
| Right to observe input behaviour | The monitoring technology itself |
| Status as central common contracting party | Makes bilateral renegotiation possible without renegotiating with everyone |
| Right to alter any *one* member's contract or membership | Gives the discipline force without dissolving the team |
| Right to sell the whole bundle | Capitalizes expected improvements into a price (see below) |

**The ideological move, flagged.** The paper opens by asserting that firm "authority" is a "delusion" — the monitor directs day-to-day task assignment, but A&D insist this is continuous mutually terminable contract renegotiation, identical in kind to a customer redirecting a grocer. **[wiki synthesis]** This is doing analytical *and* ideological work: it naturalises the employment relation as pure voluntary exchange and denies any qualitative asymmetry. The paper's own text supplies the asymmetry it denies — individual members may always quit, but only the monitor may unilaterally expel others. Note also that this is the position [[decision-rights-and-authority-theory-of-the-firm]] classifies as the **contract branch**, against a control branch that holds integration really does confer fiat.

## The typology: who holds the residual claim, and can it be sold

**[model]** Varying two axes — how concentrated the residual claim is, and whether it is capitalizable into a salable instrument — generates the paper's whole set of organisational forms and its predicted monitoring intensity for each.

| Form | Residual claim | Predicted consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Classical firm | Concentrated in the monitor, salable | The benchmark: monitoring incentive intact |
| Profit-sharing partnership | Shared equally across members | Incentive-compatible only in **small** teams; predicts partnerships stay small |
| Corporation (diffuse ownership) | Dispersed across stockholders, freely transferable | Individual stockholders have no incentive to monitor and free-ride on each other's vigilance; discipline runs instead through **periodic congealing of dispersed votes into decisive blocs** — proxy fights and takeovers |
| Mutual / nonprofit | Not capitalizable; shares often non-transferable | **More shirking** than an equivalent for-profit (asserted, never tested) |
| Yugoslav socialist firm | Profit-sharing imposed politically on the whole workforce | Inflates the manager's own shirking incentive; the compensating device is an employee committee empowered to *recommend* termination of the manager's contract to the state |

**The corporate-governance claim, stated precisely.** A&D explicitly deny that ordinary diffusion of voting power disciplines management. What disciplines it is that expected management improvements **capitalize into share price**, which funds a challenger's effort — so the operative variable is the *frequency of concentration events*, not the existence of votes. Where the residual is not capitalizable (mutual, nonprofit, and by extension any body with no salable residual), that mechanism is simply absent.

**The unfilled bridge, and it is this wiki's core question.** The paper generalises to *state-directed* enterprises (Yugoslavia) and to *private nonprofits* (colleges, churches, country clubs, mutual insurers, co-ops), but is **silent on government bureaus** — no ministry, agency or regulator appears anywhere in it. **[wiki synthesis]** A bureau likewise has no salable residual claim, so the non-capitalizable-residual prediction would extend to it if the mechanism holds. A&D do not say so, and the extension is not licensed by this source. Registered as [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D113.

## Two scale claims, both asserted

**[model]** No age or founding-date claim appears anywhere in the paper. Two size claims do, and they are different mechanisms:

1. **Team size against shirking under equal profit-sharing** (fn. 10): "Incentives to shirk are positively related to the optimal size of the team under an equal profit-sharing scheme," because each member internalises a shrinking share of the loss their own shirking causes. This is the derivation behind "partnerships stay small." The Herfindahl index of unequal profit shares is gestured at as a proxy and never computed.
2. **Firm size against monitor attenuation** (fn. 18): "the larger the set of inputs about which knowledge of performance is being collated within a firm the greater are the present costs of the collation activity... the larger the firm the greater the attenuation of monitor control. To counter this force, the firm will be divisionalized." Asserted, not derived, not tested.

**[wiki synthesis]** The second is a **third mechanism** for the same divisionalization outcome that [[knowledge-hierarchies-and-the-cost-of-scale]] and [[hierarchy-and-near-decomposability]] already supply. Garicano & Rossi-Hansberg's driver is the cost of *applying knowledge to problems*; Simon's is *assembly time* across levels; A&D's is the cost of *collating information about input quality*. Three distinct drivers, one prediction, none tested against the others — the same shape as the standing dispute at [[parkinson-vs-knowledge-hierarchy-growth]].

## Terminology: "residual claimant" is not "residual control rights"

**[wiki synthesis]** Two of the wiki's firm pages use "residual" for structurally different objects, and conflating them produces a conflict that does not exist:

- **Residual claimant** (this page, Alchian & Demsetz): title to *earnings* net of contracted payments, used to make a monitor self-policing. It is a claim on a cash flow.
- **Residual control rights** ([[property-rights-theory-of-the-firm]], Grossman & Hart): the right to decide the uses of an *asset* that the contract does not specify, which sets a party's ex post bargaining threat point and hence their ex ante investment. It is a decision right over a thing.

The two sources are not making incompatible claims about one object; they are naming two objects with one word. This is deliberately **not** filed as a conflict. Gibbons's payoff-rights-versus-decision-rights split at [[decision-rights-and-authority-theory-of-the-firm]] is the general form of the same distinction.

## What is not established

- **Nothing here is tested.** Not one proposition in the paper is measured by the authors, and they say so.
- **Nothing here is about age.** The word does not appear as a variable.
- **The nonprofit/mutual shirking prediction has no data behind it at all** and fits neatly with a prior favouring private, tradable, residual-claim ownership as the efficient benchmark. Attribute it; do not adopt it.
- **The separability criterion is checkable but never checked.** No production function anywhere in the paper is classified against ∂²Z/∂Xi∂Xj. Registered as [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D114 with that limitation stated in the row.
- **No public bureau appears.** See above.
- **Boundary-of-the-firm content is out of scope here.** A&D argue against Coase/Williamson that transaction-cost reduction alone cannot explain the putting-out-to-factory transition, since contracting costs *fell* with centralised power sources even as firms expanded. That is boundary material and belongs with [[governance-structures]].
