# Trends in the industry

- **RLVR's mechanism debate converges on sparse causal structure.** Thought Anchors (DeepMind, 131 HF upvotes) shows that planning and backtracking sentences — roughly 20% of a CoT — disproportionately determine final output via counterfactual causal analysis. Three independent methods now point to the same structure: entropy detection ([[high-entropy-tokens-rlvr]]), discriminator contrast ([[delta-token-credit]]), and behavioral counterfactuals (Thought Anchors). The remaining ~80% of the trace is either inert or actively harmful. This convergence is becoming the field's working model for where RL gradient should land.

- **GRPO's credit-assignment gap is being closed analytically.** VIMPO derives a value function from KL-regularized RL optimality conditions — no learned critic, pure policy-implied recurrence anchored at end-of-sequence. STARE adds entropy-critical token reweighting with a closed-loop target-entropy gate to prevent GRPO collapse. Both are drop-in replacements with code released. The [[token-gradient-cancellation]] / [[delta-token-credit]] cluster now has 5+ parallel approaches; competition has shifted from "does token-level credit matter" to "which formulation is theoretically grounded."

- **Language world models open a new path for agentic RL.** Qwen-AgentWorld (Alibaba/Qwen, 132 HF upvotes) trains MoE models to simulate 7 agentic domains via long-CoT and shows synthetic RL on these simulations surpasses real-environment training. The decoupled-simulator paradigm — train a world model, generate synthetic rollouts, run agent RL on those — offers scale, control, and reproducibility that real-environment RL cannot. Models (35B-A3B / 397B-A17B) and code are released.

- **MoE expert structure, not just sparsity, is the new design frontier.** DAG-MoE (ICML 2026) replaces weighted-sum expert aggregation with a learned DAG that enables multi-step reasoning within a single MoE layer. MoE-Surpasses-Dense (StepFun/Fudan) shows MoE strictly beats dense at identical total-parameter + compute + data budget, with a stable "optimal activation rate" across sizes. The community has moved past "use MoE or not" to "how should experts interact."

- **Hierarchical recurrent architectures form a maturing cluster.** HRM's foundational paper (arXiv:2506.21734, 54 HF upvotes) anchors an increasingly coherent lineage: original HRM (symbolic tasks, 27M params) → [[hrm-text]] (language, 1B) → [[gram-recursive-reasoning]] (stochastic latent extension). ParaRNN (Apple, ICLR 2026 Oral) adds a 665× training speedup for nonlinear RNNs, removing the last practical barrier to RNN-scale pretraining. Structured temporal hierarchy is consolidating as the shared winning intuition across these approaches.

- **Scaling law orthodoxy under organized challenge.** Lilian Weng's "Scaling Laws, Carefully" post synthesizes why Kaplan-vs-Chinchilla disagreements trace to fitting choices, not empirical disagreement. MoE-Surpasses-Dense breaks the standard compute-optimal dense-model baseline. [[hrm-text]] challenges Chinchilla's token-budget orthodoxy by pretraining from scratch at 40B tokens to rival 2–7B dense models. The "just scale" intuition is giving way to "scale what, at what ratio, with what architecture."
