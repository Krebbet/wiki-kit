# Trends in the industry

- **Few-shot RL floor keeps dropping — and the floor may be zero.** RLSC (arXiv:2506.06395) achieves +13.4 pp AIME24 / +21.2 pp MATH500 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B with only 16 samples per question and no external verifier — using the model's own output confidence as reward. This is the lowest-label-count RL post-training result in the wiki and directly bears on the thesis question: how much data does single-sample/few-shot RL actually need? The correctness-agnostic reward is a fundamental misspecification risk (wrong-mode amplification), but the result is real. Related: [[research/single-sample-rl-finetuning/rlsc-self-confidence]].

- **Error-trajectory construction emerging as the self-distillation primitive.** TAPO (arXiv:2606.18844) from Alibaba/Qwen/Tsinghua makes the self-distillation training signal explicit: instead of implicit KL-alignment toward a privileged prefix (OPSD), it constructs micro-reflective trajectories anchored in the model's own erroneous prefixes, only diverging at the first error point. The core claim — that the bottleneck is explicit error diagnosis, not the privileged target — is backed by the DSR result (+13.5–22.3 pp first-pass improvement over GRPO, not just reflective correction). This closes the loop on what [[research/teacher-student-rl/opsd-compresses-rlvr]] identified: OPSD cannot create new reasoning states; TAPO's mechanism does. Directly relevant to any self-play-inclusive proposal ([[research/synthesis/proposer-reward-shapes]]).

- **Circuit-level mechanistic account of RL's forgetting advantage is now available.** The circuit-level gap between RL and SFT (arXiv:2605.28860) is 13.5 pp in head retention (72.5% vs 59.0%) and the KL-to-base metric dissociates from retention — RL can have *larger* KL while retaining *more* capability. This closes the mechanistic gap that [[research/catastrophic-forgetting/rls-razor]] left open ("RL forgets less" behaviorally; now "because RL is a distributed adaptor, SFT is a compressor" mechanistically). The SFT→RL ordering recommendation in [[research/synthesis/fine-tuning-best-practices]] now has a circuit-level substrate.

- **Theory is converging on GRPO's failure modes as a structured taxonomy.** The first-principles framework (arXiv:2606.16733) classifies every post-GRPO paper as a failure-motivated response along two axes: trajectory-side (how samples enter the update) and reward-side (how signal weights the update). Compound failures (clip–variance coupling, granularity coupling) require both-axis fixes simultaneously. This organizes the proliferation visible in the watchlist's "Post-GRPO RL optimizers" section — and the missing joint theory (when do fixes need to co-vary?) is now formally named as the central open problem.
