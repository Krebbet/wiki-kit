# Trends in the industry

- **Entry-deregulation evidence keeps arriving via country-specific natural experiments, and it keeps cutting against precautionary capital/licensing barriers.** This week's Norway capital-requirements paper (NBER w35780) joins last week's and prior weeks' regulatory-natural-experiment finds in showing a barrier justified as a quality screen restricting entry without functioning as one — a pattern this wiki now has enough instances of to treat as a standing prior, not a one-off result. *(synthesis)*
- **Welfare-eligibility "ordeal" theory is being revised from within, not just attacked from outside.** This week's SNAP work-requirements paper doesn't reject ordeal-mechanism theory (Nichols & Zeckhauser 1982) — it extends it, replacing "costly but achievable" with "infeasible for a subset," which is a sharper and more damaging critique than the usual administrative-burden framing because it explains *why* the classical welfare-neutral-screening result fails rather than just asserting that it does.
