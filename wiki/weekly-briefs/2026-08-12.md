# Trends in the industry
- **Enterprise agent ROI skepticism goes quantitative.** KPMG's Q2 2026 Global AI Pulse survey (2,145 executives) found 49% of enterprises scaled back agent deployments over unpredictable, usage-based cost, and only ~7% report established ROI — the first *survey-quantified* buyer-side pullback, arriving the same week security researchers compounded the trust deficit from the other direction. *(synthesis)*
- **AI coding-agent tooling is now a proven RCE/supply-chain attack surface, not a theoretical one.** Black Hat researchers disclosed working CI-harness exploits in Claude Code (CVE-2026-54316), Gemini CLI (CVSS 10.0, CVE-2026-12537), and OpenAI Codex in the same week — and the three vendors responded with materially different postures (Anthropic and Google issued CVEs and patches; OpenAI issued neither, calling it a workflow-configuration issue).
- **Frontier labs are locking in price stability rather than raising.** Anthropic cancelled Sonnet 5's planned Sept 1 price hike and made its $2/$10 introductory rate permanent, one week after OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 pricing and removed free-tier chat limits — both read as demand-share defense, not confidence in unit economics, especially against the KPMG cost-pullback data above.
- **A "personal/open AI stack" thesis is now funded at scale, distinct from renting lab intelligence.** River AI's $1.1B round (ex-xAI's Igor Babuschkin, 2 months old) sells training your own open model instead of prompting one you don't own; xAI's Grok Bot sells persistent agents with their own dedicated compute. Both bet on AI infrastructure the buyer controls rather than API calls to a shared frontier model. *(synthesis)*
- **Enterprise agent deployment-friction tooling is crystallizing as its own sub-category.** June (Benioff-backed, "AI solves the AI deployment problem"), Skan AI ($63M, "context graph of work"), and AWS AgentCore's new governance primitives (Dogwood policy language, session-aware rate limiting) all target the exact gap KPMG's survey just quantified — agents failing on deployment/governance friction, not raw capability. *(synthesis)*
- **Compute-financing structures keep escalating.** A reported Nvidia-led $500B Wall Street AI-infrastructure financing platform (Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Goldman, KKR), plus talk of Nvidia backstopping up to $250B of OpenAI's compute leasing, extends last week's Anthropic-Volta/Blackstone financing thread into a broader, multi-lab pattern — with recurring "circular financing" concerns attached.
