# Trends in the industry
- **Non-AI-native incumbents keep buying the AI-infrastructure layer outright — now reaching the model-hub tier.** Hugging Face's reported ~$13B acquisition talks (2026-08-24) follow Stripe/OpenRouter (model-routing gateway, $7B+) and Dynatrace/Arize (agent observability, $915M) within about ten days — three distinct infrastructure layers, the same acquisition logic: buy the neutral layer rather than build a competitor to it. *(synthesis)*
- **Enterprise incumbents are starting to cede their own UI to model vendors, not just build on top of them.** Salesforce's Claudeforce partnership — pitched by Salesforce itself as "you'll never need [the Salesforce] app again" — goes a step further than April's Headless 360 (which kept Salesforce as the substrate agents build on). This complicates rather than resolves the "death of the app layer" conflict: a narrow-deep, proprietary-data incumbent previously read as *safe* from displacement is voluntarily making its own interface optional.
- **Independent analyst instruments are converging on enterprise-AI-ROI pessimism, not just adoption friction.** McKinsey's 2026 State of AI survey (6% of enterprises qualify as "AI high performers," ~90% of use cases stuck in pilot) lands in the same neighborhood as Gartner's 88%-pilots-fail figure and KPMG's 49%-scaled-back-deployments survey from the prior two weeks — three separate large-sample survey instruments now agreeing directionally, even without confirming the specific "95% fail" MIT claim this wiki has tracked since April.
- **Protocol-layer standardization is consolidating fast, ahead of the security question it doesn't answer.** Google folded its A2A protocol into the same neutral Agentic AI Foundation that already hosts Anthropic's MCP — 250+ members spanning direct competitors (AWS, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI). Interoperability is converging while the harder question (agent-protocol security — MCP RCE, Black Hat CI-harness CVEs) remains open under the same governance umbrella, not resolved by it.
- **Anthropic's pre-IPO structure is founder-control-first, and it's a real investor-risk signal, not just a financing footnote.** Supervoting shares for a CEO with ~2% economic equity, plus a Long-Term Benefit Trust that just shrank to three members, gives public shareholders minimal governance leverage post-IPO — a template worth watching for whether other AI labs follow it into their own public listings.
- **The next capital-intensive AI race may be compute-efficiency software, not just raw chip supply.** Anthropic's reported ~$6B talks to acquire Decart (chip-utilization efficiency, not a model or product) reframes the compute-scarcity story: buying software that makes existing GPU/TPU capacity go further is now expensive enough to rival mid-tier model acquisitions.
