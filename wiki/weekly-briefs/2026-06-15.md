# Trends in the industry

- **Adversarial code auditing is becoming a reproducible counter-tool.** EFF's Threat Lab did not receive a leak — they decompiled the Meta AI app binary and found dormant facial-recognition ("NameTag") code shipped to 50M+ Ray-Ban devices without any public disclosure. The technique (static analysis of consumer-facing app binaries to detect pre-activation surveillance capabilities) is reproducible by any org with a decompiler and domain knowledge. The causal chain — decompile → disclose → public pressure → rollback within 48 hours — is now a documented case study. This is a structural addition to the wiki's counter-tool inventory, not just a Meta story.

- **AI agent intent leakage is the new agentic extraction frontier.** As LLM agents become consumer-facing tools, a novel attack surface is formalised this week: speculative tool calls pre-issued to third-party APIs before the user commits to a branch disclose inferred intent irrevocably, with no post-hoc cleanup possible. The "Ghost Tool Calls" paper (arXiv 2606.02483) is the first formalization; no existing authorization model (ACLs, read-only restrictions, commit-phase filters) addresses it. Consumer-side agent deployments now have an architectural privacy liability that the wiki didn't have a mechanism page for until this week.

- **DP-resistance of collective action now has formal lower bounds.** The Solanki et al. paper (arXiv 2505.05707, ACM 2026) closes an open question: how much harder does differential privacy make consumer collective action? The answer is quantified — critical-mass threshold scales inversely with the privacy budget, and the curve is steep. This formalises what was previously a qualitative "DP-as-firm-weapon" hazard in the wiki and raises the required coordination floor for Tier 1/2 builds significantly if target firms adopt DP.

- **The OSA privacy paradox is empirically confirmed.** Three natural experiments from UK Online Safety Act milestones show +100/217/415% spikes in VPN-related Reddit posts, with users explicitly citing surveillance distrust rather than access-seeking as the driver. This is the first rigorous empirical anchor for the wiki's regulation-as-extraction-vector thesis: safety mandates can inadvertently harm privacy by driving uncoordinated exit toward riskier alternatives. The pattern generalises beyond OSA — any regulation mandating surveillance infrastructure for a safety goal should be modeled against this failure mode.

- **The cooperative AI counter-architecture debate has a new design spec.** The "Dignity-Centric Stack" (arXiv 2606.06083) is the most architecturally detailed federated AI alternative to cloud extraction that the wiki has seen. It goes beyond Scholz's 7-layer framing (Solidarity Stack) by adding a formal proof of capture-resistance via vote/surplus accumulation. The harder problem — structural capture via dominant supplier withdrawal — is named but not solved. This is a useful specification anchor for exit-pathway strategy, with the honest disclaimer that polycentric substitutability at every layer is a precondition.

- **State-level surveillance pricing prohibition is forming a statutory patchwork.** NY One Fair Price Act passed the legislature June 4 (Hochul desk); combined with Maryland HB0895 and Connecticut PA 26-64, three states now have surveillance-pricing bans in different stages of implementation. Legal commentary is calling this a compliance crisis for retailers. Simultaneously, a DOJ criminal division head explicitly framed algorithmic pricing collusion as criminally actionable — a first. The patchwork itself is now wiki-level structural context for why the enforcement and legislative thread is accelerating this cycle. *(synthesis)*
