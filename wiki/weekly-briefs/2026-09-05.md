# Trends in the industry

- **On-policy distillation (OPD) research is maturing from algorithmic-variant spam into mechanistic self-study.** [[one-shot-opd-data-efficiency]] ("OPD II") doesn't propose a new algorithm — it asks *why* the seven-plus algorithmic OPD/OPSD variants already on this wiki's radar work, via new state-coverage and absorption-rate instruments, concluding OPD is "data-overfed but algorithm-starved." - **RLVR dense-reward design keeps producing new axes, not just new methods.** [[gradient-aligned-rewards-rlvr]] scores whole rollouts by gradient-cosine-similarity to an expert-CoT anchor — a rollout-level complement to this wiki's token-level credit-assignment cluster ([[token-gradient-cancellation]] / [[delta-token-credit]] / [[vimpo]]), at <9% overhead vs. 37–44% for prior gradient-based competitors.
- **Agent-skill distillation is now a four-consecutive-week theme**, not a one-off. [[disco-arex-skill]] joins [[skillopt]] (week 1), [[memoharness]]/[[argus-agentic-runtime]]/[[macaron-v1]] (week 2), and [[skill-self-play]] (week 3) — all converging on "operational knowledge" as a distinct lever from weights or harness. *(synthesis)*
- **Agentic-environment synthesis from trajectories extends last week's self-play/dynamic-environment thread.** [[terminal-universe]] (HF Daily Papers #1 this week) inverts the usual "environment → trajectory" direction into "trajectory → environment," and its own ablations show scaling *environment count* beats scaling queries or solutions per environment — a data-scaling lesson orthogonal to model or compute scaling.
- **Long-context/memory compression gains an external-interface entry distinct from the architecture-internal cluster.** [[latentpress]] compresses history into soft tokens read by a completely frozen, unmodified decoder — a different design axis from [[delta-mem]] / [[memagent]] / [[sst-v2]], which all modify attention or backbone state directly.

# Top 3 from the watchlist

1. **One-Shot OPD: Rethinking On-Policy Distillation II** — A single training query recovers 72–87% of full-data OPD's gain; 16 diverse queries match full-data training entirely.
