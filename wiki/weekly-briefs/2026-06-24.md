# Trends in the industry

- **Custom silicon race shifts from training to inference** — OpenAI's Jalapeño chip (Broadcom co-design, unveiled 2026-06-24) joins Google TPUs and Amazon Trainium as the third frontier-lab-owned inference ASIC. Jalapeño is inference-only — pre-training stays on Nvidia. The bet: inference is the repeating cost that compounds at scale; a custom chip purpose-built for real-time coding models can recoup its development investment through sustained margin improvement. OpenAI explicitly frames this as "operating across the stack" — chip architecture through product experience. The inference ASIC category is no longer experimental; it's now a structural feature of any frontier lab that expects to run at OpenAI-scale throughput.

- **Political risk is now an operational constraint for frontier AI** — The Trump administration's export-control order against Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (June 12, 2026) is the first time export-control law has been applied to restrict model *access* rather than chip shipments. The trigger (Amazon CEO Andy Jassy raising guardrail-bypass concerns with the White House) and the follow-on (Anthropic adding biometric ID verification for flagged Claude users, effective July 8) establish a new pattern: political relationships with the current administration are a material business risk, not just PR noise. OpenAI and Google have cultivated those relationships; Anthropic has not. The cybersecurity community called the order dangerous; Claude downloads spiked. Ironically, the crackdown may be good for Anthropic's brand with users who distrust government-tech alignment.

- **ChatGPT's monopoly-era is over — market has shifted to oligopoly** — Sensor Tower's State of AI Report (May 2026) puts ChatGPT at 46.4% market share, below 50% for the first time, despite reaching 1.1B monthly users. Gemini moved from 5.4% to 27.7% in six months — driven almost entirely by Android and Workspace default-on integration, not features. Claude holds 10.3% consumer share but leads the field in subscription conversion at 13% of users paying. The market is structurally shifting from a ChatGPT monopoly to a 3-player oligopoly; the path to share gain is now distribution integration (Gemini's strategy) or premium conversion (Claude's strategy), not just model quality.

- **M&A becomes Agentforce execution strategy** — Salesforce's $3.6B Fin acquisition (formerly Intercom, June 15, 2026) gives Agentforce a production-proven omnichannel customer-service agent and Fin's proprietary Apex model. Rather than building agent capabilities from scratch, Salesforce is now buying proven traction. This follows Snowflake's Natoma MCP gateway acquisition (June 2026), Microsoft's consistent acquisition of agent tooling, and the broader pattern: incumbents acquiring agent startups that have accumulated real enterprise deployments rather than competing with them on model quality alone.

- **"Not-acqui-hire" as a formalized AI deal structure** — Nvidia's December 2025 deal with Groq — $20B in LPU IP licensing + hiring founder Jonathan Ross plus most of the executive team — established a new M&A template. Groq raised $650M six months later (June 2026), new CEO from existing team, new exec bench, and pivoted fully to an inference neocloud. Scale AI survived the same pattern after Meta's $14.3B deal and is on track for $1B revenue. The template: pay for the IP and the people, let the entity survive with fresh capital, and gain a neocoud competitor in a market you now dominate technologically. Inference cloud (Groq, Baseten reportedly raising $1.5B, DeepInfra with $107M and 5T tokens/week) is the battleground.

- **Google's talent drain is structural, not anecdotal** — John Jumper (Nobel, AlphaFold 2) → Anthropic; Noam Shazeer (Transformer co-inventor) → OpenAI; Jonas Adler + Alexander Pritzel (Gemini contributors) → Anthropic; Andrej Karpathy → Anthropic (May 2026). All from Google in less than 60 days. Google is the primary talent pool for both OpenAI and Anthropic at the moment. Google's AI business remains at scale ($662M MAU for Gemini), but losing the researchers who built AlphaFold, the Transformer paper, and Gemini in a single quarter is notable.
