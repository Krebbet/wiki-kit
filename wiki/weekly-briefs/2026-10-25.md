# Trends in the industry
- **Anthropic is pushing back on multi-agent sprawl.** New Applied AI guidance says to fold router/planner/per-domain subagents into one loop with skills and tool search, keeping subagents only for context bloat, parallel fresh contexts, or tool/instruction conflicts. This sits against the 2026 subagent-by-default pattern and LangChain's "retrieve, offload, delegate" context-engineering post (same week). *(synthesis)*
- **Background agents are getting layered safety, not just approval prompts.** Claude Duet (research preview) combines an out-of-model auto-mode-derived approval classifier, an outbound sensitive-info classifier, credentials kept out of the model, and a dedicated browser VM; efficacy is unquantified. This follows the Nvidia/Microsoft sandbox work from earlier in October.
- **Harnesses are becoming user-programmable.** Claude Code Mods (code-executing plugins on harness events) and the Agent Skills donation to the Agentic AI Foundation point the same way: the harness is an extension surface. Early practitioner data shows the cost: a supervisor mod fired on 99 of 111 turns.
- **Self-improvement claims are meeting held-out evidence.** A controlled ARC-AGI-3 study of 27 self-revising harness lineages found +2.80 held-out versus +8.42 on dev, with the held-out interval spanning zero. It backs last week's selection/activation/attribution lens and the case for abstention and rollback.
- **Memory: simple baselines are pressing elaborate systems.** A raw dense-retrieval-plus-rerank baseline ranked first in 6 of 7 settings against 12 agentic memory systems, and fact extraction cost 19.6 points. Author-run, so collect-but-confirm; it strengthens the verbatim side of the open memory conflict.
- **Eval work is moving to modality and trace fidelity.** Sierra's τ-Entity and τ-i18n extend τ-bench to voice and five languages, and a paper argues behavioral-detector labels miss what the trace shows.
- **Signal-quality caveat.** Reddit, openai.com and marktechpost.com were unreachable this week; the scan relied on primary index pages, arXiv, HF Daily and Willison.
