# Trends in the industry

- **Location data is now constitutionally protected — but the commercial surveillance gap just got more visible.** *Chatrie v. United States* (SCOTUS, June 29) narrows the third-party doctrine for app-generated data, constraining government access. Simultaneously, the FTC permanently banned Kochava from selling sensitive location data without express opt-in. Both moves close government-access and broker-sale vectors — yet data brokers operating with consent-chain architectures remain entirely outside these rulings. The gap is precisely documented this week: "law closes the door on government; market surveillance drives a truck through the window."

- **Regulatory mandated APIs are emerging as a concrete counter-tool substrate.** The UK CMA's Google search data portability order (June 17, implementation ~September 2026) is the first competition-law instrument to position consumer-side authorized third parties — not just regulators or businesses — as legal data recipients. Cooperative price-comparison or demand-aggregation services can apply. This is distinct from DSAR (different legal basis, potentially different data scope), and the authorization process is still undefined — but the hook now has a statutory existence.

- **The state surveillance-pricing prohibition cluster is consolidating.** New York's One Fair Price Act (passed June 4, Hochul pending signature) makes NY the third state with a prohibition — and the first state with both a disclosure regime (APDA, 2025) and a prohibition stacked simultaneously. Reported private right of action (unconfirmed vs. bill text) would make this the first US state surveillance-pricing ban with consumer litigation standing. NJ and California are explicitly named as next candidates; diffusion is now an active multi-state legislative process.

- **Mobile SDK fingerprinting is structurally invisible to all current browser-layer counter-tools.** Specter et al. (arXiv 2506.22639) audit 228K SDKs and 178K Android apps and find that ad-focused regulation (Apple ATT, Privacy Sandbox) covers only 30% of fingerprinting SDK behavior. The remaining 70% sits in Security & Authentication, Analytics, and Tools categories — categories assumed to be privacy-neutral by most blocking tools. Browser defenses (Privacy Badger, uBlock, etc.) provide zero coverage for native-app SDK fingerprinting.

- **Consent-mechanism counter-power is under direct attack in the EU.** noyb reported that the EU Commission's plan to replace cookie banners with automated browser-signal-based consent was blocked by Germany, France, and Google at Council level. The consent-banner status quo — already documented as an enforcement failure at scale — is being actively defended by member states and industry against technical/systemic reform. The space for consent-as-lever is narrowing at the policy layer; technical consent-enforcement tools (GPC, Consent-O-Matic) are correspondingly more important as the only operative channel.
