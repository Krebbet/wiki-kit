# Trends in the industry

- **Non-developer agent adoption has crossed a new inflection point.** OpenAI's internal Codex data (June 26) shows Legal, Finance, and Recruiting completed their crossover by April 2026 — average lawyers and recruiters now generate >85% of output tokens on Codex. Non-developer user count grew 137–189× since August 2025; 25.6% of users delegated tasks estimated at >8 hours of equivalent human work; power users hit >60 agent-hours/day across parallel agents. The [[anthropic-internal-study]] framed the engineer-as-orchestrator shift; this is that same shift reaching non-technical departments at scale, displacing "AI assists specialists" with "AI does the task."

- **Process discipline is the missing evaluation layer for coding agents.** RigorBench (arXiv 2606.22678) finds that across 9 existing benchmarks — SWE-bench, BigCodeBench, DevBench, Terminal-Bench — none measure planning behavior, verification coverage, or recovery strategy. The process–outcome correlation is r=0.87: how an agent works is nearly as predictive as whether it works. The most striking finding: zero baseline agents abstained from any impossible task, while disciplined agents correctly refused ~62%. Evaluation is shifting from "did it succeed?" to "did it proceed correctly?" — a frame that makes outcome-only leaderboards look incomplete.

- **Agent memory: workload determines architecture, verbatim consistently beats extraction.** The first systematic 12-system cross-benchmark evaluation (arXiv 2606.24775) finds no universal winner — graph/temporal systems (Zep) lead knowledge-update; trace-preserving systems (MemoChat) lead task execution; raw long-context still leads some factual recall tasks. Verbatim raw storage outperforms LLM-extracted summaries on LoCoMo (24.2 vs. 8.5 EM); localized maintenance is ~20× cheaper per query than global reorganization. This is the fourth independent result (after GroupMemBench, EvoMemBench, LongMemEval) showing extraction-based systems underperform simpler approaches on specific task types.

- **The agent protocol landscape is formalizing toward a layered stack.** TU Munich's taxonomy of 9 actively-maintained protocols (arXiv 2606.19135) identifies a fundamental Agent Communication Trilemma (versatility vs. efficiency vs. portability) and predicts long-term convergence on an OSI-style federation: agents.json at discovery, MCP at tool-execution, session-aware evolving-schema protocols (Agora, ANP) at deliberation. Current gaps: discovery is underdeveloped (4 of 9 protocols use static config); no protocol addresses compliance or privacy at spec level; MCP is correctly classified as stateless/context-only by design — multi-agent use patterns are extensions, not its design intent.

- **Computer-use has a hard ceiling: nobody above 21% on 318-step real-world tasks.** OSWorld 2.0 (June 25) raises the bar from ~30-step scripted sequences to 318-step median workflows averaging 1.6 human-hours. Claude Opus 4.8 leads at 20.5% / 225K tokens; GPT-5.5 is most token-efficient at 14% / 37K tokens. Doubling token budget from ~110K to ~225K yields only 2.3 pp additional reward — a sharply nonlinear frontier. Computer-use is not approaching saturation; the prior benchmarks were too easy.
