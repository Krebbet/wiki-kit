# YC Summer 2026 (S26) Demo Day Batch Overview

Y Combinator's Summer 2026 Demo Day ran 2026-09-10 in San Francisco — 235 companies, the largest batch in YC history (+20% batch-over-batch), pitching ~1,500 investors (byteiota, 2026-09-16 capture). 52% B2B; AI companies are 60% of the cohort, up from ~40% two years prior. The batch-defining signal: agent infrastructure — identity, memory, evals, compute-cost routing — has crystallized into a distinct, dominant startup category, displacing the app-layer-agent framing that defined the prior (P26/Spring) batch.

## Batch stats (2026-09-10, byteiota)

- **235 companies** — largest YC batch ever, +20% vs. the prior (P26) batch's 194.
- **52% B2B**; consumer at 5.5%.
- **AI = 60% of the cohort**, up from ~40% two years ago (byteiota's own trend framing; the P26 page separately reports 95% of that batch "touch AI" — different measurement basis, not directly comparable).
- **Industrials surged to 23%** of the batch (from 12.8% in the prior batch) — humanoid/warehouse/data-center robotics (Nori, OS3, Manifold, Proprio Robotics) and robot-training infra (Robocurve, Markov). Byteiota's read: agent-infrastructure patterns (identity, evals, routing) are starting to transfer from software agents to physical ones.
- **FinTech dropped** from 10.2% to 6.8% of the batch.
- Across seven YC batches tracked (W25 through S26), infrastructure/devtools share rose from roughly a quarter of each batch to over two-fifths (byteiota, citing New Economies' YC S26 analysis).

## The agent-infra-as-category thesis

Byteiota's core claim (2026-09-16 capture): for the first time, the agent infrastructure stack looks like a real, layered stack rather than a grab-bag of hacks. Identity, memory, evaluation, and compute economics are each drawing dedicated, standalone companies in S26 — not features bolted onto a broader platform. The framing: an "AWS moment for agents," where developers assemble independent infra services rather than build each capability themselves. This is a shift from the P26 (Spring 2026) batch, whose dominant framing was still agent *applications* — vertical agents replacing specific jobs (see [[startups/yc-p26-2026-06]]) — even though P26 already had a large (55-company) "agent infrastructure" cluster.

Byteiota also flags convergence: at least four independent S26 companies attack different slices of the inference-cost stack alone (compute economics), which it reads as a signal the "dedicated tool" approach is about to displace roll-your-own routing/fine-tuning/eval-harness setups.

## Standout companies (agent infra)

### Inkbox
**Identity and communication layer for AI agents** | YC S26

One API gives an agent its own email inbox, phone number (call/text), iMessage handle, persistent internet address for webhooks, and an encrypted credential vault with TOTP/2FA — so agents can sign up for services, receive confirmation codes, and act on context across every channel without borrowing a human's credentials. Founders: Ray (CEO, MIT CS PhD, previously cofounded Empallo), Alex (CTO, Northeastern MS CS). Framed by byteiota as solving the "agent authenticates with borrowed user credentials" security/debugging problem — the identity layer of the emerging agent-infra stack.

### Understudy Labs
**Distills production LLM traffic into cheap fine-tuned open-weight models** | YC S26 (rebranding to "Orchestra" as of capture; also launched on YC as "self-optimizing inference cloud")

A drop-in gateway (Anthropic/OpenAI API-compatible) captures production LLM traces, turns them into evals that set a quality bar, then fine-tunes a smaller open-weight model on the same prompts/traffic. Deploys only if the smaller model clears the eval bar; refuses to ship regressions. Runs in Understudy's cloud, the customer's cloud, or on-prem — model weights are customer-owned. Pitch: cut inference spend on tasks that don't need frontier-model capability, per byteiota's "compute economics" framing.

### HyperProbe
**AI on-call / incident engineer** | YC S26 (note: some directory listings tag it YC S25 — verify batch before citing further)

Takes an open production incident to a confirmed root cause via read-only probes dropped into the running service (no redeploys/restarts) — captures exact variable state that logs miss, can place a virtual "breakpoint" on a suspect line mid-execution. Connects to PagerDuty/Datadog/Slack alerts; supports Node.js, TypeScript, Java, Kotlin; self-hosted or private-VPC; claims <1% overhead at 3,000 RPS. Founded 2026 by Karan Raina and Shailendra Singh, 8 employees, San Francisco. Pricing (as captured 2026-09-16): free for 1 service; Professional $99/service/mo ($79 annual, 3-service minimum); custom Enterprise.

**Caveat:** search results returned mixed batch attribution (one Hacker News source-of-truth tagged "YC S25"). Confirm S26 vs. S25 batch membership before treating HyperProbe as an S26-only data point.

### Assemble
**AI coworkers for enterprise IT** | YC S26

Agents that do development work inside core enterprise systems — ERP, CRM, HRIS (Salesforce, SAP, Workday, NetSuite, custom apps) — scoping dependencies, building logic, and validating changes. Targets the multi-month IT-implementation problem caused by accumulated custom logic/integrations in these platforms. Founded 2026 by Shaurnav Ghosh, Aliyan Ishfaq, and Shrish Janarthanan; 4 employees, San Francisco. Overlaps [[startups/yc-p26-2026-06]]'s Inth and qomplement (both enterprise-system automation) but targets IT/dev work directly on ERP/CRM/HRIS internals rather than compliance or supply-chain exceptions.

## Batch-over-batch: agent infra as a growing category

Reading the three most recent batches together (figures use each source's own taxonomy — categories are not identically defined, treat comparison as directional):

| Batch | Companies | AI share | Agent-infra framing |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| W26 (Winter, 2026-04 capture) | 199 | ~70% AI-adjacent | "Developer infrastructure" (agent infra, dev environments, monitoring) = 34 cos, 17% of batch (Extruct taxonomy) — [[landscape/yc-w26-ai-batch]] |
| P26 (Spring, 2026-06-16 capture) | 194 | 95% touch AI | "Agent Infrastructure" = 55 cos, largest single category in the batch (Fluenta taxonomy) — [[startups/yc-p26-2026-06]] |
| S26 (Summer, 2026-09-10 capture) | 235 | 60% AI (byteiota's own metric, different basis than P26's "95% touch AI") | Byteiota's headline claim: agent infra is now *the* dominant, crystallized category — a real layered stack (identity/memory/evals/compute-economics), not a features-of-a-platform grab bag; infra/devtools share of batches W25→S26 rose from ~25% to ~40%+ |

Directional read: agent infrastructure's *share* of each batch looks roughly flat-to-growing across W26→P26 (17%→28% of batch, respectively, on non-identical taxonomies), and S26's own trend claim (infra/devtools ~25%→40%+ across seven batches) supports continued growth into S26. But the underlying category definitions and data vendors differ per batch (Extruct → Fluenta → byteiota/New Economies), so treat the trend line as qualitatively consistent, not a clean quantitative series. What's clearer than the percentage: the *framing* has shifted batch-over-batch, from "agent infra as one cluster among many" (W26) to "infra is the largest single cluster" (P26) to "infra is now treated as a structurally distinct, maturing stack layer, displacing the app-layer framing" (S26).
