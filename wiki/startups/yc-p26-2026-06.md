# YC Spring 2026 (P26) — Demo Day Batch Overview

194 companies demoed on June 16 2026. ~61% B2B (119 companies); 95% touch AI; ~137 are building LLM agents. The batch skews heavily toward infrastructure — 55 companies in "agent infrastructure" (runtimes, sandboxes, memory, observability, payments, identity), 56 in "AI workforce" (vertical agents aimed at specific jobs), 39 in "physical world" (robotics, drones, defense), 44 in "care and capital" (health + fintech). Nobody pitches augmentation; the dominant framing is replacement, headcount reduction, and outcomes-based selling. Internal collision is acute: agent memory appears three times in a single group; agent sandboxes and testing four times. No company scored above 65.5 on Fluenta's six-signal public-data rubric — the batch landed in a tight band with no runaway. The recurring diagnosis: narrow down, not go wide.

## Notable companies (enterprise AI / agent infra / vertical agents)

### Netter
**Data ops for mid-market — "Palantir for mid-market"** | Agent Infrastructure · Data & Analytics | Fluenta score 62.4

Self-described as governed, agentic data access for companies too large for a spreadsheet but too small for Palantir's enterprise motion. Strong demand signal: 11.1k/mo searches, 94% transactional, KD 22. Real pain: Quora anxiety around Python/ETL obsolescence. The architecture is a governed data access layer sitting above existing sources, not a full data platform replacement.

**Why notable for this wiki:** Direct positioning against [[platforms/databricks|Databricks]] (moving downmarket) and Palantir (AIP, mid-market push). Also competes with Alteryx and Appian on the analytics/automation side. Tests whether "governed agentic data access" is a defensible category between self-serve BI and full AIP.

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** Fluenta flags a 559-month payback on the published $2–$250/mo self-serve pricing — the price band cannot fund the motion. Real ACV for mid-market data ops is $50k–$150k. The self-serve entry and the enterprise promise do not reconcile at current pricing. Databricks and Alteryx are actively moving downmarket with familiar tooling.

### Armature
**Product analytics for AI agents** | Agent Infrastructure · Developer Tools & Infrastructure | Fluenta score 63.2

Instruments agent sessions the way Amplitude/PostHog instrument user sessions: replay, funnel, retention for agent-driven workflows. Targeted at product and engineering teams building agents who need to know which agent paths succeed, where sessions drop off, and how agent behavior changes across model versions.

**Why notable:** First-mover claim in "agent analytics" as a distinct category from LLM observability (Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, Braintrust). The wedge is product metrics, not trace debugging. Relevant to any enterprise building agents on top of foundation models who has a product team (not just an infra team) asking questions.

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** 260 searches/mo; category must be created, not captured. Amplitude and PostHog could ship "agent sessions" as a minor feature extension on existing instrumentation pipes. $2–$49/mo against $156–$519 CAC = 14-month payback. Thin.

### Superlog
**Self-healing logging** | Agent Infrastructure · Developer Tools & Infrastructure | Fluenta score 63.2

Applies LLM reasoning to log streams to identify root cause and surface remediation — not just alerting but closing the loop. Competes in the observability/APM layer against Datadog, New Relic, Dynatrace, Splunk.

**Why notable:** Relevant to any enterprise team running agent workloads in production who wants automated log triage. The "self-healing" framing is the differentiation claim — if credible, reduces MTTR on agent failures.

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** Funded observability challengers (Lumigo $29M) got absorbed, not independent. Datadog can ship this as a feature sprint. CAC $580–$1,932. CPC $58 (the highest in the batch) signals expensive customer acquisition against a very entrenched buyer.

### Memory Store
**Managed memory layer for AI agents** | Agent Infrastructure · Productivity & Collaboration | Fluenta score 62.4

Provides persistent, cross-session memory for agents outside the context window — structured retrieval rather than naive context stuffing. Positioning: "memory, not bigger context windows."

**Why notable:** Competes in the most crowded sub-thesis in AI infra (mem0, Zep, Letta, Cognee all building the same thing). If persistent agent memory becomes a standard production requirement, this category consolidates around 1–2 vendors. Watching which one gets embedded into major agent frameworks first.

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** Three identical companies in the same YC cohort. If next-generation models ship durable native memory (Gemini/Claude long-context direction), the category shrinks to edge cases.

### Saffron
**AI-native technical assessment with AI-reliance scoring** | AI Workforce · HR, Hiring & Talent | Fluenta score 59.9

Scores how engineers actually use AI during assessments via session replay and "reliance scoring," rather than grading final code output. The 2026-native wedge: HackerRank and Codility cannot natively measure how much of the work an AI did vs. the candidate.

**Why notable:** Real demand growth signal (leading diff +1.79). Burning pain sourced from r/ycombinator (hiring managers), r/csMajors (Cluely-cheating complaints), and HN DevDay ("we hire engineers like it's 2015"). The assessment market ($HackerRank, Codility, CodeSignal, TestGorilla, SHL) has no native AI-reliance measurement — genuine gap in 2026.

**Competitive reads:** Could complement or compete with ATS vendors (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday) via embedded assessment API. Not directly competitive with Salesforce/Databricks ecosystem but relevant to any enterprise HR-tech integration conversation.

### qomplement
**Agentic ERP for supply-chain exception handling** | AI Meets the Real World · Mobility & Transportation | Fluenta score 60.1

Exception-handling, replenishment, and procurement workflow automation sitting above existing ERP/WMS systems for mid-market 3PLs and supply-chain ops teams. Demand: 5,790/mo at 100% transactional, KD 2. CPC $30.77 signals real budget. Category momentum: DualEntry $90M (Oct 2025), Doss $55M (Mar 2026).

**Why notable:** Direct play on the gap between Blue Yonder/IBM enterprise supply-chain and the mid-market firms that cannot afford full WMS implementation. The "agentic ERP" framing — agents handling exceptions rather than rules-engine automation — is the 2026 re-take on older supply-chain automation theses.

**Competitive reads:** Blue Yonder, IBM Supply Chain, Pando all moving toward AI-native supply chain automation. Relevant to Databricks customers running supply-chain analytics who need an action layer.

### Inth
**Privacy-compliance enforcement embedded in the codebase** | AI Workforce · Legal, Compliance & Govtech | Fluenta score 58.0

Enforces privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA, etc.) at the code level — intercepting violations before they reach production — rather than as a consent-management or policy-tracking layer on top. Targeting mid-market B2B engineering teams.

**Why notable:** Different architecture from OneTrust/TrustArc (consent management UI) and Vanta/Drata (SOC2 evidence collection). Code-level enforcement is the novel angle. Relevant to any engineering org that ships regulated software and wants shift-left compliance.

**Competitive reads:** Oblique pressure on Salesforce's compliance tooling and any enterprise data platform requiring data residency/privacy enforcement at the pipeline level.

### Cohesion
**Agentic teammate for buy-side investment research** | Care & Capital · Accounting/Finance | Fluenta score 59.8

Autonomous tracking and synthesis across differentiated datasets for public-equities investment teams. Competes with AlphaSense, BamSEC, Quartr, Koyfin on filings and transcript search — the differentiator claimed is agent-driven continuous monitoring rather than query-driven search.

**Why notable:** [[startups/alphasense|AlphaSense]] ($600M ARR as of June 2026) is the primary competitor. This is the clearest direct threat to AlphaSense from the P26 batch — positioned on the same buy-side analyst workflow, with agent autonomy as the wedge. Watch for whether Cohesion can close on enterprise relationships AlphaSense doesn't currently serve (smaller funds, smaller RMs).

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** AlphaSense already integrates dozens of datasets with enterprise search and owns the SERP. Buy-side trust bar is high — one hallucination on a tracked name kills the account. $100/mo pricing suggests SMB/prosumer entry, not enterprise motion.

### Clawvisor
**Access control and permissions for AI agents** | Agent Infrastructure · Cybersecurity & Identity | Fluenta score 54.8

Manages what actions and data AI agents are authorized to access at runtime — scoped permissions, policy enforcement, audit logging for agent deployments. Demand: 1,760/mo, 99% transactional, KD 9.

**Why notable:** Agent identity and access management is a real 2026 enterprise security gap. As organizations deploy agents into production with access to real systems, the authorization model (which was designed for human users) breaks down. The timing is sharp: Cisco and Okta are both shipping agent access control, confirming the category is real.

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** Cisco and Okta are both moving into this space as of June 2026. Seed startup selling security to enterprises who buy from incumbents faces a brutal clock. Priced at $700–$799/mo flat — enterprise security motion with SMB-tier pricing.

### HEVN
**Global banking and payments API for AI agents** | Care & Capital · Fintech & Payments | Fluenta score 56.3

Lets software teams and AI agents open real bank accounts, automate cross-border payroll, invoices, and payouts programmatically via API/CLI. Best demand profile in the payments cluster: 1,490/mo, 90% transactional, KD 24.

**Why notable:** "Agent-native financial infrastructure" is a genuine design requirement for autonomous agents that need to hold or move money. Relevant to any enterprise deploying financial workflow agents. Allowance (same batch) is a related but narrower play (rule-based virtual card spending for agents).

**Competitive reads:** Airwallex, Wise, Payoneer have cross-border payout APIs; Stripe has programmable card issuing. HEVN's moat claim is the agent-native surface (API/CLI design, not web UI) plus agent-specific policy primitives.

### Light Anchor
**AI-run consumer brand operating system** | AI Workforce · AI & Automation | Fluenta score 39.1

Operates e-commerce brands end-to-end via AI agents — marketing, sourcing, operations, support — with minimal human involvement. Currently running one flagship brand (Seoul Dispatch) and multiple experiment stores. Founders previously led AI agent product at Sendbird (YC W16). The architecture is a shared agent platform that compounds across brands: each new brand launched improves the shared model rather than starting from scratch.

**Why notable (architecture, not score):** Light Anchor is architecturally distinct from anything else in the batch and from tracked enterprise vendors. The model is not "AI tool for brand managers" but "AI as the operating entity of a brand" — humans are shareholders, not operators. This is the most explicit enterprise-level test of whether AI agents can own a P&L. The vision (thousands of compute-capped brands) is closer to an AI holding company than a SaaS product.

**Out-of-scope verdict:** Consumer brand operations — out of scope for this wiki's enterprise/B2B AI focus. Noted for architecture novelty. Relevant as a stress test of agentic autonomy claims. Watch for whether the model generalizes to B2B back-office operations (if agents can run a DTC brand, the same architecture applies to a cost center).

**Risk flags (2026-06-16):** Fluenta score 39.1 — weakest in the batch on public-data signals. Zero organic search demand, 95-month payback at stated pricing, $725M in category capital (Quince $500M, Kai $125M, Oro Labs $100M) without an agent-operated-brand breakout. Daydream (Unilever, Sephora) and Gorgias already serve this market.

## Batch-level themes

**Agent supply chain, not agent apps.** The dominant batch thesis is infrastructure for agents — payments, identity, memory, sandboxes, observability, testing — not end-user agent applications. The market is building the supply chain before the demand is fully proven.

**Replacement framing.** Every pitch is headcount reduction or outcome replacement, not "helps your team." This is a signaling shift from 2024–2025 YC batches where augmentation was the safe framing.

**Internal collision is acute.** Agent memory (3 companies), agent sandboxes/testing (4 companies), AI-SDR (multiple companies). For most of these categories the first diligence question is intra-cohort differentiation, not market size.

**Monetization is the trap.** Fluenta's core finding: the payback math on the highest-scoring companies (by their scoring model) is often the most broken. Netter 559 months, Amboras 511, Replicas 508. Strong monetization scores do not mean the CAC/ACV assumptions are coherent — the pricing is almost always priced for self-serve while the product requires enterprise motion.

**Tight score band, no runaway.** Scores range 65.5 (Kuli) to 32.7 (RentAHuman). No company dominated all six signals simultaneously. The scoring consistently recommended narrowing rather than expanding.

**"Palantir for X" and "Databricks for X" pitches.** Multiple companies in the data/analytics group are positioning against Palantir (Netter) or the Databricks/Alteryx ecosystem. As Databricks moves downmarket and Palantir extends AIP, these entrants are betting the established players cannot serve the mid-market with the same economics. Jury is out.
