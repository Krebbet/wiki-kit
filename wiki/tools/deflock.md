# DeFlock

DeFlock.me is a crowdsourced mapping project that documents the physical locations of Flock Safety automated license plate reader (ALPR) cameras across the United States. Created by Will Freeman, the site inverts the surveillance gaze: rather than accepting opaque police-tech infrastructure as a given, volunteers geolocate individual cameras—reaching approximately 90,000 documented units across 50+ cities—and publish the results as a public map, enabling privacy-aware route planning and generating actionable evidence for municipal contract cancellation campaigns. When Flock Safety issued a cease-and-desist citing trademark law in an attempt to silence the site, Freeman partnered with EFF, which defended DeFlock as First Amendment-protected criticism; Flock Safety backed down. The tool demonstrates that crowdsourced, citizen-generated data can both expose surveillance infrastructure and anchor legal defenses against corporate suppression of counter-tools.

## What DeFlock Does

**Threat addressed.** Flock Safety sells ALPR camera systems to police departments and private communities. Cameras scan every passing vehicle's license plate, timestamp, and location, feeding a shared nationwide database accessible to law enforcement. The network's geographic footprint is otherwise invisible to residents.

**Mechanism.** Volunteers submit camera locations via DeFlock.me; submissions are aggregated into a public map. Users can consult the map for privacy-aware routing. The documented inventory (~90,000 cameras) makes Flock Safety's network scope concrete and legible to journalists, advocacy groups, and city councils.

**Outcomes.** Over 50 cities have canceled or declined Flock Safety contracts, an outcome Freeman and EFF attribute in part to the public pressure DeFlock's data enables.

**Legal confrontation.** Flock Safety sent a cease-and-desist letter alleging trademark violations—a pattern consistent with using IP law to suppress criticism rather than protect a genuine mark. EFF intervened, characterized the site as protected speech and grassroots advocacy, and Flock Safety withdrew its legal threat. Freeman has noted that without institutional legal support, most individuals would capitulate to such letters; EFF's involvement was structurally decisive.
