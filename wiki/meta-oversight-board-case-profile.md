# Meta Oversight Board Case Profile

The Oversight Board — a body Meta itself created, funds through an irrevocable trust, and designed to be able to overrule its own content decisions — is the richest single test case in this wiki for **D112** (formal/real authority gap, Aghion & Tirole): a body with textbook "binding" formal decision rights whose real authority is bounded, at several distinct points, by judgments Meta itself continues to make.

**Evidence tier note.** **[empirical]**, from the Oversight Board's own Charter and Bylaws (June 2025) — Meta's and the Board's own constitutive documents, read for what they actually grant and withhold, not for either party's characterisation of the arrangement.

## The binding clause, and its qualification, in the same sentence

**[empirical]** Charter, Article 4: "The board's resolution of each case shall be binding and Meta will implement it promptly, **unless implementation of a resolution could violate the law**." Bylaws §2.3 restates the same structure. This single sentence is D112's formal/real authority gap in miniature: a clean grant of binding authority, immediately conditioned on Meta's own (not externally adjudicated) legal judgment. **[wiki synthesis]** No mechanism in either document specifies who determines whether a given implementation "could violate the law," or what happens if the Board disagrees with Meta's determination — the qualification is real, stated plainly, and structurally unresolved.

## Four further places the real/formal gap runs

**[empirical]**
1. **Remedy scope is Meta's to define**: Charter Art. 3 §5: "Meta may make additional resolutions or other technical remedies available to the board" — the Board's toolkit for resolving a case is bounded by what Meta chooses to make available.
2. **Extension to duplicate content is feasibility-gated, not automatic**: both documents require Meta to act on identical content elsewhere only after "analyzing whether it is technically and operationally feasible" — a term neither document defines or bounds.
3. **General support is resource-conditioned**: Charter Art. 5 §3: Meta supports Board requests "to the extent that requests are technically and operationally feasible and consistent with a reasonable allocation of Meta's resources."
4. **Meta holds a permanent veto over its own self-binding**: the Charter can be amended "only with... the agreement of Meta and a majority of the board" (Art. 6 §1) — meaning the entire arrangement, including the binding clause itself, remains renegotiable at Meta's option, subject to Board agreement. **[wiki synthesis]** This is functionally closer to a revocable, continuously renegotiated delegation than an irreversible transfer of authority, whatever either party's public description of it says — directly the caution this wiki's [[formal-and-real-authority]] page (Aghion & Tirole) already carries in the abstract, here confirmed on a real, named, current institution.

## Where the independence is real, not nominal

**[empirical]** Funding runs Meta → irrevocable Trust → LLC → Board, and **neither document contains any provision letting Meta alter, condition, or withdraw funding once committed to the Trust** — the irrevocability is total as written, and member compensation is explicitly insulated from case outcomes ("will not be conditioned or withheld based on the outcome of board decisions"). Case *selection* is genuinely the Board's own: "The board has sole discretion to accept or reject cases" (Bylaws §2.1.1), and Meta cannot compel review of a referred case. Board-member removal requires a Code-of-Conduct violation, explicitly **not** content decisions, decided by a two-thirds board vote subject to the Individual Managers' and Trustees' approval — bodies structurally once-removed from Meta itself. **[wiki synthesis]** This is a genuinely mixed case, worth stating plainly rather than resolving toward either "real oversight" or "corporate theatre": funding independence and case-selection independence are both real and specifically engineered (the irrevocable trust is a credible-commitment device in the sense of [[credible-commitment]]); implementation authority is real but law-and-feasibility-qualified; and the terms of the whole arrangement remain within Meta's continuing consent.

## Operational veto-point data, for once fully countable

**[empirical]** Case panels: 5 members per standard case, drawn from a variable-size pool (no fixed board size — "calibrated to accommodate case volume and scope"), with a randomly-assigned regional member. All panelists must participate or the case reschedules; abstention is disallowed. A full-board re-review requires a majority vote. Bylaws amendment requires a two-thirds board vote. Member removal for cause requires two-thirds of the board (excluding the member in question) plus Manager approval with Trustee consent. **[wiki synthesis]** This is a complete, documented veto-point/threshold inventory of the kind [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D44 asks for — unusually, for an internal governance body rather than a legislature, and unusually complete because bylaws-level operational documents rarely survive into this wiki's captures.

## A working compliance-tracking mechanism

**[empirical]** Meta has 7 days to implement a binding decision; the Board's mandatory Annual Report must include "a report on the timeliness of Meta's implementation and response to board decisions and recommendations," and Meta must respond publicly through its own newsroom to every decision. Non-binding policy recommendations get a required public response within 60 days — response, not adoption. **No penalty or escalation mechanism is specified anywhere in either document for a missed deadline** — the enforcement mechanism, such as it is, is public disclosure of a compliance record, not a sanction.
