# Covenants With and Without a Sword

Ostrom, Walker & Gardner's laboratory experiment isolates, for the first time in this wiki with a controlled counterfactual, what field data on commons governance cannot cleanly separate: the causal contribution of **communication alone** versus **communication plus self-imposed sanctioning** to cooperation in a repeated common-pool-resource game. Against Hobbes's "covenants, without the sword, are but words," cheap-talk communication — with zero change to the payoff structure and zero enforceability — raised net yield from a 21–35% (baseline, no communication) range to 55–99%, even though the game-theoretic equilibrium prediction is completely unaffected by talk.

**Evidence tier note.** **[empirical]** — a controlled lab experiment (n=8 subjects/group, Indiana University undergraduates, 20+ rounds, real cash payoffs), the strongest-design source in this job.

## Covenants alone: real, partial, and decaying

**[empirical]** Repeated communication (a discussion opportunity after every round) achieved 99% of optimal yield at low endowment (5% defection) but only 73% at high endowment (13% defection) — "a more challenging decision environment," since as few as 3 of 8 subjects overinvesting could ruin the high-endowment commons versus 7 of 8 at low endowment. One-shot communication (a single 10-minute discussion) produced a large jump (from −27% to 74% in the immediately following rounds) that decayed to a 55% average over the full post-communication phase, with 25% defection. **Communication is causally sufficient for substantial improvement with zero enforceability — but incomplete and unstable, especially under harder payoff structures.**

## Swords without covenants: worse than nothing, net of cost

**[empirical]** An imposed costly sanctioning mechanism, introduced with **no communication permitted**, raised gross yield from −38% to 37% — but netting out the cost of fees and fines paid, net yield rose to only **9%**. Of 176 observed sanctioning instances, 77% targeted the prior round's heaviest investors (as intended), but 16% were "lagged punishment" or "blind revenge" — misdirected sanctioning with no efficiency payoff. **A formal sanctioning mechanism imposed without any communication channel performed worse than communication alone**, once its own costs are counted.

## Best result: sanctioning chosen, not imposed, alongside communication

**[empirical]** When subjects had one-shot communication *and* a majority vote on whether to adopt a sanctioning mechanism, groups that chose to adopt it averaged **93% gross / 90% net yield, 4% defection** — outperforming even a mechanism the experimenters imposed alongside communication (85% gross / 67% net). Groups that voted the mechanism down averaged only 56%, with defection as high as 42% in one case. Of 32 subjects across four experiments, 11 of the 14 who voted against adopting sanctioning had previously experienced a harsh, badly-calibrated fee/fine ratio in the sanctioning-alone treatment — **prior bad experience with a punitive institution measurably discouraged adopting a beneficial one later**, a hysteresis effect.

## What this confirms and sharpens in the wiki's existing Ostrom material

**[wiki synthesis]** This is a controlled isolation of mechanisms this wiki's field-based design-principle content (Cox, Arnold & Villamayor-Tomás's 91-study meta-analysis, already the anchor of [[polycentric-governance]]) cannot separate, since real commons institutions rarely have graduated sanctions without also having communication. Two results sharpen existing rows rather than requiring new ones: (i) **D5** (self-designed sanctioning) is extended with a direct experimental confirmation that *self-chosen* sanctioning outperforms *externally imposed* sanctioning of the identical type — a clean causal comparison field data cannot supply; (ii) the finding that imposed sanctioning without communication is actively counterproductive once costs are netted is a caution worth attaching to any wiki claim that formal sanctioning mechanisms are unconditionally beneficial.
