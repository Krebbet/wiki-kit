# Stop-and-Scan Room Mapping with a Rover — stations, odometry-backed pose graph, floor-plan product, self-evaluation

**Summary.** Stop-and-scan is the oldest mapping protocol in robotics and the standard one in surveying: scan only when stationary, plan the next station from what you have, use odometry as the *initial estimate* and registration as the *correction* — the RTC360's field workflow tolerates a 5 %-of-path odometry prior and our rover already beats that (2 cm / 0.5 m ≈ 4 %). It buys a scan with zero motion distortion (our 0.1 cm per-scan wall RMS versus the 16 cm band of the hand-held EDA064 map) and an odometry backbone that turns the pose-graph problem from "all-pairs with ambiguity alarms" into "chain plus verified revisits"; it costs time and a set of protocol rules about *where* to stop. In a small cluttered room the binding constraints on station placement are not range or overlap (both are enormous at 12 m range in a 3 × 4 m room) but **observability (≥ 2 non-parallel surfaces per station), incidence angle (≤ 70°), wall length subtended (≥ 20 returns), the 24° blind cone, and the desk the 1.1 m plane cannot see**.

> **Scan-plane note (librarian, 2026-09-24).** The "~1.1 m" scan plane on this page is the **June capture rig** (a tripod carrying the stereo camera, `data/calib/rig_geometry.json`). The **prototype rover** scans at **0.2972 m** — sd 0.5 mm, seven independent down angles at open floor, drone-prototype `eda/EDA228-actuator-verification/FINDING.md` (2026-09-24; an earlier 0.337 m was wall hits read as floor). **Read every 1.1 m hazard on this page against 0.2972 m.** The rover does not pass *over* a desk or a counter — it passes **under** them and cuts their legs, so §Hazards' "the desk footprint reads free" changes shape rather than vanishing: legs are marked, the span between them is still certified free. What the plane genuinely cannot see is now everything **above** ~0.3 m (tops, counters, shelves), which is what the tilt ladder is for. Canonical statement and the tilt consequences: [[tilting-2d-lidar-multiplane-capture]] §CORRECTION.

## 0. What the June recipes settled, and what changes on a rover

Already settled ([[lidar-multiscan-capture-recipe]], [[perimeter-capture-recipe]]): a static multi-revolution scan gives a 0.2–0.5 cm wall band; coverage, not data quality, is the limit; each new position must re-see **≥ 2 non-parallel walls** already seen (two shared parallel walls leave the along-wall translation unobservable, EDA058 §3c); a near-square room has a ±90° registration ambiguity that only a *known* rotation between positions resolves; stand off-centre near corners; constant sensor height; perimeter loop rather than interior criss-cross. All of that carries over verbatim.

What is different for the rover: (1) it has **odometry** (σ ≈ 2 cm / 3° per move, [[odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]]) so the ambiguity and the registration seed are no longer guessed; (2) it has a **24° blind cone** behind (bearings 164–188°) instead of an operator's body; (3) it is **short and cannot see the desk** — the 1.1 m plane makes the desk footprint read as free (the "counter certifies false free space" hazard, [[lidar-floorplan-extraction]] §6.5); (4) the moves are **discrete and repeatable**, so revisits are exact stations rather than "somewhere near the start"; (5) its guard sees only a forward corridor while driving.

## 1. Stop-and-scan as a method

**Where it is used.** Surmann, Nüchter & Hertzberg's 2003 Kurt3D system is the canonical robotic instance: *"the digitalization process is a stop, scan, plan and go setting"*; the odometry pose *"serves as a first estimate and is corrected by registration"*; the next-best-view maximises information gain penalised by distance and turn, `V̂(p) = V(p)·exp(−c₁‖r−p‖)·exp(−c₂|θr−θp|)` with c₁ = 0.05 cm⁻¹, c₂ = 0.01; two scans count as overlapping *"iff more than 250 corresponding point pairs exist"*, and the global step is a queue-driven simultaneous ICP against the union of all overlapping neighbours *(demoed)* [src: surmann-2003]. González-Baños & Latombe (2002) is the 2D-laser version: sense at a stop, compute the *safe region* (largest region guaranteed free from the readings so far), sample candidates inside it, score by visibility gain *"based on sensor range and expected overlap of previous mapped regions"* minus travel [src: gonzalez-banos-2002, quoted via robotic-exploration-survey]. In surveying, every tripod TLS survey is stop-and-scan; Soudarissanane & Lindenbergh formalise station planning as coverage of a **2D horizontal cross-section at sensor height** under a range constraint and an **incidence-angle constraint of α_max = 70°**, noting that *"too little acquisitions and far apart might result in scans that cannot be registered due to insufficient overlap"* while too many are wasted [src: soudarissanane-2011]. Cloud-to-cloud field guidance is **≥ 20–25 % overlap** between adjacent scans [src: lsf-overlap]. The shipping modern form is Leica's RTC360: a visual-inertial system tracks the scanner *between* setups, designed so the *"deviation of the estimated position amounts to a maximum of 5 % of the path length between the two viewpoints"* — chosen because *"with sufficient scan overlap, it allows a sufficiently accurate starting value for an automatic cloud-to-cloud registration"*, followed by bundle adjustment of the between-setup track and cloud-to-cloud refinement *(shipping)* [src: leica-vis]. A 2024 quadruped stop-and-go TLS system registered 18 stations fully automatically at 4.4 cm, 100 % success, in 424 s *(demoed)* [src: park-2024]. Zebedee is the continuous-motion counter-example: a spring-mounted 2D scanner whose *every* scan is motion-distorted and must be solved by continuous-time trajectory estimation [src: zebedee].

**What it buys.** No intra-scan distortion: [[lidar-floorplan-extraction]] §6.4 derives 7–17 cm of yaw-induced skew at 28–67 °/s and 3 m range, and the hand-held EDA064 map bottomed out at a 16 cm band with E1 (motion distortion) as the dominant residual; our rest scans measure 0.1 cm wall RMS. No deskew code path. Every station scan is a *label-quality* keyframe, so the map keyframes are also the odometry labels (EDA216). Exact revisits: closure is measured between two settled scans at the same station, not inferred.

**What it costs.** Time (one station ≈ move 3–5 s + settle 0.3 s + 3–5 revolutions 0.3–0.5 s + registration); a planning problem instead of a driving problem; and the odometry heading term (3°/turn) is spent on every 90° turn, so the protocol should minimise turns [src: odometry-closed-loop-pipeline].

**Station planning in a small cluttered room — the actual constraints, with numbers.**
- *Prior in basin.* Every mainstream matcher accepts ±0.1–0.25 m / ±20° per step ([[odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]] §2). With σ = 2 cm / 3° per move, a station-to-station move of ≤ 1 m and ≤ 90° lands the prior 5–10× inside the basin even at 3σ. **Spacing is therefore not limited by odometry.**
- *Overlap.* Two stations ≤ 1.5 m apart in a 3 × 4 m room see the same walls; overlap is > 50 % by construction, far above the 20–25 % TLS rule [src: lsf-overlap]. Overlap collapses only across an occluder (desk, cabinet) or through a door — those are exactly the places to add an intermediate station.
- *Wall length subtended (the real spacing driver).* At 0.72° a wall of length L at range d yields ≈ L/(d·0.01257) returns; the wiki's noise-floor table needs ≥ 20 returns for σ_angle ≈ 1.3° (1 m wall at 4 m) and ≈ 57–100 for 0.2–0.3° [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §6.6]. Rule: **every wall you intend to assert must lie within ~3 m of at least two stations**; a 1 m wall stub needs a station within ~2 m.
- *Incidence angle.* Keep the asserting observations at ≤ 70° incidence [src: soudarissanane-2011]; a station near a corner sees both adjoining walls at ≤ 45°.
- *Observability.* ≥ 2 non-parallel surfaces per station (Censi's corridor degeneracy, [[weak-scan-registration-methods]] §0); check it *numerically* at the station with the point-to-line Hessian eigenvalue ratio before accepting the scan as a keyframe ([[measurement-selection-uncertainty]] §1).
- *Blind cone.* A station scan is missing its rear 24°; whatever lies there is *occluded*, not free. Alternate headings (about-face at lane ends) so every wall run is seen outside the cone from some station.
- *Minimum station count.* Soudarissanane's simple room is covered from one viewpoint even under the 70° constraint; a complex room with occlusions needs 10–19 [src: soudarissanane-2011]. For a ~3 × 4 m room with a desk: **6–10 stations** (perimeter ring at ~1 m from walls, 0.5–1 m spacing, plus one behind each occluder) is the practical range; the 2.8 × 1.0 m lane is 5–7 stations at 0.5 m.
- *Next station heuristic.* Keep it deterministic first ([[active-slam-exploration]] §9-A): perimeter stations in order, then interior. If a frontier layer is added, use Surmann's form — gain × exp(−c₁·distance) × exp(−c₂·turn) — with the turn penalty **on** (`explore_lite` ships `orientation_scale = 0`), plus a hard ≥ 2-surface observability gate [src: surmann-2003, active-slam-exploration].

## 2. Pose graph with odometry factors

**Edge information from measured σ.** Information = Σ⁻¹. Odometry edge per move: Ω_odo = diag(1/0.02², 1/0.02², 1/0.0524²) = diag(2500, 2500, 365) in (m⁻², m⁻², rad⁻²) — GTSAM's `BetweenFactor<Pose2>` with `noiseModel::Diagonal::Sigmas(Vector3(0.02, 0.02, 0.052))` [src: gtsam-pose2]. Registration edge from rest-scan ICP (label quality ≈ 0.2–0.3 cm / 0.1–0.2°): Ω_reg ≈ diag(1–2·10⁵, 1–2·10⁵, 1–3·10⁵). So registration edges are 50–100× stiffer in translation and ~500× in heading; the odometry edge is a *soft backbone*, not a competitor *(derived)*. Two rules from the literature: cut the odometry **rotation** weight further if scan-to-map residuals show heading fights (Cartographer RFC 18 keeps wheel translation, drops wheel rotation) [src: odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]; and do **not** trust ICP's self-reported covariance for Ω_reg — Censi/ICET show it is not conservative — use the empirical label σ from EDA216 or CSM's volume covariance [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §1].

**Why the backbone matters beyond the prior.** Every robust back-end that failed silently in the hand-held analysis (DCS, max-mixtures, RRR) failed *because there was no odometry chain*; with one, they are usable and the χ² composed-loop test (PCM, χ²₃: 95 % → 7.815) has a real statistic to test against [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §2.3]. EDA064 showed Huber alone accepts a gross false loop against *soft* odometry but rejects it against 100× stiffer odometry — the stop-and-scan graph is the stiff case.

**Loop closure in one room.** SLAM Toolbox's detector cannot fire (every node is graph-linked within 3 m) and Cartographer's submaps have no valid size ([[lidar-floorplan-extraction]] §2.1). What works is explicit **station-to-station registration with a gate**: for every pair of stations whose predicted overlap (from the current graph) exceeds a threshold, run CSM-seeded PLICP from the graph-predicted relative pose; accept as an edge if inlier fraction ≥ 0.8, RMS ≤ 0.5 cm, correction from the prior ≤ 3σ_prior, Hessian well-conditioned (λ_min/λ_max above the normal-spread rank threshold), and the composed-loop χ²₃ ≤ 7.815. This is EDA064's verification gate (≥120 inliers ∧ RMS ≤ 5 cm ∧ correction ≤ 0.6 m) tightened by an order of magnitude because the scans are settled. Add **deliberate revisit stations** (return to station 0 at the end; one mid-run revisit) so closure is a measurement, not a hope.

**Symmetric-room ambiguity.** A rectangular lane is 180°-symmetric (a square, 90°); EDA058 could only flag it, and the campaign runner already had to gate registration to 0.3 m / 30° of the prior to escape *"the symmetric-lane basin trap"* (`campaign.py` §pose_vs_reference). With a 3° heading prior the wrong basin is 60σ away — the odometry prior kills it outright; keep the gate, and keep Olson's λ₁/λ₂ ≥ 2 alarm on the all-pairs hypothesis set as the independent check [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §2.3].

**Global optimisation with < 50 stations.** Cost is nil (g2o: 943 poses in 2.5 ms; 40 stations with ~500 verified pairs is well under a second) [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §2.6]. Pairwise-plus-verification alone leaves the closure residual sitting on whichever edge was last; a batch solve distributes it and yields per-edge residuals and marginal covariances — the two evaluation quantities §4 needs. Run it, with a robust kernel (GNC-TLS or Huber given the stiff backbone), after every accepted revisit; the joint pose+map (BALM-style) refinement of §2.4 remains optional polish.

## 3. Map product: grid, lines, doors

**Grid.** Ray-carve an occupancy grid at **2 cm** (C1 accuracy ±30 mm, resolution 15 mm; 5 cm is Cartographer/SLAM Toolbox default and Turner & Zakhor's quadtree) with the **three-way label** occupied / empty / occluded per station and the Adán–Huber rule "occluded only if occluded from every station" [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §5.3]. Carve *only* along station→return rays truncated at the first return and along the **driven** station→station segments (Turner & Zakhor's pose-to-pose carving) — never from the free-looking desk footprint, which the 1.1 m plane cannot certify. The grid is the navigation/costmap product and the ray-visibility ledger; it is not the floor plan.

**Lines.** Extract per station with ordered split-and-merge (Nguyen et al.: fastest and lowest false-positive class; RANSAC/Hough on an accumulated cloud discard scan order) and merge segments across stations with each segment carrying its observing station [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §3.1]. Then closure by Delaunay + line-of-sight carving (formulation B) rather than corner assembly (formulation A, the E13 failure) [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §3.3].

**"Reliable pieces" → asserted vs flagged** ([[robust-evidence-mapping-principle]]): a wall segment is **asserted** if seen from ≥ 2 stations separated by ≥ 0.3 m, incidence ≤ 70° at at least one, per-station lines agree within 1 cm offset / 1° angle, and length ≥ 0.5 m; **flagged** ("unconfirmed structure") if seen from one station or only inside the blind-cone margin or at > 70° incidence; **unknown** where the three-way label is occluded. Return density is not evidence (the kitchen cabinet-front wall pick) — viewpoint diversity is [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §6.5].

**Doors — the trajectory-crossing certificate.** Nikoohemat et al. detect doors as voxels with wall above, a trajectory point within ~15 cm, and (for open doors) void within ~50 cm: 12 of 17 doors found, including 4 *closed* ones, which no geometry-only method can see [src: nikoohemat-2017]. EDA211 reproduced the idea on the kitchen: 1 crossing, 15.4 cm from the GT open door, 0 false positives on 3 closed doors. A rover *certifies* a door by driving through it: a pose-graph edge whose endpoints lie on opposite sides of an asserted wall line, with the crossing point on that line and the guard's forward corridor clear during the move. That is the door's aperture position (to ≈ rover width); its width comes from the empty-label run either side. A door the rover never drove through stays a flagged `empty` run, never a certified door.

## 5. Hazards specific to this rig

- **1.1 m plane.** Desk tops (~0.75 m) and chair seats/backs (~0.45/0.9 m) are below it; monitors and shelf fronts are in it. Rays pass over the desk to the wall and certify the desk footprint free — the counter hazard, measured here as 1.8 m² per 3 m of counter [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §6.5]. The guard uses the same plane and cannot save you. Mitigation: the station set is confined to a **human-verified drivable polygon** (the lane); free space outside it is *unknown* regardless of rays; label the desk region from the flagged monitor/edge returns.
- **Blind cone (164–188°)** *(measured 2026-09-17: two rig zones, mast 163–188.5° and screw 234.5–248.5°, both filtered at the device — §What this rig measured)*. Rear 24° per station is occluded, not free; alternate headings; never let an asserted wall rest only on returns within 5° of the cone edge.
- **Glass / mirrors.** The C1's `quality` is a hard-coded 47, so intensity detectors are unavailable; glass is invisible except within ±2° of normal, mirrors map geometry behind their plane; a plain grid keeps half the glass returns and a quarter of the phantoms — use a Reflectance-Field-Map-style ray-geometry test and flag, never assert, any wall run seen only near-normal from one station [src: lidar-floorplan-extraction §6.1–6.2].
- **People.** A pauser needs ~15 s of clear observation to erase and corrupts *poses* not just points; the mapping-while-following study puts GMapping at 39–80 cm error with 1–2 walkers [src: mapping-while-following]. Rule: a station scan whose revolutions disagree (> 1 cm on any wall) is discarded and re-taken; a run with a walk-through is re-run.
- **Guard forward-only.** Drive to a station only along a path whose corridor the guard covers: forward moves and in-place turns; a reverse or strafe move is allowed only after the current station scan shows the rear/side corridor clear (the runner's `clear_behind` check).
- **Two surfaces / rug edge.** A floor bump injects a permanent Δθ ≈ ΔD/b and the mecanum effective track is floor-dependent (1.65×, two floors) [src: odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]. Put a station immediately before and after the transition so the crossing is one short odometry edge with inflated Ω (e.g. σθ = 6°), and let registration carry it.

## 6. Automation: the mapping runner

The odometry campaign runner already drives, settles, scans, and station-keeps against a reference scan; the episode logger records every rest scan, command, telemetry and guard event (`episode.py`: `rest`, `move_start/end`, `scan`, `guard`). A mapping runner adds five things: (1) a **station list** (planned poses in the map frame) with a per-station heading; (2) **drive-to-station** as a sequence of turn/forward moves through the drivable polygon, each ≤ 1 m / ≤ 90°; (3) **settle + N ≥ 3 revolutions**, median-fused, with a revolution-disagreement check; (4) **register with prior** — CSM-seeded PLICP against the current map from the odometry-predicted pose, gates of §2, edge inserted, batch solve if a revisit closed; (5) **decide next** — next station in the list, an inserted intermediate station if overlap or observability fails, a revisit if the marginal covariance of the current station exceeds a bound, stop when the list is exhausted *and* §4 criteria are met (termination on agreement, not coverage, [[active-slam-exploration]] §5). Match the SAE "final stabilisation" pattern: end with the return to station 0.

**Log for re-processing** (beyond what the logger records): station id and planned pose; odometry Δ and Ω per edge; per-revolution raw scans (not just the median); settle time and vbat; registration record (seed, result, inliers, RMS, Hessian eigenvalues/vectors, gate outcomes); every candidate pair tried and why it was rejected; the solved graph after each batch; the drivable polygon; a `note` for anything the operator saw (person, door state). Raw immutable, labels versioned — the existing layout.

## Recommendation for this rig — the mapping protocol

1. **Define the drivable polygon by hand** (the 2.8 × 1.0 m lane) and mark rug edges; nothing outside it is ever labelled free.
2. **Plan 6–7 stations** along the lane at 0.5 m spacing, ~0.5 m from the long wall, plus one station at each lane end with the heading reversed (about-face) so the blind cone swaps sides; add one station before and one after the rug edge.
3. **Per station:** stop, settle ≥ 0.3 s, capture 5 revolutions, reject the station if any revolution's walls disagree > 1 cm, median-fuse; compute the point-to-line Hessian and reject a keyframe with λ_min/λ_max < 0.02 (rank-1: one surface only) — move 0.3 m and retry.
4. **Register with prior:** seed from odometry, CSM window ±0.15 m / ±15°, PLICP refine; accept if inliers ≥ 80 %, RMS ≤ 0.5 cm, correction ≤ 3σ_odo (6 cm / 9°); Ω_reg from the empirical label σ, Ω_odo = diag(2500, 2500, 365).
5. **All-pairs among stations** with predicted overlap ≥ 30 %: same gate + composed-loop χ²₃ ≤ 7.815 + λ₁/λ₂ ≥ 2 on the hypothesis set.
6. **Revisit station 0** mid-run (after the far end) and at the end; batch-solve (GNC-TLS or Huber) after each revisit; report closure before/after.
7. **Build the product:** 2 cm three-way grid carved from station rays and driven segments; per-station split-and-merge lines merged across stations; assert walls only on ≥ 2 stations / ≥ 0.3 m baseline / ≤ 1 cm agreement / ≥ 0.5 m length; Delaunay + LoS carving for the closed polygon; door certified only by a driven crossing.
8. **Accept the map when:** closure ≤ 1 cm / 0.3°; all edge χ²₃ < 7.815; every asserted wall band ≤ 1 cm; every station scan-to-map RMS ≤ 0.5 cm with ≥ 80 % inliers; ≥ 90 % of the lane perimeter asserted; one tape span within 1 cm. Otherwise insert stations where the failing wall is and repeat from step 3.
9. **Then** add the gyro ([[odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]] §3) and extend the same protocol to the whole office with a perimeter ring at ~1 m from the walls, 1 m spacing, plus one station behind each occluder.

## What this rig measured (2026-09-17 → 21) — prototype feedback

*Source: drone-prototype `eda/EDA218-office-map-from-rests/FINDING.md` (M0: the office map rebuilt from the odometry-campaign rests, no new driving) and `eda/EDA222-M1-mapping-runner/FINDING.md` (M1: the mapping routine `src/drone/mapping/`, `offline_map.py`, `mapper.py`; M2: the first live stop-scan-plan-go run, `20260921-152004_m1-map`); diary 2026-09-17 (night) and 2026-09-21. The recipe above was written 2026-09-17; these runs are its first contact with the floor.*

**What held.** Stop-and-scan with an odometry prior maps the office: M0 gives a ≈ 3.0 × 2.9 m box whose four main walls layer to **0.4–1.3 cm across stations**, per-station heading consistent to p90 0.6–1.1°, from a ≈ 1.5 × 1.2 m patch the rover never left; M2 (13 stations, 31 moves, both revisits reached, every station registered live at 0.4–1.0 cm rms) gives **heading p90 0.49°, seven asserted walls with 0.2–0.6 cm bands**, W–E 2.990 m and N→bed 2.72 m — **M0 and M2, four days apart with different station sets, agree to ~1 cm** (the plan's reproducibility check). Both runs measure the same **−2 % W–E span against the tape** (6–7 cm short of 3.048 m) while N–S is within 1.7 % — not a global scale error; a C1 range bias or what sits on the W/E walls at 1.1 m is the open question.

**What the data reversed or sharpened (each one a rule now in the code):**

- **Register on walls + objects, build the plan from walls alone.** Walls-only registration FAILs in the near-square office (two stations 5° off on the west wall); walls + objects PASS — the desk clutter is what breaks the ±90° symmetry. §1's "match against wall lines" is too narrow for a symmetric room.
- **"Select stations" means quality, not sparsity.** Thinning to 60 stations ≥ 0.10 m / 20° apart cut the loop closures ~890 → 140 and the map failed (heading p90 2.38°); keeping all 175 clean stations (thinned only at 4 cm / 10°) passes (p90 1.24°). Do not thin for economy; discard for quality (revolution disagreement, rank, redundancy).
- **A far wall looks like an object to one scan.** At 3 m the C1's ~1° spacing puts ~10 points on 0.5 m; a minimum-points rule dropped every far wall and the rank check then rejected the station. Fixed by a point minimum of 10 plus a map-informed relabel pass — a wall is asserted from many stations, never classified from one.
- **The optimiser's residuals certify nothing.** A wrong-basin loop closure that wins the vote has a tiny residual (M0 first pass: 0.4 cm / 0.2° residuals, the west wall drawn three times). The checks that caught every error use information the optimiser never touched: each station's *own* long-wall directions transformed by its pose vs the map consensus (mod 90°), and the per-station layering of *all* points about each asserted wall. Both gate a PASS/FAIL per run (§4's "evaluation without ground truth", made concrete). The RANSAC band (1.1 cm every time) is self-selected and says nothing.
- **Loop gate + robust kernel must be tight, and local registration multi-start.** Single-stage ICP + a 30 cm / 20° gate + Huber δ = 0.25 let wrong loops outvote correct local edges; multi-stage ICP (0.30 → 0.10 → 0.04 m), a 10 cm / 5° gate and δ = 1.0 fixed it. The prior can be wrong by 45°, not 3° (ticks +88.9° for a spin the LiDAR shows was +42.3°): local ICP now runs prior ± 90° in 30° steps, clusters basins, and accepts only a strong *and* unambiguous winner. 10/253 moves disagree with the LiDAR by > 10° in heading — the EDA216 label gate had hidden that tail.
- **The C1 counts clockwise.** Fed into a right-handed ICP every spin prior has the wrong sign until the scan frame is mirrored once (`y = −r·sinθ`). Step-one check on any new scanner.
- **The rig sits in its own scan — and the "blind cone" is not blind.** A survey over 27.5 M logged returns: the mast returns at 7 cm over **163–188.5°** and the screw over **234.5–248.5°**; `src/drone/lidar_filter.py` drops both zones at the device and in every offline loader. §5's "blind cone 164–188°" should read "rig zones, filtered"; re-survey after any mount or cable change.

**The planner's six office lessons (M2, six live attempts before one completed the protocol):** (1) "everything asserted from one spot" — require ≥ 3-station support and ≥ 0.3 m baseline, add a new-viewpoint term, minimum 8 stations; (2) a leg refused after a turn must not be fatal — keep the rotated view and re-plan; (3) the 180° candidate never passes its pre-check because it points into the mast zone — decide after the turn; (4) a leg clear ahead can still end where the rover cannot spin — all-round clearance at the *destination* (spin_clear + 8 cm); (5) the planner walked into the bed-side corner — a clearance term; (6) a revisit that demands a 1.4 m straight shot fails — short guarded legs with sidesteps, non-fatal. Mapping goals stay ≤ 0.5 m because a goal refreshes its own deadman ([[odometry-closed-loop-pipeline]] §What this rig measured).

**Still open.** Furniture flush to a wall (a 0.63 m run 0.3 m inside the north wall, 29 stations at 0.6 cm) is asserted as wall — indistinguishable by geometry at 1.1 m; the permanent / semi-permanent / mobile layering is the next stage. Objects are one class. A 17k × 17k SVD in the wall fit (2.3 GB per call) was replaced by 2 × 2 eigen fits — fit lines from the scatter matrix, never from a full SVD of the points.
