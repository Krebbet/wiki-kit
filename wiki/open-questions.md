# Open Questions

The live register of questions this wiki exists to answer. Every question carries a status and points at the
research job or page that will settle it. Questions are added here whenever an ingest surfaces something the
wiki cannot yet answer; they are never left in chat history.

Status: `open` (no evidence yet) · `partial` (some evidence, no settled answer) · `answered` (a page states the
answer and cites it) · `dissolved` (the question turned out to be malformed — say why).

## Core theory

| # | Question | Status | Bears on | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | What is the minimal definition of an institution that covers a federal agency, a corporation, and a village irrigation association without becoming vacuous? | partial | Outcome 2 | [[what-is-an-institution]] states three competing definitions (North, Ostrom, Williamson) and proposes a candidate minimal one; unsettled because the candidate dissolves North's institution/organization boundary. |
| Q2 | What are the *primary* dimensions of institutional variation — the smallest set that explains the most behavioural variance? | partial | Outcome 2 | [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] registers 34 named axes with operationalisations; none is yet shown to explain variance, so the *primary* set is still open. |
| Q3 | Which of those dimensions are genuinely invariant across public and private institutions, and which only look invariant because of shared vocabulary? | partial | Outcome 2 | Ostrom's empirical finding that ownership form does not predict forest condition (Hayes 2006) is real negative evidence; Williamson's remediableness symmetry is a methodological argument, not evidence. Both in commons/private settings only. |
| Q4 | Is institutional behaviour better predicted by formal structure (rules, decision rights) or by selection effects (who ends up inside)? | open | Outcome 2 | Untouched by the foundations-NIE sources — none names personnel selection as an axis. This is the test that would settle Q1. |
| Q5 | What is the correct unit of analysis — the institution, the institutional field, or the rule-set? The literature mixes all three. | partial | Outcome 1 | Confirmed and specified: North uses the societal matrix (and mixes levels unflagged in the JEP essay), Ostrom the meso-level governance regime, Williamson the transaction. [[what-is-an-institution]] tabulates the mismatch. No source adjudicates. |

## Raised by ingest (foundations-nie, 2026-08-19)

| # | Question | Status | Bears on | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q19 | Do Ostrom's eight design principles transfer beyond small-to-medium common-pool resources to large formal institutions (agencies, firms) with no physical resource and no measurable yield? | open | Outcome 2, 3 | Principles 3, 4A/4B and 7 are statements about decision rights, monitor accountability and state recognition and look portable; 1A/1B and 2A/2B are bound to resources with a boundary and a yield. Untested either way. |
| Q20 | Is the path dependence of an institutional *matrix* the same mechanism as ossification inside a single ageing *organisation*, or two different things sharing a name? | open | Outcome 4 | All age and lock-in claims in the foundations-NIE sources are matrix- or field-level. North is explicitly silent on intra-organisational scale and age; Ostrom makes no ageing claim at all; Williamson's limit argument is about size, not age. The organisational reading is currently an inference by analogy. |
| Q21 | Where is the boundary between resources that user self-organisation governs well and those needing assigned transferable rights — and is it set by party count, party heterogeneity, resource value, or boundary observability? | open | Outcome 2, 3 | The likely dissolution of [[self-organisation-vs-property-rights-in-commons]]. Testable by pooling Ostrom's and Libecap's coded case bases against D14, D16 and D23 in [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]]. |
| Q22 | Is the credible-commitment problem genuinely invariant across the polity/firm boundary — a ruler who cannot bind himself and an owner who cannot bind his own fiat being one mechanism? | open | Outcome 2, 5 | The parallel between North's ruler-shackling and Williamson's selective-intervention failure is the wiki's synthesis, not either source's claim. Flagged on [[credible-commitment]] as a hypothesis requiring a source that examines both sides. |
| Q23 | Does the discriminating-alignment hypothesis actually predict organisational form in the public sector, or only in private make-or-buy decisions? | open | Outcome 2 | Williamson claims TCE applies "in any sector whatsoever" but concedes the public-bureau application is aspirational and undeveloped. The symmetry argument is methodological; the predictive claim is untested. |
