# Anthropic Series H: $65B at $965B Valuation + S-1 Filing + Compute Scale-Up (May–June 2026)

Anthropic closed a $65B Series H at $965B post-money valuation (official announcement June 2026), surpassing OpenAI's $852B March 2026 valuation and making Anthropic the most-valued private AI company. Pre-announcement reporting (May 22) estimated $30B at $900B+ — the final round was materially larger on both dimensions. Simultaneous investor disclosure (May 20) revealed Q2 2026 projected operating profit of $559M — Anthropic's first-ever profitable quarter — on $10.9B projected quarterly revenue; by round close, revenue run-rate had crossed **$47B annualized**. Anthropic filed a confidential Form S-1 with the SEC on June 1, 2026, beginning the public-market process. No share count, price, or roadshow date disclosed.

## Compute Scale-Up (June 2026)

Significant compute agreements signed alongside or just before the Series H close:

- **Amazon:** Up to **5 gigawatts** of new capacity (Amazon compute deal); AWS remains primary cloud provider and training partner
- **Google + Broadcom:** **5 gigawatts** of next-generation TPU capacity (Google+Broadcom partnership)

## Compute Efficiency Trajectory

- Q1 2026: 71¢ compute per $1 revenue — operationally marginal
- Q2 2026 target: 56¢ — meaningful efficiency gain in one quarter
- Driver: model distillation, inference optimization, scale amortization; Google TPU deal + Amazon Trainium provide committed capacity at negotiated rates
- Risk: Anthropic has flagged aggressive compute capex ahead; the 56¢ ratio is a transitional data point, not a stable floor; the four-supplier compute scale-up announced at Series H close will pressure this ratio in H2 2026

## Enterprise Buyer Implications

- **Financial sustainability:** First profitable quarter removes near-term runway risk; enterprise buyers can now treat Anthropic as a going concern without requiring a near-term exit
- **Concentration risk persists:** Revenue heavily weighted toward a small number of enterprise accounts at this scale; 80% enterprise mix is a signal of depth, not breadth
- **Pricing floor:** Compute efficiency gains give Anthropic pricing room to compete with OpenAI on API cost without margin destruction — but planned capex expansion may suppress this for 2–4 quarters
- **Model continuity:** $30B infusion provides multi-year runway for model development independent of quarterly P&L — Claude 4/5 roadmap is not contingent on sustained profitability

## Pre-IPO Governance & Financing Cluster (2026-08-13 → 2026-08-19, captured 2026-08-26)

The three items flagged above as "not yet captured" — resolved this run with independent sourcing:

- **Supervoting shares (reported 2026-08-18, independently confirmed by both The Information and Bloomberg per TechTimes' aggregation):** Anthropic is preparing a new stock class giving CEO Dario Amodei and the other six co-founders enhanced voting rights ahead of an IPO "expected as soon as late September." Context that makes this load-bearing: Amodei owns roughly **2% equity** after $85B+ of cumulative dilutive fundraising since 2021 — standard one-share-one-vote economics would leave him without effective board influence post-IPO. Compounding factor: Anthropic's **Long-Term Benefit Trust** (the independent body that elects a board majority) just shrank from **four trustees to three** (Neil Buddy Shah, Richard Fontaine, Ben Bernanke) after a trustee moved into an executive role. Combined effect: founders control votes on one axis, a 3-person trust controls board composition on another — public shareholders get economic exposure with minimal governance lever. Comparable precedent cited: Zuckerberg holds 60% voting control at Meta despite a much smaller equity stake; SpaceX gives Musk ~85% voting power. The Council of Institutional Investors' standard objection (dual-class structures reduce accountability absent a ≤7-year sunset) applies here with no sunset provision disclosed.
- **Pre-IPO credit facility expanding past $10B** (Bloomberg, 2026-08-18, cited via secondary aggregation — not independently captured with a full source document this run) — up from a $2.5B facility a year prior; banks reportedly competing for the eventual IPO underwriting mandate. Treat as reported-not-verified until a primary or non-Bloomberg secondary capture is available.
- **Decart acquisition talks, ~$6B** (Bloomberg, 2026-08-13; independently captured via PYMNTS): Anthropic in talks to acquire Israeli world-model/chip-efficiency startup **Decart AI** (founded 2023 by brothers Dean and Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev) for ~$6B — would be **Anthropic's largest and fifth acquisition of 2026** if it closes. Decart's product reduces AI training/serving cost by improving chip-utilization efficiency; framed explicitly as a cost-control move to help Anthropic's existing compute infrastructure absorb surging demand, not a capability/product acquisition. Round valued Decart at ~$4B (up from $3.1B in August 2025) — the reported $6B deal price is roughly a 50% premium over that. Not finalized; talks could fall through.
- **OpenAI IPO timeline pushback (same window, for competitive context):** OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar reportedly told employees OpenAI now targets a public listing in **2027, not 2026** — while preserving the option to move sooner if growth continues — following a **$7B secondary share sale at $852B valuation** (closed 2026-08-10). This widens the IPO-timing gap with Anthropic (reportedly targeting a filing "as soon as late September" 2026) into a more explicit race, not just a growth-rate contest.

**Net read:** all three of last week's flagged items check out as real, independently-sourced signals (two now with primary captures, one still secondary-only). The pattern across all four items is consistent — Anthropic is optimizing pre-IPO for founder/board control and cost efficiency simultaneously, while OpenAI is explicitly slower-walking its own listing.

## Compute-deal cluster (running tally, updated 2026-09-02)

Anthropic's disclosed compute commitments have compounded into a running tally of megadeals, each with a different counterparty and financing structure:

| Date reported | Counterparty | Size | Structure / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~2026-08-24 | SpaceX | $45B | Colossus 1/2 GPU capacity (Memphis) |
| ~2026-08-24 | Fluidstack | $50B | Neocloud capacity deal |
| ~2026-08-24 | Nscale | $45B | West Virginia data-center campus capacity |
| **2026-08-31** | **Lambda** (Nvidia-backed) | **$35B** | Texas data center (Hut 8-developed, Nueces County); **Nvidia holds the lease** on the facility, not Anthropic or Lambda directly — Nvidia is increasingly financing/anchoring cloud-provider capacity to guarantee downstream chip demand. Per Bloomberg, Lambda is separately in talks to raise up to $3B at a valuation "of as much as $12 billion or more." *(Deal duration reported as six years by secondary trade-press pickup — Benzinga, Investing.com/WSJ — not stated in this page's own captured source; treat as reported-not-primary-confirmed.)* |

**Read:** the Lambda deal's structure is the most telling data point yet — Nvidia isn't just selling Anthropic chips, it's underwriting the *building itself* (holding the lease) to guarantee Lambda's capacity gets built and gets filled by an anchor tenant. This is Nvidia doing to the compute-supply chain what it did to Lambda's own financing round (Nvidia is also an investor in Lambda) — vendor-financing demand for its own chips one layer removed. Combined with the SpaceX/Fluidstack/Nscale deals above, Anthropic's disclosed forward compute commitments now run well past **$175B** in aggregate, against a **$965B** Series H valuation and a **$65B+** annualized revenue run-rate (2026-07). None of these deals have individually confirmed capital sourcing (Anthropic's own cash vs. debt-financed vs. counterparty-financed) — treat the aggregate figure as a capacity commitment, not a cash outlay, until financing structure is disclosed per-deal.

## Open Questions

- Will Q2 $559M operating profit prove durable, or is it a one-quarter artifact of compute timing?
- The four-supplier compute scale-up is substantial; does the 56¢ compute ratio hold as AWS/GCP/SpaceX/Akamai capacity comes online?
- Google's equity stake (from the $40B compute deal) creates governance complexity at $965B valuation — unclear if Google has pro-rata rights in Series H
- IPO timeline: no roadshow date disclosed as of 2026-08-17, though reporting now points to "possibly fall 2026"; Alphabet's successful $85B raise (see [[alphabet-85b-equity-2026-06]]) is a positive signal but Anthropic's IPO pricing will be the first real stress test of public market appetite for a loss-making (at OpenAI scale) frontier AI lab
- Anthropic vs. OpenAI revenue-metric comparability: the FT's own caveat (differing revenue-calculation methodology) means the "7x vs. 2x growth" comparison should not be over-read as apples-to-apples until both companies disclose metric definitions
- Dual-class supervoting structure has no disclosed sunset provision — will public-market investor pushback (per Council of Institutional Investors' standard objection) force one before the IPO prices, or does Anthropic's control-tight cap-table leverage let it skip that concession entirely?
- Does the Decart acquisition close, and if so, does "$6B for chip-utilization efficiency software" read as a genuine cost-control technology buy or as compute-scarcity panic-buying at a premium valuation?
