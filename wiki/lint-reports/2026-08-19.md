## Cross-job consistency

The wiki was written by five concurrent page-writing agents across seven research jobs. They shared a number
allocation but not a semantic one. This section covers what that produced.

### 1. Semantic collisions in the register

The register is *far* better self-reconciled than the concurrency implies — D74 explicitly argues why venue
count is registered separately from D44's veto count; D76 explicitly reconciles itself against D55 and D56;
D34 explicitly refuses to re-register a second naming by the same author; D47 explicitly distinguishes itself
from D25 and D8. Those are the right instinct, executed well. What follows is what escaped it.

- **[HIGH] D42 and D51 are the same count under two names, and neither row mentions the other.**
  - **D42 External-control density / layering** (Bozeman, via `red-tape`, `risk-aversion` batch):
    "distinct external bodies with authority to issue rules the organisation must comply with — parent agency,
    political superiors, central management agencies (OMB-equivalents), auditors, regulators,
    interorganisational partners, funders with conditions."
  - **D51 Principal multiplicity and heterogeneity** (Dixit, via `incentives-under-multiple-principals`,
    `incentives-and-institutional-form` batch): "actors holding a **funding, statutory, veto, judicial or
    bargaining** lever over the institution — appropriators, the executive, courts, unions, regulated clients,
    organised beneficiaries."
  - Both are "count the external bodies with leverage over this organisation, and score their heterogeneity."
    D51 adds an inclusion test (has the actor changed behaviour by threatening a consequence?) that D42 lacks;
    D42 adds a jurisdictional-layer count that D51 lacks. Those are two refinements of one axis, not two axes.
    Cross-references: D42 is referenced by D1, D9 and D61; D51 only by D9. **Neither references the other.**
    An analyst scoring an institution would produce two near-identical numbers and not know it.
- **[HIGH] D14 and D74 are the same count under two names, and neither row mentions the other.**
  - **D14 Polycentricity** (Ostrom/North, `foundations-nie`): "Count formally independent decision centres with
    authority over the same domain; add a concentration index."
  - **D74 Independent-venue count** (True/Jones/Baumgartner, `enabling-institutional-change`): "Enumerate the
    arenas with formal authority to act on the issue… and count those whose decision cannot be directly
    overturned by another on the list."
  - D74 goes to real trouble to distinguish itself from D44 (start-a-change vs. stop-a-change) and never
    notices that D14, sixty rows earlier, is the same enumeration. This is exactly the failure mode D74's own
    warning describes — "a single 'multiplicity of authorities' number would be uninterpretable" — committed
    against a row its author could not see.
- **[MED] D25 measurability of output vs. D50's outcome-observability limb.** D25: "Can a third party verify
  the output at acceptable cost?" D50 (Dixit after Wilson, 2×2): "could anyone establish what **resulted** from
  it, without contest." D50's outcome axis is D25's question. D25 has been refined twice already (D39/D40 for
  the information-flow half, D25a for the measurability half) and each refinement is recorded in the row —
  but D50 is recorded nowhere in D25, and D50 references neither D25 nor D25a nor D47. The measurement cluster
  now has four rows (D25, D25a, D47, D50) from three different batches with a partial reference graph:
  D25a→D25, D47→D25, D50→nothing.
- **[MED] D10's operationalisation is a veto count, and D44 is the veto-count row.** D10 (Constraint on the
  top decision-maker): "Count of independent actors able to block an expropriative act, and whether any has
  ever exercised it." D44 (Veto-point count × preference divergence): "enumerate actors whose assent is
  required to change the status quo." D10 is `promoted`, D44 is `candidate`, and D44's entire argument is that
  a bare count "predicts nothing — anything can happen" without the preference profile. D10 is a bare count
  and is not covered by that warning, because neither row cites the other. D10 does cross-reference D11
  ("market-priced alternative"), so its author was reconciling — just not forward, into a row written later.

### 2. Duplicate and near-duplicate open questions

The Q-blocks were written by agents who could not see each other's additions. Numbering held; content did not.

- **[MED] Q3 / Q64 / Q73 are one question at three scopes, and none of the three points at the others.**
  Q3 (foundations, core theory): "Which of those dimensions are genuinely invariant across public and private
  institutions…" · Q64 (`institutional-stagnation`): "Do any of these mechanisms — goal displacement,
  cooptation, capture, accretion, procedural ossification — operate in **private-sector** institutions?" ·
  Q73 (`enabling-institutional-change`): "Does anything in this wiki's account of institutional renewal hold
  for private firms?" Q64 and Q73 are the decay-side and renewal-side instances of Q3. Q25 and Q38 are two
  further instances (each asking whether the real variable is funding mechanism / principal count rather than
  public ownership) — and those two *do* cross-reference each other, which shows the practice existed and was
  not applied across batch boundaries. Consolidating or explicitly nesting Q3/Q64/Q73 is a human decision.
- **[MED] Q20 and Q61 are the same methodological question raised twice.** Q20 (`foundations-nie`): "Is the
  path dependence of an institutional *matrix* the same mechanism as ossification inside a single ageing
  *organisation*, or two different things sharing a name?" Q61 (`institutional-stagnation`): "Is the
  substitution of regime, corpus or polity duration for organisational age the field's central confound rather
  than incidental sloppiness?" Both are the level-of-analysis substitution. Q61 is the sharper statement and
  says it was "raised because the substitution is now visible as a *pattern across a whole batch*" — its
  author did not know Q20 had already named it from a single batch. Neither references the other.
- **[LOW] Q7 / Q60 / Q39 are correctly reconciled** — Q60 states it is "the successor to Q7's age limb at the
  *organisational* level, as Q39 is its successor at the [matrix] level." This is the model the pairs above
  should follow.
- **[LOW] Q16 is Q60/Q61 stated at thesis level, and carries no notes** while Q60 and Q61 carry detailed ones.
  The batch that produced Q60/Q61 was commissioned to test exactly what Q16 asks.

### 3. Terminology drift

- **[HIGH] Two incompatible evidence-tier vocabularies are in use.** `CLAUDE.md` specifies three tiers:
  (a) empirically established, (b) theoretical model, (c) wiki/user synthesis. Two encodings evolved:
  - *Named form* — `**[empirical]**` (13), `**[model]**` (42), `**[framework]**` (39), `**[wiki synthesis]**` (111).
  - *Lettered form* — `**[a — randomised]**`, `**[b]**` (4), `**[c — wiki synthesis]**` (7), and the inline
    italic style `*(a) empirical, at one remove*` / `*(b) essay*` used throughout
    `conflicts/decay-as-real-vs-decay-as-overstated`.
  36 pages use the named form, 47 use some lettered form, many use both. Worse, the named form has **four**
  tokens for three tiers: `[model]` and `[framework]` are both tier (b) and no page defines the distinction
  between them. In practice `[framework]` appears to mean "a conceptual scheme" and `[model]` "a formal model
  with derived results", which is a genuinely useful split — but it is undocumented, unevenly applied, and
  invisible to anyone grepping for tier (b).
- **[MED] Three names for the veto-count construct, split cleanly by authoring batch.**
  `veto-points-and-bureaucratic-autonomy` (risk-aversion batch) uses "veto point" 12× and "veto player" 6×;
  `gradual-institutional-change` (institutional-evolution batch) uses "veto player" 4× and "veto point" 0×;
  Thelen's own "veto possibilities" appears 9× and is the term the register uses in its `## Related` note on
  D55. Plus "independent venue" (D74) and one "blocking point". No page states the mapping. A reader following
  the veto thread across three pages meets three vocabularies for what D44 and D74 jointly decompose into two
  measurable things.
- **[LOW] Field terminology.** "institutional matrix" (10, North), "organisational field" (6) / "organizational
  field" (3, DiMaggio & Powell — the US spelling is inside quotation), "institutional field" (1). Three
  distinct units of analysis with overlapping names; `what-is-an-institution`'s `## Related` on
  `institutional-isomorphism` correctly identifies the field as "a fourth unit of analysis sitting between the
  single organisation and the institutional matrix" — that clarification exists on exactly one page and is not
  reflected in `what-is-an-institution`'s own body.
- **[LOW] Timidity vocabulary.** "risk aversion" (26), "timidity" (26), "risk-averse" (16), "cautious" (9),
  "mediocrity bias" (4), "conservatism" (3). `risk-aversion-in-large-institutions` is the umbrella page and
  does not fix a preferred term. Q30's whole point is that the construct is unmeasured; five names for an
  unmeasured construct compounds it.

### 5. Overlapping page scope

- **[MED] Hierarchy span and depth are split across three pages with no owner.**
  `knowledge-hierarchies-and-the-cost-of-scale` is the theory (Garicano & Rossi-Hansberg; register credits it
  with D36 span and D37 depth); `hierarchy-and-near-decomposability` is Simon's rationale for depth and the
  cognitive ceiling on span; but the **only panel evidence in the wiki on what corporate hierarchies actually
  look like** — Rajan & Wulf, 300+ US firms 1986–1998, the numbers that make D36 and D37 `promoted` — sits in
  `governance-structures`, a Williamson transaction-cost-economics page, under "Empirical evidence: span and
  depth over time". A reader on the span/depth pages gets the theory without the measurements. Worth a human
  decision on whether the Rajan & Wulf section moves.
- **[MED] Two "punctuated equilibrium" pages with near-identical names and a one-way link.**
  `punctuated-equilibrium-and-the-state` (Krasner, institutional-evolution batch) and
  `punctuated-equilibrium-and-policy-change` (True/Jones/Baumgartner, enabling-institutional-change batch).
  The policy page links the state page twice and explains the relation; the state page does not link back and
  does not acknowledge that a second punctuated-equilibrium page exists. Nothing on either page states the
  division of labour, so the titles are the only guide and they are one word apart.
- **[LOW] Three isomorphism pages, linked in one direction only.** `institutional-isomorphism` (DiMaggio &
  Powell), `institutional-myths-and-decoupling` (Meyer & Rowan), `isomorphic-mimicry-and-capability-traps`
  (Andrews, Pritchett & Woolcock). The third links the first two and carefully distinguishes itself
  ("a different mechanism producing similar artefacts; kept separate"); neither of the first two links the
  third. Scope is genuinely distinct — no merge indicated — but the entry point is wrong for a reader landing
  on the older pages.
- **[LOW] `capture-manifests.md` is tooling documentation filed in the content namespace.** It contains
  capture command manifests and a note about the host machine's GPU VRAM degradation and
  `~/bin/gpu_safe_setup.sh` (line 31). It has no evidence tiers, is exempt from most checks by nature, and is
  the only page in `wiki/` describing local hardware. Consider whether it belongs under `tools/` or `docs/`.

### 6. Where the wiki's own rules were applied unevenly

- **[HIGH] Seven `promoted` register rows breach the register's own promotion criterion, and none argues an
  exception.** The criterion, stated in the register's own "How to read this" and emphasised there:
  *"(i) at least two independent sources name it, **and** (ii) a measurement procedure can be stated…
  Both conditions, not either."* The register also establishes elsewhere (D34) that "a second naming by the
  same author is not a second piece of evidence." Single-source rows currently marked `promoted`:
  | Row | Named by | Batch |
  |---|---|---|
  | **D3** Enforcement party | North only | foundations-nie |
  | **D4** Direction of monitor accountability | Ostrom only | foundations-nie |
  | **D10** Constraint on the top decision-maker | North only | foundations-nie |
  | **D30** Time-to-correction and rent dissipated | Libecap only | foundations-nie |
  | **D70** Personnel selection and recruitment mechanism | Finan, Olken & Pande only | enabling-institutional-change |
  | **D71** Citizen–state interest alignment | Finan, Olken & Pande only | enabling-institutional-change |
  | **D72** Reform accountability infrastructure | NAO only | enabling-institutional-change |
  Grepped each row for exception language (`promotion criterion`, `criterion (i)`, `single source`,
  `only source`, `two independent`, `despite`): **zero hits in all seven.** The pattern is legible — each
  batch promoted its own strongest, best-evidenced axes, and criterion (i) was read as "or" rather than "and"
  by two different batches independently. D70–D72 are the register's only randomised or near-census axes, so
  the temptation is understandable; the rule as written does not permit it. **This needs a human ruling:**
  either relax criterion (i) to admit a single source of sufficient evidential quality (and say so in the
  register), or demote the seven to `candidate` with the second-source requirement recorded as their
  promotion condition. Leaving it silent means the register's status column no longer means what the register
  says it means.
- **[MED] The register's `## Related` is now 36 entries long and functions as an inverse index.** It is
  genuinely useful — most entries say which D-rows the page supplied — but it is doing catalog work that
  `index.md` is meant to do, it contains the duplicate `governance-structures` entry noted above, and it is the
  only `## Related` section in the wiki whose length approaches the page it belongs to.

## Harvest checkpoint

Three items look kit-level rather than project-specific. They have **not** been written to `master_notes.md` —
this run is diagnostic-only — and are surfaced here for a decision:

1. **`tools/audit_captures.py` reports a clean bill on a corpus it did not inspect.** Three of its four checks
   are gated behind `if pdfs_dir.exists():` (line 71) and the fourth needs image refs. On a PDF-free,
   image-free corpus all four no-op and the tool prints `Total issues: 0`. It should distinguish
   "0 issues found" from "0 checks were applicable", and print a coverage line (`N markdowns inspected,
   M checks applicable`). Any wiki whose `/research` path does not retain PDFs has the same blind spot.
   Scope: kit.
2. **Concurrent page-writing agents need a semantic reservation, not just a numeric one.** The five agents
   were given reserved D- and Q-number ranges, which worked perfectly — no collisions in 74 rows and 73
   questions. Every defect in the Cross-job consistency section above is the *same* construct entering twice
   under two names, which numeric reservation cannot prevent. A cheap fix for `/research` and `/ingest`: before
   registering a new axis or question, require the writing agent to grep the register's operationalisation
   column (not just its names) for the measurement procedure it is about to write, and to record the near-miss
   it found. The register already contains four excellent examples of an agent doing this voluntarily
   (D74 vs D44, D76 vs D55/D56, D34's refusal, D47 vs D25) — the practice exists and just needs to be
   mandatory. Scope: kit.
3. **A generic lint check worth adding: "does the page obey the wiki's own stated rules?"** The
   highest-value finding in this report (seven `promoted` rows breaching the promotion criterion) came from
   reading a rule the wiki wrote for itself and testing rows against it. Any wiki with a register, a status
   column or an admissibility criterion has the same exposure, and no generic check currently looks for it.
   Scope: kit.
