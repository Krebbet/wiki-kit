# Pay Dispersion and the Direction of Scientific Work

Un-refereed arXiv preprint (2611.16747, Cao 2026; forthcoming-journal status stated in the capture, not verified). Payroll records for 105,050 tenure-line faculty in 4,839 departments at 93 US public universities (2004-2026), linked to OpenAlex, show that departments with more pay dispersion unexplained by their own rank and seniority ladder publish more and are cited more, but produce less disruptive, less novel, more conventional work. The author disclaims causality.

**Evidence tier note.** (a) for the associations — administrative records, cross-departmental, observational. The mechanism (legible rewards pull effort toward conventional, quickly recognised output) is the author's **[model]**. Weakening factors: coarsened-exact-matching nulls on disruption and productivity, small effect sizes, unobserved endowment confounds, and retirement tests that move the Gini only modestly. Scope is US public universities; a corporate-R&D extension would be **[wiki synthesis]** at most.

## Findings

- **[empirical]** Residual (ladder-unexplained) pay dispersion is positively associated with publications and citations, as tournament theory predicts, and negatively with disruption and novelty.
- **[empirical]** Individual pay premia attach to team size and breadth of output, i.e. to what is visible and metered; a selection-at-appointment test is reported.
- **[model]** Mechanism: pay follows legible output, evaluators are conservative, so effort is redirected from hard-to-observe frame-revising work.
