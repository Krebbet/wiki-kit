# Education-Democracy: A Fixed-Effects Critique

Acemoglu, Johnson, Robinson & Yared directly name and replicate [[schooling-and-institutional-quality]]'s source paper — Glaeser, La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes & Shleifer (2004) — and show its schooling-predicts-democracy result depends on a specific, correctable omission: **no year fixed effects.** Reproducing Glaeser et al.'s own regressions exactly (their Table 4, replicated here as this paper's Table 4 Panel A), education coefficients on democracy/autocracy measures are 0.096–0.909, mostly significant — matching the original. Adding year dummies (Panel B) **flips all four coefficients negative and insignificant** (−0.137 to −0.432). A country-fixed-effects-only panel of 108 countries, 1965–2000, shows the identical pattern on its own headline specification: pooled OLS γ=0.027 (significant at 1%); with country fixed effects, γ=−0.005 (insignificant, wrong-signed).

**Evidence tier note.** **[empirical]** — dynamic panel regressions (pooled OLS, fixed effects, Anderson-Hsiao IV, Arellano-Bond GMM), Freedom House/Polity/Przeworski-Boix-Rosato democracy measures, Barro-Lee schooling data, 108 countries 1965–2000. This is a **direct, named, technically executed engagement** with the exact paper this wiki's schooling page is built on — not a diffuse critique of modernisation theory.

## The mechanism of the disappearing effect

**[empirical]** Because education and democracy both trended upward globally over 1965–2000, a regression with country fixed effects but no year dummies attributes the shared secular trend to education's causal effect. Adding year fixed effects removes that trend and, in the authors' own words, "the impact of education on democracy disappears entirely." This is stated as a **narrower and more technical claim than a general identification dispute** — both papers use similar measures and similar Barro-Lee schooling data; the disagreement is specifically that country fixed effects alone are insufficient without time fixed effects as well.

## What is and is not being claimed

**[empirical]** The authors are explicit about the limit of their own result: **"It is important to emphasize that our paper does not answer this question [whether there is no long-run causal relationship]... It is possible that changes in education have very long run effects, say over 50 or 100 years, that do not manifest themselves in the shorter timeframe that we have looked at."** This is a rebuttal of the specific short/medium-run panel evidence Glaeser et al. presented, not a categorical claim that education never causes institutional or regime change.

## Scope relative to Glaeser, La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes & Shleifer's Table 12

**[wiki synthesis]** This paper's Table 4 replication targets Glaeser et al.'s **democracy/political-institutions regressions** (their broader claim, stated in this paper's own words: "differences in schooling are a major causal factor explaining not only differences in democracy, but more generally in political institutions"). Whether Table 4's specific regression is identical to [[schooling-and-institutional-quality]]'s Table 12 sequencing panel (schooling predicting subsequent change in executive-constraint measures) or a related-but-distinct specification within the same 2004 paper is not fully resolved by the capture — treat this as a **direct, technically rigorous engagement with the same paper and the same broad claim**, satisfying much of what independent replication would require, without asserting it is a literal re-run of Table 12's exact numbers.
