# O.U.R. COOP

O.U.R. COOP is an international social cooperative of visual artists, headquartered in Belgrade, formally founded in late May 2026 via a five-day founding assembly in Nikšić, Montenegro (over fifty artists, cultural workers, researchers, and cooperative practitioners). It is the outcome of Shared Visions, a Creative Europe–funded project bringing together institutional partners (Fund B92 and SULUV in Serbia, Avans University of Applied Sciences in the Netherlands, Landscape Choreography in Milan, KicKVoidLoop in Porto, the Montenegrin Institute of Contemporary Art) with technical collaborators Curve Labs and [Bread Cooperative](https://breadchain.xyz/). **This is an adjacent-vertical case, not a core wiki subject** — the wiki tracks consumer/tenant/driver counter-power, not the artist/creator economy, and O.U.R. COOP is not consumer-facing. It's captured here because its governance-technology layer — a member-transferable, euro-pegged community currency plus quadratic-voting resource allocation — is a transferable pattern other cooperatives, including consumer-facing ones, could adopt.

## Governance and currency infrastructure

The cooperative's technological centerpiece is **OUR Coin** (name provisional): a stablecoin pegged 1:1 to the euro, built on Gnosis Chain, with smart contracts designed by Breadchain. Mechanics:

- **Issuance on sale.** When a patron acquires an artist's work through the cooperative, the transaction is validated by the cooperative and the artist receives OUR Coin tokens.
- **Internal circulation.** Tokens are non-speculative and transferable among members; they can be used for collective purchasing of materials/supplies, for shared services, or held as a transparent record of a member's contribution to the common pool.
- **Cash-out path.** Tokens are redeemable for euros through the cooperative's coordinators, who handle fiat exchange, withdrawals, and fund execution — the coop is the sole fiat on/off-ramp, not individual members.
- **Yield layer.** Pooled funds accumulate yield via DeFi protocols; the generated interest helps fund cooperative activities. Governance of how that yield is allocated is explicitly still emergent, developed in dialogue with members rather than pre-specified in the protocol.
- **Rationale (per the source article, quoting researcher Erik Bordeleau):** blockchain as infrastructure for "accounting otherwise" — letting a transnational membership track contributions, distribute resources, and make collective ownership legible across the fragmented legal/banking systems of a multi-jurisdiction Europe, without depending on any single national banking system.

Separately, the cooperative has been piloting **quadratic voting** for participatory resource allocation. In an autumn workshop at the SULUV gallery (Novi Sad), members tested QV as a mechanism for democratic art commissioning: each participant receives a budget of vote credits distributable across competing proposals, with each additional vote on the same proposal costing quadratically more — capturing intensity of preference while preventing any single voter from dominating the outcome. The pilot used a mobile voting interface; observed frictions (interface learning curve, social dynamics of visible vs. anonymous voting) are feeding directly into the cooperative's emerging governance framework. Governance protocols are being built "from culture," i.e., deliberately after and alongside community-building work, rather than imposed as a pre-built protocol — see Origin below.

Together, the community-currency layer and the quadratic-voting layer form a general-purpose "governance-technology" stack for member-run resource distribution: euro-pegged internal currency for cross-border, bank-independent settlement; quadratic voting for preference-weighted, capture-resistant allocation decisions. Neither component is specific to visual art — both are transferable to any cooperative (including consumer-facing ones on this wiki, e.g. buyer clubs or tenant unions) that needs to move value and make collective allocation decisions across members without a shared bank or a one-member-one-vote bottleneck.

## Origin

O.U.R. COOP traces to a group of members of ULUS (the Serbian visual-artists' association based in Belgrade), frustrated with representative-democracy advocacy toward unresponsive government, who revived the cooperative form — drawing on the Yugoslav tradition of workers' self-management and social ownership (SULUJ, the federal Union of Associations of Visual Artists of Yugoslavia, founded 1947). The Shared Visions project funded two years of preparatory work before the May 2026 founding: reading groups (on *DAOs: Artists, Activists, and Worldbuilders* and on *What Is To Be Done?*), LARP sessions in Belgrade and Berlin rehearsing collective resource-allocation dilemmas, and the PROBLEMZINE publication inverting the artist-portfolio format to collectively surface shared material problems. The founding documents were signed on the final day of a five-day working assembly in Nikšić, Montenegro, in late May 2026. As of the source article (August 2026), the cooperative is roughly three months old and still in incubation — its economic activities (editorial work, collective exhibitions, shared services) are still taking shape, with two years remaining in the Creative Europe funding window to reach a stable business model.
