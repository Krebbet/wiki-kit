# Information Institutions and the Regulatory Charge

Provisional, [model]-tier page on an un-refereed arXiv preprint (Jaag, 2611.13715). A formal model in which a regulator facing hidden, network-dependent harm chooses jointly a corrective charge on an activity and an *information institution* — purchased monitoring, signed certification, or a self-report backed by committed random audits. The choice between institutions switches discontinuously with network exposure, and the cost of verifying truthful reports feeds back into the optimal charge. No empirical claims.

**Evidence tier note.** (b) throughout: a pure formal model, internally derived. The regulator is a benevolent, uncaptured planner and audits are assumed precommitted with collectible fines; neither assumption is tested. The preprint is un-refereed.

## What the model says

- **[model]** The regulator cannot observe the realised network configuration that determines harm. An aggregate outcome can be exact and stable yet hide the configuration policy needs.
- **[model]** Three information routes compete on cost: Gaussian monitoring (paid for directly), certification (a signed claim), and reports verified by random audits. Which is cheapest depends on network exposure, and the optimum jumps between routes rather than shifting smoothly.
- **[model]** Verification cost is not separable from the charge: costlier audits raise the optimal charge, because the regulator substitutes price for information it cannot afford.
- **[model]** Audits only work if committed in advance. An uninformed regulator has no incentive to audit sequentially, so [[credible-commitment]] is the load-bearing assumption.

## Why it matters here

**[wiki synthesis]** The model gives a formal route by which an information gap — the same gap behind the right-to-decide vs control split in [[formal-and-real-authority]] — can be closed by institutional design rather than by delegation. It is the benevolent-planner counterpart to [[regulatory-capture]], where the informed party's preferences are opposed and the information channel itself is the point of capture. Candidate rows for [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]]: *verification enforceability* and *separation of verifier from allocator*.
