# FDA Case Profile

The wiki's first case-study page: the FDA's human-drug-review function, scored against [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] from three primary documents — the FY2025 PDUFA financial report to Congress, the agency's current organisational chart, and an HHS Office of Inspector General report on accelerated-approval confirmatory-trial delays. This is the first time any register axis has been run against a real institution rather than proposed from literature.

**Evidence tier note.** **[empirical]** throughout — every claim below traces to a primary government document, not a secondary academic source.

## Funding mechanism (D35) — scoreable, and cleanly so

**[empirical]** FDA's human-drug review is funded **77% by industry-paid user fees, 23% by congressional appropriation** in FY2025 (\$1.360bn user fees / \$399.0m appropriations of \$1.759bn total obligated). This is not discretionary: the FD&C Act (as amended by PDUFA) sets three binding conditions each year before FDA may even collect the fees — (i) FDA's non-fee appropriation must meet an inflation-adjusted 1997 floor (FY2025: \$3.522bn actual vs. \$1.594bn floor), (ii) the year's fee ceiling must be specified in that year's appropriations act, and (iii) FDA must spend a statutory *minimum* from non-fee appropriations on drug review (FY2025: \$398.96m obligated vs. \$287.57m floor). **This is a real, working instance of D35's anti-substitution safeguard**: industry funding cannot simply displace public appropriation, because public appropriation is independently floor-tested first.

**Fee-purpose firewall, concretely observed**: "PDUFA user fees are not a fee for service. User fees are pooled and may be used for the allowable activities as defined in the FD&C Act" — a legally enumerated 14-category activity list explicitly *excludes* generic-drug review, most OTC review, and enforcement-policy development unrelated to specific statutory sections. Payroll allocation is checked via an activity-based time-reporting system so fee dollars fund only allowable work in proportion to time invested — a real, running instance of the kind of construction audit [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] **D120** proposes in the abstract.

## Discretion, enforcement friction, and the withdrawal tool (D9)

**[empirical]** FDA can approve a drug on a surrogate endpoint under accelerated approval, in exchange for a sponsor's statutory commitment to complete a confirmatory trial "with due diligence." The enforcement tool — withdrawal of approval — exists (21 CFR § 314.530) but is rarely and only recently used: **35 of 278 approvals withdrawn since 1992 (13%), more than half (18) since January 2021.** The OIG report's own framing locates the failure not in FDA capacity but in the withdrawal process itself: "the process to withdraw a drug's approval can be cumbersome, and can take months to years" — a full administrative hearing before the Commissioner, then a court petition available to the sponsor. One case (Makena) sat in a pending withdrawal hearing for roughly two years as of the report; another (Proamatine) saw a 2010 withdrawal attempt reversed after patient and clinician appeals, and remains on the market 85 months past its confirmatory-trial deadline. **This is the register's first real-institution data point on a specific enforcement tool's actual base rate of use versus its formal availability** — directly the kind of "has any blocker ever exercised the power" check [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] D10 calls for, applied here to FDA's own enforcement discretion rather than to a constraint on FDA.

## An axis this case surfaced that no literature source named

**[wiki synthesis]** FDA's current organisational chart (captured 2026-08-20) shows the Office of the Commissioner itself with the **Commissioner (Acting)**, **Principal Deputy Commissioner (Vacant)**, **Chief of Staff (Acting)**, and, one tier down, the **Office of the Chief Medical Officer (Vacant)** — four of the agency's most senior leadership lines either acting or unfilled, visible directly from a single primary document with no interpretation required. No source this wiki has ingested proposes leadership-vacancy share as a dimension of institutional variation, but it is directly observable, directly comparable across institutions and across time for the same institution, and plausibly bears on decision-making capacity and continuity. Registered as [[dimensions-of-institutional-variation]] **D134** — the register's first row born from a case profile rather than a literature source, flagged as such.

## What could not be scored, and why — the point of this exercise

**[wiki synthesis]** Several promoted or candidate axes this wiki holds could not be run against FDA from the three captured documents, and the gap is itself informative:

- **D110/D111** (management-insulation index; management-practice interview survey) — both require either a private-company control-rights document (D110) or a live respondent survey (D111); FDA has no charter/bylaws in the corporate-governance sense and was not the subject of a World Management Survey-style instrument. **Not inapplicable in principle, just not capturable from these three documents.**
- **D128** (ultimate ownership/control-type) — has no analogue for a government agency; there is no "ultimate controller" to trace in La Porta et al.'s sense. This is a genuine scope limit on D128, not a gap in this case's capture.
- **D8** (residual claimancy) — trivially "no" for FDA staff (salaried civil servants), which is correctly predicted by the axis rather than a failure of it, but adds no new information.
- **D91** (performance-conditionality of institutional *support*) does not run in its usual direction here — FDA is not a granting body being conditioned, but the recipient of conditioned funding (PDUFA's own hiring commitments, e.g. "CDER hired 1 of the 4 established reviewer positions... by the end of FY 2025"). Worth noting as a directional inversion of the axis's usual use, not a new row.
